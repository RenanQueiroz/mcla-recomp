# Poll wait and wake on demand by default with 5 or fewer logical processors? (2026-10-28)

**Update 2026-10-29 (v0.3.1): 4 cores without SMT are no longer left out, and `audio_thread_priority = 3` and `audio_adaptive_queue` are set on by the same switch.** The sections
"Left out: 4 cores without SMT" and the table of masks below describe v0.3.0; the new evidence (chain `audio21`), what it does and does not answer, and the new `CPU:` line are in
`small-cpu-audio-2026-10-29.md`.

**State: ON by default since v0.3.0** (decided after the chain below, whose verdict for 4 cores without SMT was FAIL: see Results). The chain was run on the build of branch `cpu/small-cpu-defaults` (bbf43c6, the switch
still off, the variants set on the command line); the numbers are in "Results". The default was then flipped (`mcla_small_cpu_defaults`, `src/mcla_app.cpp`) in the integration
branch; that build was not run again for this. **4 logical processors on 4 cores without SMT are left out** (owner's decision after the chain, see "Left out: 4 cores without SMT"); the `CPU:` log lines of that rule are to be read in the final validation of v0.3.0 (not run when this was written).

## The switch

`mcla_small_cpu_defaults` (Windows, default ON since v0.3.0, `src/mcla_app.cpp`, `ApplyCpuDefaults`). On, a process that may run on 5 or fewer logical processors gets
`mcla_pump_wake_on_demand` and `mcla_poll_wait` on by default, next to the `timer_queue_hr_wait` it already gets there. A value from the command line, the environment or
`mcla.toml` wins, as for the other automatic values. The `CPU:` line says what was chosen:
`CPU: 4 logical processors on 2 cores: timer queue high-resolution wait on, pump wake on demand on, poll wait on (automatic; 5 or fewer, with mcla_small_cpu_defaults)`. With 6 or more logical
processors the switch changes nothing (the line is the same with it on). The cvar's default was `false` on the branch and is `true` in v0.3.0 (one word in `mcla_app.cpp`); `--mcla_small_cpu_defaults=false` is the old rule.
Linux and the PS5 do not have it (the other CPU defaults are Windows only too).

## Evidence so far (from the cpu3 and hand-off chains, 2026-10-28; RTX 5080, D3D12, native resolution, a Ryzen 7 9800X3D limited by affinity)

- At affinity 0xF (4 logical processors = 2 physical cores with SMT; logical processors 2k and 2k+1 are one core on this PC), uncapped, the update thread spent 47% of a frame waiting for
  a command ring slot (one traced run): the frame was the pump's 7 ms Sleep rhythm (6.96 ms).
- `mcla_pump_wake_on_demand` at 4 logical: uncapped frame 7.09 to 4.90 ms and 6.93 to 4.58 ms (swaps/s +39% and +51%), 2 runs a side; no benefit at a 30 FPS cap. Audio in those 2 runs:
  0 steady underruns, 7 and 6 late wake-ups (8 ms or more), longest 9.6 and 9.9 ms. The off run that opened its batch had 574 steady underruns on its own.
- `mcla_poll_wait` at 4 logical, uncapped: frame -4.4% (+-0.5), p99 -9.0%, process CPU -26% (+-0.9), Main thread -37% (toggle inside a run, 3 windows); A/B 2 runs a side CPU -28% and -30%.
  At caps (30, 60, 120) and at 8 and 16 logical it was noise.
- Not measured: both together; 0x55 (4 physical cores without SMT siblings) and 0x155 (5 cores); 5 runs a side; the audio over such a set; 6 logical with the switch (the proof that it
  changes nothing there).

## The chain (`SP\smallcpu\chain.ps1`, written, not run)

Variants `off` (`--mcla_small_cpu_defaults=false`) and `on` (`=true`) of one build, A-B-B-A order, n=5 a side after a warm-up, on 0x55 (4 cores), 0xF (2 cores with SMT), 0x155 (5 cores),
and one pair at 0x3F (3 cores with SMT = 6 logical). The route: boot, then free roam uncapped, a 60 FPS cap, a 30 FPS cap. A mask passes when, for the `on` runs against the `off` runs:
the frame median is at most +1.0% and the p99 at most +3.0% in every segment, the caps hold within 1%, the audio has no steady underrun and no watchdog trip in the run (and none at
shutdown), the swap-interval spread is not worse, the game clock is 1.000 +- 0.01, and either the uncapped frame is 3% shorter or the process uses 10% less CPU per swap, by more than the
runs' own spread. The report prints PASS, LOOK or FAIL per mask and the noise it measured.

## Left out: 4 cores without SMT (decision of 2026-10-28, owner; v0.3.0 only: reversed on 2026-10-29, see the update at the top)

With 4 logical processors that are 4 physical cores (a CPU with 4 cores and no hyper-threading) `mcla_small_cpu_defaults` is not applied: the game keeps the v0.2.x rule there, which is the
timer queue's high-resolution wait only, with `mcla_pump_wake_on_demand` and `mcla_poll_wait` off. At 4 logical processors on 2 cores (SMT), at 5 logical processors (any cores) and at 1 to 3 logical
processors the defaults are applied as before; at 6 or more the switch changes nothing.

Why. In the simulation of that case (the 0x55 mask, 5 runs a side, see Results) 3 of the 5 runs with the options on gained about 40%, but one of those 3 stuttered (free-roam p99 21.7 ms,
driving 29.5 ms, 77 late audio wake-ups of 8 ms or more, against 4 to 44 in the other 9 runs; no silent frame, no watchdog trip) and 2 of the 5 had a short audio dropout (2 and 5 silent frames)
that made the watchdog pause the wake on demand. The cause is not known. Runs with the options off had silent frames too (3 and 1), so the dropouts are not only the options'. The owner chose to
keep the old behaviour for that CPU rather than ship a result that went either way. This is a decision on a simulation with n=5, not a measurement that the options are bad on such a CPU.

How the game counts (`PhysicalCoresAvailable`, `src/mcla_app.cpp`). The logical processors are counted as before: the bits of the process's affinity mask (which includes a job object's limit), or
every processor of every group when the mask is the whole group. The cores are the entries of `GetLogicalProcessorInformationEx(RelationProcessorCore)` that have at least one logical processor in
that mask (same group as the process), so the affinity limits of the test harness behave like a real CPU with that many cores. On the test PC (Ryzen 7 9800X3D, logical processors 2k and 2k+1 are
one core):

| Mask | Logical | Cores | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0xF | 4 | 2 | applied |
| 0x55 | 4 | 4 | left out (v0.3.0; applied since v0.3.1) |
| 0x155 | 5 | 5 | applied |
| 0x3F | 6 | (not counted) | 6 or more: unchanged |

In v0.3.0 the `CPU:` line said it: `CPU: 4 logical processors on 4 cores: timer queue high-resolution wait on, pump wake on demand off (automatic; 5 or fewer; mcla_small_cpu_defaults not applied: 4 cores without SMT)`.
If the call fails or the process has a mask in more than one processor group, the cores are "unknown": in v0.3.0 the defaults were then not applied at 4 logical processors (the line said so), at the other counts they were. Since v0.3.1 neither case is left out and the core count is only printed.
A value the player sets for `mcla_pump_wake_on_demand`, `mcla_poll_wait` or `timer_queue_hr_wait` still wins.

Checked: the code compiles in CI (host code against the SDK). Not run yet: the masks above in the final validation chain of v0.3.0 (short runs at 0xF, 0x55 and 0x155, the `CPU:` log lines). Not checked: a real 4-core CPU
without SMT, a CPU with efficiency cores (a core with one logical processor counts as one core, so 4 logical processors made of 1 core with SMT and 2 efficiency cores are 3 cores: applied),
more than 64 logical processors.
