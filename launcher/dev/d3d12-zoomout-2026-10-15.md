# D3D12 hitches in the map zoom-out, 2026-10-15

Windows 11, RTX 5080, Ryzen 7 9800X3D, the launcher's settings. The map
zoom-out is the loading screen after the title, when the camera pulls back over
the whole city and then dives to the player (`boot-to-roam.pad`, segment
`map_zoom`). The D3D12 against Vulkan benchmark (`vulkan-2026-10-14.md`) found
the same median frame on both there, but much bigger hitches on D3D12: 1% low 9
against 32 FPS at 3x, 18 against 50 at native, real time.

## What the hitches were

From the per-frame counters of those benchmark runs (`perf.csv`, the GPU
command thread) and then from CPU timers around the D3D12 calls that can stall
(`d3d12-hitch-timers.sdk.patch`, below):

- **Both APIs create the same textures:** about 3,000 new texture views per
  zoom-out (`texture_cache_misses` counts new SRV descriptors on D3D12 and new
  image views on Vulkan, about one per new texture).
- **D3D12 paid far more for each.** Every texture was a committed resource
  (`CreateCommittedResource`), an allocation of its own. Timed: 183-195 us to
  create one and about 80 us to release one. Vulkan sub-allocates textures
  from 256 MB blocks with VMA; its whole frame cost per new texture was about
  35 us.
- **Three moments in the zoom-out:**
  1. 0.8-1.4 s in: 130 to 630 new textures a frame, frames of 40-146 ms.
  2. About 3 s in, at 3x only: the texture cache goes over its hard memory
     limit (768 MB, plus 192 MB at 3x for render-to-texture) and evicts
     1,370-1,520 textures in one frame. Releasing them took 119 ms; the frame
     took 137-146 ms. It only created 8 textures, which first hid the cause.
  3. When the city appears (draws jump from about 900 to 3,600 a frame): 750 to
     890 new textures in one frame took 133-154 ms (and at 3x another 61 ms
     for 765 evictions). The frame took 160-250 ms; Vulkan's took 30-44 ms.
- **Not the cause:** the scaled-resolve tile heaps (6 a run, 0.5 ms each),
  render target creation (8 a run, about 1.8 ms each), shared-memory tile heaps,
  scratch buffer growth and descriptor heap pages; all small.
- **Left after the fix, on both APIs:** a 55 ms frame about 2.7-3.2 s in. It
  shows no timed GPU work, as on Vulkan, so it is the game's own.

## The fix

`patches/rexglue-d3d12-placed-texture-heaps.patch`, setting
`d3d12_texture_heaps` (default on). Textures are placed resources in shared
256 MB heaps, first fit, with freed neighbours merged. Textures over 128 MB stay
committed. Timed with it:

| Per texture | Committed | Placed |
|---|---|---|
| Create | 183-195 us | 8-11 us |
| Release | about 80 us | under 1 us |
| City-arrival frame (750-890 new textures) | 133-154 ms of creation | 4.1-4.5 ms |
| 3x evictions in one frame | 119 ms for 1,515 | 0.4 ms for 650-730 |

## Checks

- D3D12 debug layer (`--d3d12_debug=true`, its output read with a DBWIN
  listener): the zoom-out and the drive at native and at 3x, through the 3x
  eviction, gave no errors or corruption messages. The only warnings were the
  ones it gives without the patch: buffer initial states ignored, MIN/MAX blend
  factors ignored, and the live-object report at exit.
- Screenshots, timedemo, with the setting on and off: the map during the
  zoom-out, the car standing at the spawn point and two frames of the drive, at
  native and 3x. Every picture was whole and correct. The standing frames
  matched (mean difference 0.5 and 1.4 of 255). The zoom-out frames and the
  drive's traffic differed between runs, as the zoom-out is timed in real time
  and traffic is random.
- Soak: 15 minutes of scripted driving through the city at 3x, fullscreen,
  uncapped (74,778 frames, median 12.0 ms, 1% low 61.5 FPS), with the heaps'
  debug log (`--log_level=debug`). It exited cleanly. Five heaps (1.25 GB) were
  created by the end of the zoom-out; one was released 30 s later, and four
  (1 GB) served the whole drive with none created or released. The texture
  cache's own limit at 3x is 960 MB; the heaps' 1 to 1.25 GB is that plus
  gaps between textures and the spare heap. One frame of the drive took 164 ms. It created no
  texture, and the log shows a graphics pipeline being created at that moment,
  so it was a shader compile, not texture memory.

## Instrumentation

`d3d12-hitch-timers.sdk.patch` (fork only, every line marked `TMPHITCH`;
applies on top of the placed-heaps patch). Times texture creation, loading and
release, the scaled-resolve tile heaps and the wait for the submission thread
before them, shared-memory tile heaps, render target creation, scratch buffer
growth, fence waits and SRV descriptor pages, and logs one line per frame over
25 ms or with over 2 ms of timed work (`TMPHITCH frame N ms: ...` in the game's
log).
