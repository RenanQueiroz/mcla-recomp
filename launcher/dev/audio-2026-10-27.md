# Audio: the radio's balance, desync, and static in cutscenes (2026-10-27)

Reports: [#2](https://github.com/RenanQueiroz/mcla-recomp/issues/2) (the radio's balance "goes wild" after a couple of seconds; "the
same bug as Xenia; the fix on Xenia Edge was xma_decoder old to new") and #7 (a 6-core i5-10400 at 80-90 FPS: audio "goes crazy" in
cutscenes, reverb and static on voices, radio songs desynced). Nobody has heard it here; the work below is what could be found without
listening, and how to check it without listening. The SDK patch is `patches/rexglue-xma-decoder-fixes.patch` (its section in
`patches/README.md` has the changes and the credits).

## What the SDK decodes with

One XMA implementation, `src/audio/xma_context.cpp`: the state machine Xenia Canary calls `new`. Not Xenia's old decoder: Xenia Canary replaced
its own `XmaContextNew` with one derived from this SDK on 2026-05-11, and has had `new` as its default since 2025-12-01. So the "old to new"
fix of the Xenia reports has no counterpart here. What Canary and Xenia Edge changed in it afterwards is small (the patch lists the
commits). FFmpeg needs nothing newer (Canary/Edge use an 8.0 base with a rewritten `xmaframes` decoder; the frames decode the same; its
fixes are a flush callback, error handling and not skipping the first frame, none of which changes a good frame's samples). Licences: the SDK,
Xenia Canary and Xenia Edge are BSD 3-Clause (same text), so the code can be taken with its notice (`THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md`); the one
other prior art, ForzaHorizon1-ReImagined's patch 0105, is MIT.

## What the review found (to be measured)

1. `StoreContextMerged()` writes back `output_buffer_read_offset` from the snapshot taken when `Work()` began, though only the game ever
   changes it (it advances it as it consumes PCM). A decode that overlaps the game's consume rewinds the game's read pointer: blocks consumed
   twice ("reverb", "doubled" music, stutter). Xenia Canary and Edge have the same line.
2. `Work()` tests `is_enabled()` before the lock; a context the game has just locked is decoded anyway (ForzaHorizon1-ReImagined found the
   same). A kick wakes a worker sweep as well as decoding inline; when the worker wins the race for a context, the game goes on while the
   worker still decodes it.
3. Host scheduling: kernel thread priorities are ignored, so the audio worker (the guest's XAudio2 mixer, once per 5.33 ms) and the XMA
   thread compete at normal priority with ~15 game threads, and the audio client table sits behind the global lock the guest's interlocked
   helpers take. A callback that is late means an underrun: a frame of silence in the output stream (a click), and the voice's position falls
   behind the picture for good: lip-sync in cutscenes, "desync".

## What is measured, and how (nothing here needs listening)

`audio_stats` prints counters every few seconds and at exit, and `audio_dump_dir` writes files. Both are off by default.

- **What the game wrote during a decode** (`game wrote during a decode`): per `Work()` call, whether the game changed the read offset, a valid
  bit or anything else in the context while it ran. With `xma_race_fixes` off these writes are overwritten; on, they are kept. A non-zero
  `read_offset` with the switch off is the overwrite of finding 1 happening.
- **Who decodes** (`work inline` / `worker`): `Work()` calls on the kicking thread against the XMA worker thread (finding 2).
- **The pump** (`pump:`): the guest mixer callback's duration (`avg_us`, `max_us`, `over4ms`, `over8ms`: the frame period is 5.33 ms), the
  longest time between two callbacks (`gap_max_us`), callbacks more than 8 ms after the previous (`late`); `xma_us_per_pump` in the analysis
  is how much of it the inline XMA decodes take.
- **Underruns** (`sdl: underruns`, and the first twenty with their time in the log): callbacks that found no frame queued.
- **`mix.wav`** (32-bit float, what the device is given, before the mute): runs of exact digital silence in playing audio (`gaps`: the underruns as
  they sound), isolated discontinuities (`clicks`: a linear-prediction residual spike that is not followed by more), left/right level per second.
- **`xma_ctx<N>_s<M>.wav`**: what one XMA context decoded; the radio is the long stereo one. Left/right balance per second and how much it
  moves (`balance_step_p95`), windows in which one channel is silent while the other plays (`dropouts`), clicks.
- **The reference decode** (`--ref`): the packets that context was given (`.xma`, the input buffers in the order they were first seen) are wrapped in a
  WAVE/XMA2 container (`xma2_riff.py`) and decoded by FFmpeg's own `xma2` decoder; the two outputs are aligned by cross-correlation and
  compared (`blocks_mismatched_pct`: 1024-sample blocks more than 64 LSB apart). A constant lag with no mismatched blocks means the SDK's
  decoder equals the reference; mismatches mean it splices, repeats or drops audio somewhere (first time given).

Tools (`launcher/dev/audio/`): `audio_analyze.py` (one run), `analyze_all.py` (a set of batches: table, decision rules, listening clips),
`xma2_riff.py`, `check_smoke.py`, and self-tests on synthetic audio (`selftest_analyze.py`, `selftest_all.py`). They need numpy and FFmpeg
(`ffmpeg` in PATH): `python -P -E launcher/dev/audio/analyze_all.py ...` (not `-I`: numpy is in the user's site-packages).

## The runs

`launcher/dev/scripts/audio-route.pad`: the start-up and the title (the attract movie's sound), the map zoom-out, the city with the radio at
the spawn point, a drive. Real time (no `--fixed-dt`), `--intro normal`, D3D12, native, uncapped, the display awake, every run muted
(`--audio_mute=true`: the mix is dumped before the mute), the game in an affinity job of 12 logical processors (6 cores / 12 threads: the
i5-10400's shape; not its speed, cache or memory) or 8.

| batch | runs | question |
|---|---|---|
| S | one run of `boot-to-roam.pad`, every switch on | do the dumps and the statistics appear |
| A | warm-up, old, new, new, old (12 logical) | the decision: `old` = every switch off, `new` = `xma_race_fixes`, `xma_canary_fixes`, `audio_thread_priority=1`, `audio_client_lock` |
| B | race, canary, prio, new16 (12 logical) | which switch does what; `new16` = new with `audio_maxqframes=16` |
| C | old, new (8 logical) | a harder case |
| D | the shipped behaviour (`old`) with the frame cap at 30, 60, uncapped, 30, 60 (12 logical) | is the radio balance drift / desync frame-rate dependent (game-side timing: audio code ticked per frame) rather than a decoder matter; batch A's two uncapped `old` runs count as more uncapped samples |

Decision rules (`analyze_all.py` prints them): the switches may be turned on by default when `new` is not worse than `old` in decode errors,
underruns, gaps, clicks per minute (within a quarter), the radio's mismatch against the reference and its one-channel dropouts. A "no
regression" result is not evidence that the reports are fixed unless `old` shows the effect (underruns, late pumps, overwritten read offsets,
dropouts) at all; on a PC with 16 logical processors it may not.

## Results

Batches A to D ran (17 runs, 12 and 8 logical processors of a 16-thread PC, exit code 0 in all, the real save untouched). Read them in this
order.

**1. The radio is two mono streams, and the shipped decoder loses frames in them.** The game plays a stereo song as two mono XMA contexts (one
per channel, 44.1 kHz, started within 10 ms of each other, 600 input packets refilled in blocks of about 31). `radio_pairs.py` pairs them
and compares each channel with FFmpeg's decode of the packets that context was given (`radio_all.py` does it for every run; the table is
`RADIO.md` of the work folder):

| decoder | runs | frames lost in 95 s | blocks off the reference | left/right correlation at lag 0 | left/right best alignment off by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| shipped (every switch off; race only; priority only) | 11 | 6 to 11 | 50 to 100 % | about 0 (-0.12 to 0.63) | 512 to 2048 samples for most of the stream |
| `xma_canary_fixes` (all on; canary only; with 16 frames of queue; 8 logical processors) | 6 | 0 | 0 to 0.2 % | 0.76 to 0.95 | 0 |

The lost frames are whole 512-sample XMA frames, each channel loses them at its own moments (10 to 20 s apart), so the two channels drift
apart by 512, 1024, 1536 samples (11 to 35 ms): the stereo image wanders (the reported "balance gone wild"), the channels comb against each
other, each loss is a discontinuity, and the song runs ahead of its time. `xma_race_fixes` and `audio_thread_priority` do not change it; the Canary
commits `6bef1fbed` (a frame left partly handed out when a pass exhausts the input buffer) and `aee0871dd` (a frame whose header starts in the last
bits of a packet) are the cause, and `xma_canary_fixes` is now on by default. It reproduces at every frame rate (30, 60, uncapped) and at 8 and 12
logical processors, which is why the frame-rate test (batch D) found "no dependence": there is nothing frame-rate dependent in a decoder that
drops frames on its own schedule.

**2. The rest of the earlier summary was measurement artefacts.** The SDL underruns (47 to 57 per run in every variant) are all in the first 0.3 s
of the run (the output device starts before the game's mixer has made its first frame); after the first second no run had one, except one 30 FPS run
in which the whole process stood still for 240 ms once (the XMA decode that was running took 237 ms): the queue never fell below 4 of 8 frames. The
"late guest mixer callbacks" (about half of all) are the output device asking for 480 samples every 10 ms and so for two 256-sample frames at once: the
gap between two callbacks is 10 ms and 0.5 ms alternately; the guest mixer ran at 187.2 to 187.4 callbacks a second against 187.5 nominal, and the radio
streams were consumed at 1.0005 to 1.0009 of wall-clock speed. The 146 clicks per minute of `new16` were a bursty detector hit in a quiet noisy passage
(the clicks are not aligned to the 256-sample frame and their amplitudes are 0.01 to 0.08), not a splice. The audioFrameTimer thread's 7 ms sleep
(branch `cpu/hot-paths-v013`) is not in the mixer's path: the mixer is paced by the output device's consumption (a semaphore of credits, one per frame
the device played).

**3. Not known yet: a weaker CPU.** Nothing on this PC starves the audio worker. `audio_thread_priority` 3 (MMCSS "Pro Audio") and
`audio_adaptive_queue` are in the patch, off, with counters (`underruns_steady`, `silence_max`, the wake histogram) that separate a real underrun
from the start-up silence; batch E (4 and 16 logical processors, the pump-sleep cap `mcla_pump_sleep_max_ms` 1, 2 and the shipped value) measures them.
Batch E results: see the work folder (not in this document yet).

Listening clips (not committed): `radio-<run>-decoder.wav` is the pair as the shipped decoder produced it, `radio-<run>-reference.wav` the same input
decoded by FFmpeg at the same song time, `radio-<run>-mix.wav` the device mix.
