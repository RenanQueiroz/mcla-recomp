# Anisotropic filtering: what the game asks for, how it switches, what it costs (2026-10-27)

The SDK's `anisotropic_override` (`src/graphics/pipeline/texture/cache.cpp`, default 3) forces anisotropic filtering on every texture that has mip levels, linear
min and mag filters and a point or linear mip filter, whatever the game's sampler asks for (-1 leaves the game's own choice; 0 is off, 1 to 5 are 1x, 2x, 4x, 8x, 16x).
The fork never showed it. It is a setting now: the launcher's *Anisotropic filtering* (after *FSR upscaling*) and the pause menu's PERFORMANCE > ANISOTROPIC FILTERING
(GAME DEFAULT, OFF, 2X, 4X, 8X, 16X; 4X, the SDK's default, stays the default). 1x has no row: it is trilinear, which OFF already is for all but the textures that ask
for a point mip filter (9 to 12% of the eligible ones, below).

## What the game asks for

`launcher/dev/sampler-tally.sdk.patch` (a development patch, applied with `gt-build.ps1 -Layer sampler-tally`) counts, for every sampler worked out for a draw, the
level the draw's fetch constant holds, whether the texture has mip levels, and its three filters. RTX 5080, Direct3D 12, native resolution; the city is free roam and
a short drive (8.4 million samplers), the race the start of Sunset and Vine and ten seconds of driving (33.6 million):

| | city | race |
|---|---|---|
| samplers eligible for the override (mip levels, linear min and mag, point or linear mip filter) | 33% | 30% |
| samplers of textures without mip levels (render targets, the HUD, shadow and environment maps), which the override never changes | 64% | 70% |
| of the eligible, the level the game asks for: none | 36% | 35% |
| 2x | 1.8% | 4.4% |
| 4x | 52% | 27% |
| 8x | 7.8% | 7.8% |
| 16x | 2.7% | 26% |
| of the eligible, with a point mip filter (the override makes them trilinear) | 12% | 9% |

Every shader's own anisotropy is "use the fetch constant", except the fetches with an explicit LOD (those say none, as the SDK's translators decided). So the SDK's 4x
is the console's own for about half of the eligible textures in the city, adds 4x to a third that had none, and cuts the game's 8x and 16x (10% of the eligible in the
city, 34% in the race: the road) to 4x. *Game default* is therefore not above 4x everywhere, and 16x is the only value that is never below what the game asks for.
The tally weighs samplers per draw, not pixels.

## A change reaches the samplers at once, on both APIs

D3D12: `D3D12TextureCache::GetSamplerParameters` runs for every draw (`D3D12CommandProcessor::UpdateBindings`); the draw compares the result with the samplers the
last draw used, and a difference writes the descriptors again (bindless: the sampler map is keyed by the parameters; bindful: the heap is written again). Vulkan:
`VulkanTextureCache::GetSamplerParameters` per draw, and `UseSampler` finds the `VkSampler` for those parameters (created on first use, least recently used ones
retired). The option is `kHotReload`. The dev pad's `set anisotropic_override <n>` and the pause menu row both changed the pictures within the frame (the cost pads
below step it eight times a run). On Vulkan the level is clamped to the device's `maxSamplerAnisotropy` and to 1 where `samplerAnisotropy` is missing.

## What it costs

Method (the pads are `aniso-cost.pad` and `detail-cost.pad` of the scratch folder of the session that wrote this): the arcade race Sunset and Vine (one opponent, clear, noon), five seconds
of full throttle (a long straight street ahead), then the game's timer frozen with `mcla_freeze` and the throttle still held, and the option stepped through its values inside one run. The freeze is not complete in a race: the
race clock and the traffic stop, but the car's physics still takes a small step every frame, so the car and the chase camera creep (the HUD's speed went from 37 to 42 mph in two minutes; releasing
the throttle made the camera ease backwards for seconds): pictures 20 frames apart are the same, pictures a minute apart are not, and the drift shows in the numbers below as the spread of the base segments. Each
anisotropic value is measured twice, in the order 4x, game default, off, 2x, 8x, 16x, 16x, 8x, 2x, off, game default, 4x (so a linear drift cancels), 2 s to settle and 12 s
timed; each detail option is measured against the base segments before and after it (8 s timed). PresentMon's frame time and its GPU busy per present, per segment. Exclusive
lock, display awake, fixed 1/60 s steps, uncapped, windowed, `--hdr=off`, scratch save; the Radeon is the integrated one of a Ryzen 9800X3D (`--vulkan_device=1` /
`--d3d12_adapter=1`, Vulkan being what Automatic picks there), the RTX 5080 at 3x is where the frame is GPU bound (GPU busy equals the frame time). The Radeon has slow hours (1.4x), so
only segments of one run are compared. The two instances of one value differ by 0.1 ms on average (0.28 ms at most); the base segments of a detail run drift by 1.6 to 5% over its
six minutes (5% on the 5080: an option that saves less than 0.3 ms there is not seen).

| value | RTX 5080, D3D12, 3x: frame / GPU | Radeon iGPU, Vulkan, 1x: frame / GPU | Radeon iGPU, D3D12, 1x: frame / GPU |
|---|---|---|---|
| Off | 8.54 (+0.03) / 8.54 (+0.04) | 38.24 (-0.42) / 38.20 (-0.23) | 41.87 (-0.35) / 41.86 (-0.19) |
| Game default | 8.53 (+0.02) / 8.52 (+0.02) | 38.32 (-0.34) / 38.28 (-0.16) | 42.04 (-0.18) / 42.05 (-0.01) |
| 2x | 8.56 (+0.05) / 8.56 (+0.06) | 38.27 (-0.39) / 38.23 (-0.21) | 41.93 (-0.30) / 41.94 (-0.12) |
| 4x (default) | 8.50 (+0.00) / 8.50 (+0.00) | 38.66 (+0.00) / 38.44 (+0.00) | 42.22 (+0.00) / 42.05 (+0.00) |
| 8x | 8.48 (-0.02) / 8.48 (-0.02) | 38.43 (-0.23) / 38.39 (-0.05) | 42.10 (-0.13) / 42.10 (+0.04) |
| 16x | 8.44 (-0.07) / 8.44 (-0.06) | 38.74 (+0.08) / 38.69 (+0.25) | 42.49 (+0.26) / 42.48 (+0.43) |

(the two instances of a value differ by 0.10 ms on average, at most 0.28 ms)

Reading: **there is no measurable cost on the RTX 5080 at 3x** (all six values within 0.12 ms of 8.5 ms, the spread of two runs of one value; 16x came out 0.06 ms faster than 4x),
and **at most 1.3 to 1.5% on the integrated Radeon at native resolution** (16x against off: +0.50 ms of 38.2 on Vulkan, +0.61 ms of 41.9 on Direct3D 12; 4x against off +0.42 and
+0.35; the p99 moves the same way). GPU busy is within 0.2% of the frame time on the Radeon too, so this is GPU time. The scene is a long street at a slant, the case anisotropic filtering exists for,
and the GPU there is a 2-compute-unit one; a GPU with fewer texture units, or one short of memory bandwidth, was not available. So 16x is the value for any GPU here, and 4x
costs nearly what 16x does.

## The Low-end preset, and a Lowest tier

- **Anisotropic filtering is not in the Low-end preset:** off saves 0.4 to 0.6 ms (1 to 1.5%) on the iGPU and nothing on the 5080, for the smear on every road and pavement
  (pictures: off against 4x against 16x on the road ahead and beside the car). Keep it at its default.
- **A Lowest tier is worth having, with one option in it:** Low-end plus SHADOWS off is 26.9% off the Radeon's frame (34.2 to 28.8 ms: 5.4 ms more than Low-end alone, 15.8% of its frame)
  and 17% (28% of the GPU time) at 3x on the 5080. Nothing else measured adds anything. It costs the sun's shadows altogether (cars lose the shadow under them, buildings lose theirs).
  A cheaper stage in between does not exist yet: the SHADOWS row is on or off, the fourth cascade alone is 2.85 ms of the iGPU's frame (`gpu-weak-2026-10-27.md`).
