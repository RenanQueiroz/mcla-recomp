# CPU: the main thread's wait, the timer thread at a cap, the GPU command thread (2026-10-28)

Status: **written and compiled by CI, nothing run in the game yet.** Every option below is off by default. The numbers in this note
are the earlier measurements it starts from (marked as such); the measurements of these changes are in the chain described at the
end, and go in a section "Results" when it has run.

## What the v0.2.0 numbers say

the timer queue's high-resolution wait and no wake at 5 or fewer), D3D12, native, the city, Ryzen 7 9800X3D limited to 16 and to 4
logical processors (job object). Means of 3 runs (2 at 4 logical) from the A/B of `profiling-2026-10-26.md`, "Wake on demand" and
"The timer queue and the frame-pacing thread by processor count". ms of CPU per swap:

| 16 logical, 120 FPS cap | 8.33 ms | 21.2 | 8.33 | 4.32 | 3.00 | 2.69 | 1.02 | 0.21 | 1.63 |

At a cap, which is how the game is played (its own default is 30 FPS), two threads use CPU that does no work:

- **The timer thread** (6 or more logical processors): one whole core, 16.6 of 34.4 ms a frame at 60 FPS. Measured before (wake off,
  6 logical): its high-resolution wait cut the process's CPU by 40 to 50% at the caps with the frame and p99 unchanged, and cost
  0.4 to 1.7% of frame time uncapped, which is why it is off there.
- **The main thread**: 8.5 to 8.8 ms a frame at 60 FPS and 17 to 17.5 ms at 30, a half of a core, while its whole CPU, polling included, is 2.7 to
  3.3 ms uncapped at 240 FPS. It is in the loop of `sub_82148A10` that waits for a free slot of the command ring
  (`Sleep(0)` and two clock readings a turn). Measured before (v0.1.3, uncapped, wake off): `--mcla_poll_wait` cut it by 61 to 64%
  and the process by 17 to 19%, and failed the frame-time rule once by 0.1 point.

## The options (all off by default)

| Option | Branch | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `mcla_timer_hr_at_cap` | `cpu/timer-at-cap` | While a frame-rate cap is set, `timer_queue_hr_wait` is on (6 or more logical processors; below that it is on anyway); uncapped, the value the game started with applies. A value the player set is never changed. |
| `mcla_poll_pause_turns` | `cpu/poll-loop` | A pause spin of N reads of the ring's word ahead of the game's `Sleep(0)`; skips the `Sleep(0)` if the word changed. The wait ends at the same moments, with fewer system calls. It does not give CPU back. |
| `mcla_poll_wait` (exists) | `cpu/poll-loop` | The main thread sleeps on a timer and the event the freeing thread sets, between a short spin (`mcla_poll_spin_us`, 40) and a spin before the loop's deadline. The measurements to repeat on v0.2.0, with the wake on demand, at 16, 8 and 4 logical processors and at the caps. |
| `gpu_float_constants_run_copy` | `cpu/command-thread` | `patches/rexglue-gpu-float-constants-run-copy.patch`: a draw's float constants copied a run of consecutive constants at a time. The same bytes. D3D12 and Vulkan. |

Who frees the ring's slots is known (it was not when the task was written): the only store to a word at +4 of a ring is the one in
`sub_82160088`, called by `sub_82144888`, the loop of the frame-pacing thread; the word at +0 is stored by the main thread itself
(`profiling-2026-10-26.md`, "The wait for a ring slot"). `mcla_poll_release` (a midasm hook after that store) already wakes a
sleeping main thread. What was missing was a wake-on-demand build to measure it on; v0.2.0 is one.

## The chain

`scratchpad/cpu3/chain.ps1` (the author's work folder, not in the repository): builds the integration branch `cpu/cpu3-chain`
(v0.2.0 and the three branches above, the SDK with DWARF line tables) and runs, in one exclusive hold of at most 45 minutes, on the
RTX 5080 at native resolution:

1. **in-run A/B** (`launcher/dev/scripts/cpu3-toggle.pad`): the car stands at the spawn point and the options are switched every 4
   seconds, off against `mcla_poll_wait`, the same with `mcla_poll_spin_us` 300, `mcla_poll_pause_turns` 128 and
   `gpu_float_constants_run_copy`, uncapped and at 60 FPS, at 16 and at 4 logical processors; every state window is compared with
   the mean of the off windows on both sides of it (frame median and p99, CPU per swap by thread);
2. **alternating A/B** (`cpu3-ab.pad`: standing, driving, caps 120, 60, 30), 2 runs a side in turn: off against on (the poll wait and
   the float constants) at 16, 8 and 4 logical, plus `mcla_timer_hr_at_cap` at 8 and the wake on demand with the poll wait at 4;
   audio counters in every run;
3. **sampling profiles** of the shipped game (D3D12 at 16 and 4 logical in the city and a race, a 60 FPS cap, Vulkan at 16) with
   line tables, reported by thread, with the GPU command thread's call tree and the source lines of `UpdateBindings`,
   `RequestRanges` and the other hot functions.

## Reading the results

Rule for turning an option on by default: the frame median at most 1.0% and p99 at most 3.0% above the off side in every segment,
the caps held within 1%, 0 steady underruns, the game clock within 0.01. The CPU columns are what the options are for.
