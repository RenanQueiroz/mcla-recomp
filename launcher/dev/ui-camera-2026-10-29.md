# The pause menu's camera and 3D panels, drawn between the script's steps (2026-10-29)

Owner's report, after trying Smooth UI at 3x, a 120 FPS cap, with a controller: "The UI feels amazing to use now, the navigation is super smooth, and the controls respond correctly", but "the camera movements that happen when entering/exiting the pause menu and when going into sub menus is still kinda janky and not smooth".

**Status:** `mcla_ui_interp_camera` (on by default, only counts while `mcla_ui_interpolate` is on). Everything below is **read from the recompiled game and the pause movie's script, not run**. The code compiled in CI; the first run of it in the game is the check chain at the end. Where a number is computed, it says so.

## What moves, and why it was in steps

The pause menu is not only a Flash movie drawn flat. Its page is a quad in the 3D world, seen at an angle, with a separate 3D frame mesh around it, and the picture behind it is the game's own world seen from a camera the menu's script moves. The display-list blending of Smooth UI cannot see any of that: none of it is a display-list node.

All of it is set by the movie's script (the `PAUSEMOVIE` ActionScript 2, read earlier for the settings pages: `C:\mcv\menulayout\work\dec1.txt`, `dec2.txt`; frame script at 0x026a0c to 0x029006 of `dec2.txt`) **once per movie step, 30 times a second**, and the game reads the numbers again in every update or drawn frame. At 120 FPS that is four identical frames and then a jump.

| What | Script variables (per step, in order) | Where the game reads it | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blend between the game's own camera and the menu's**, 0 to 1 | `player0_cam_ctrl`: opening, `temp_player0_cam_ctrl_bend += 3` (0 to 100) and `ctrl = sin(acos(1 - bend/100))`, so 0.24 after the first step, 0.34, 0.42, 0.48, 0.53 ... 1 after about 34 steps (1.1 s); closing, the same backwards (`1 - sin(acos(bend/100))`); 1 at once when `player0_cam_skip_bend` is set | `sub_827238B0` (below), `lfs f0,80(r1)` at 0x827238F8 | every update |
| **The menu's camera**: eye, target, field of view | `player0_cam.position`, `.lookat`, `.fovy`, each eased toward the page's camera by `1/transitionspeed` of the distance per step (`transitionspeed` is `camera_coord[cur_camera][10]`: 5 for every page; 15 in two other pause modes). `camera_coord` has 40 rows: row 0 (`cur_camera` 0: eye (0, 1.8, 2.8), target (0, 1.8, 0), fov 43, menu at (-0.6, 2.2, 0)) and 39 identical ones (eye (0, 1.8, 4), target (0, 2, 0), fov 35, menu at (0, 2, 0)) | `sub_82722A10` (via `sub_82723570`) reads `position` and `lookat` with `sub_82722440` and `fovy` with `sub_822222F0` | every update |
| **The panels** (the page's quad, the frame mesh, the background): place, turn, size, alpha | scene graph `_global.scenegraph = [menu_group]`, `menu_group.meshes = [screen_mesh, resultsPanel_mesh, backgroundmesh]`; `menu_group.trans/scale/rot` from `cur_MenuPosition` (eased `/5` per step), `cur_MenuScale`, `cur_menuRotation` (`/5`); `screen_mesh.scale/trans` from `cur_width` and `cur_height` (eased `/3` per step toward the list's size, which is why a wider page grows the frame in a few steps); `screen_mesh._alpha = meshalpha * 0.5` | the scene draw `sub_82724CF0` (-> `sub_82724838` -> `sub_82723120` reads `rot`, `scale`, `trans` with `sub_82722440`; `sub_82724220` reads `_alpha` with `sub_822222F0`) | every drawn frame |

How the camera gets to the screen (addresses are guest addresses):

1. `mcUIManager::Update` `sub_821FC588` (0x821FC588) steps each movie with `sub_82720898` (0x82720898): `[wrapper+68]` enabled, then the context's virtual advance (vtable slot +4), then `sub_82724010`.
2. Every UI movie's player context is of one class (vtable 0x820CACB8, built by `sub_82724EE0`). Its advance is **`sub_827238B0`** (0x827238B0): the base advance `sub_825EDC08` (where the fork's step hook gates the movie to 30 a second), then `GetFloat(ctx, "player0_cam_ctrl")`; when the value is not 0 it stores it at `ctx+560` and calls `sub_82723570(ctx, "player0", ctx+564, ctx+496)`, which builds the camera matrix (64 bytes at `ctx+496`) from `player0_cam.position/lookat` and returns the field of view (stored at `ctx+568`; `ctx+564` is the object's `projection` number). This runs in **every update**, whether or not the movie stepped. With the movie not due, the numbers it reads are unchanged, so the result is the same.
3. `sub_821FC588` zeroes `mgr+4200` at 0x821FC6F4, then, for the movie of the object at `mgr+44` (0x821FC7F4 to 0x821FC84C) and for each enabled movie of its map (0x821FC93C to 0x821FC988), copies `ctx+560` to `mgr+4200`, the matrix to `mgr+4336` (64 bytes), `ctx+564` to `mgr+4400` and `ctx+568` to `mgr+4404` when `ctx+560` is not 0.
4. The game's camera function `sub_8220AA68` (0x8220AA68, called from `sub_822C0320`) reads them (0x8220ABC4, 0x8220AC8C to 0x8220ACF0): with `mgr+4200` 1.0 it uses the menu's matrix as it is, between 0 and 1 it mixes the game's own view with it (`sub_82205A18`), and when the value jumps between exactly 0 and 1 it calls `sub_82207EF0` (inferred: it tells the camera code of a cut).

So the camera the player sees is `mix(game's own, menu's, ctrl)`, and `ctrl`, the menu's eye, target and field of view all change only when the movie steps. The first step on opening moves the blend 0.24 of the way, the next 0.10, and so on: that is the jump the owner sees. The panels are the same: the script's numbers change per step and the draw reads them every frame.

Not moved by the script and so not in the problem: the pause menu's 2D nodes (Smooth UI blends them), the world behind the menu (the game's own view, which the blend above mixes with the menu's camera), and the `target0` render-target cameras (`sub_82723ED8`, once per step; the pause movie does not define `target0_cam`).

## The fix

Both ways of reading the numbers go through two small functions, one for an array of three numbers (`sub_82722440`) and one for one number (`sub_822222F0`). Hooks (`config/frame_timing.toml`, code in `src/frame_timing.cpp`, `UiSceneBlend`) sit on their success path (0x82722524, 0x82722374), read the property's name (the pointer saved on the stack: 96(r1) and 80(r1)) and, for `position`, `lookat`, `trans`, `rot`, `scale`, `fovy` and `_alpha`, replace the numbers with the blend, by **the rule of the display list**:

- the series (object, name) keeps what the script set at its last step (`to`), where the glide started from (`from`: what was shown, so nothing jumps back), when it started (`t0` = the step's time on its 30 a second schedule, the same `step_us` the nodes use) and what was shown;
- it is shown at `from + (to - from) * alpha`, `alpha` = the time since `t0` over the step period (`mcla_ui_interp_window_pct`), with one time for the whole update or drawn frame (the same frame time as the nodes);
- one step behind, as the nodes: a value reaches the game one step period after the script set it;
- shown at once (a cut): a series that is new, not read for 200 ms or for more than 3 steps, or a change longer than its limit (distance of the numbers): `position` and `lookat` 25, `trans` 8, `rot` 1.2, `scale` 0.6, `fovy` 20, the blend 0.5. These are far above one eased step (a few tenths) and below the script's own jumps (the 2D layout sets the camera 46 units away at a field of view of 10; skipping the bend sets the blend to 1 at once). A cut is logged (`ui interp event: .. kind=snap why=scene3d`);
- nothing at all when the frame time is over 0.6 of a step (under 50 FPS), when `mcla_ui_timing_fix` is off, or when the context is not paced by the step hook.

The blend value itself (`player0_cam_ctrl`) is blended at 0x827238F8 (a hook after the `lfs`, which can replace `f0`). While its last step to 0 glides, `f0` stays above 0 so that the update still builds the camera. Everything is gated by where it is read: the hooks at 0x827238F8 and 0x82723934 (inside the advance) and at the entry and the last jump of `sub_82724CF0` (the scene draw) open and close a window (the context in a thread-local), and a read outside it is left alone. A script's own call into the game's camera (`sub_822082D8`) and the lights are such reads.

The camera is built from the blended eye and target, not from a blend of matrices, so the view stays a rotation. The panels' matrices are built by the game from the blended `trans`, `rot` and `scale`.

Why not blend at the end (the matrix in the manager, or the final camera)? Matrices of rotations blended element by element shrink in the middle; blending the inputs lets the game's own arithmetic do the rest, and it covers the panels, whose matrices exist only inside the draw.

## Check chain (`C:\mcv\uicam\chain`, one exclusive hold, built from GitHub)

`launcher/dev/ui_camera.py` writes the pad and reads the log (`pad`, `report`).

- **cam** (1x, D3D12, per-frame log): from the city, Start (the pause menu opens, `copen`), LB then RB (a tab change, `ctab`), A then B (a submenu and back, `csub`), Start (closes, `cclose`), with the option off, on, off, on at a 120 FPS cap and off, on at 60; then bursts of 20 consecutive pictures right after the press for the opening, the submenu and the closing (cap 120, off and on).
- **mi** (menu input, the shipped defaults, cap 120 and uncapped) and **dur** (the pause menu's closing and opening timed from the press; its report line `PASS arithmetic` compares every blended node with the game's own).

Verdicts: `smooth camera|panels <move> cap N` (different camera or panel states drawn per 33 ms step of movement, median: 1 with the option off, at least 3 at cap 120, at least 2 at 60); `transition copen|cclose cap N` (the blend takes the same time to go from 0.1 to 0.9 off and on: the script's own within one step, what the game got within one step and a frame; and the delay of its middle, by design about one step); `NOTE cuts`; `PASS arithmetic`; `burst` (consecutive pictures that differ). `PASS` only means the numbers held; the contact sheet of the bursts (`out\cam-bursts.png`) is for the eyes.
