# Weak GPUs: where the frame goes, and what was done (2026-10-27)

The GPU work of this fork was tuned on an RTX 5080, where a frame is bound by the game thread and by the cost of each draw. This note is
about the other end: the integrated Radeon of an AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D ("AMD Radeon(TM) Graphics": 2 RDNA2 compute units, 486 MiB of its
own memory and 15.9 GiB shared with the DDR5, driver 26.8.1), on Windows 11, Direct3D 12 and Vulkan, `--hdr=off`, windowed, 2x MSAA, the
scripted city run (`launcher/dev/devrun.py`, fixed 1/60 s steps, uncapped). It plays the game at 25 to 30 FPS at native resolution, 7 FPS
at 2x and 2.5 FPS at 3x, and the 3D engine is 96 to 99.5% busy: the frame is bound by the GPU. Other weak GPUs will differ in size, not in
kind (a GTX 1650 has 14 compute units and its own memory).

## Where the GPU time goes (native resolution, free roam, GPU timestamps)

Median of ten frames, ms, Direct3D 12 (the timestamp layers of `launcher/dev/gpu-timestamps*.sdk.patch`, `gt-phases.py`; the RTX 5080 for
reference).

| | Radeon 1x | Radeon 2x | RTX 5080 1x |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPU frame | 38.9 | 178.7 | 4.8 |
| Before the main view (shadow cascades, environment map, reflection) | 12.2 | 46.4 | 2.1 |
| The scene | 17.2 | 86.6 | 2.6 |
| Post | 4.9 | 22.5 | 0.4 |
| Tone map | 3.0 | 17.1 | 0.07 |
| Draws | 24.4 | 97.2 | |
| Resolves (render target to shared memory) | 8.2 | 50.8 | |
| Texture loads (shared memory to texture) | 3.8 | 15.7 | |
| EDRAM transfers, barriers | 1.5, 1.0 | 10.3, 4.9 | |

Copies and barriers are 37% of the frame at native and 46% at 2x. The scene's targets are resolved to memory and loaded again as textures,
the same screen-sized surfaces every frame, and on this GPU a pass over memory costs far more than on the 5080 (15 to 20 times, against 4 to
5 times for the draws).

## The resolves were waiting for memory, not moving it

The resolves took 8.2 ms of 38.9 at native (shadow mask 1.3, the HDR colour 1.25, the depth 0.87 and 0.70, the four 640x640 4x MSAA shadow
cascades 0.78, 0.70, 0.69 and 0.63) at 10 to 16 GB/s, while the texture load decode, a pass over memory of the same size, reached 50 GB/s on
this GPU; and 4 times the pixels (2x) cost 6.2 times as long. The direct resolve shaders (`rexglue-direct-resolve.patch`) loaded the samples
of a thread's 4 or 8 pixels one at a time, in loops kept rolled so that the conversions are there once, with a conditional on the format in
the loop: each load of a multisampled render target (through its FMASK and metadata) was waited for before the next one was issued, and a
GPU with 2 compute units does not have enough threads in flight to hide that. `rexglue-resolve-direct-batched-loads.patch` issues a thread's
loads together. Measured with the first version (a thread's loads together, the samples of the averaging copies still one after the other), four
runs off, on, on, off, Direct3D 12, native resolution, GPU timestamps: the resolves fell from 8.5 to 5.8 ms a frame, the depth ones by
half (the 1280x720 ones 0.87 to 0.49 and 0.70 to 0.34 ms, the four cascades 2.80 to 1.40 ms), the shadow mask and the HDR colour only from
1.31 to 1.22 and from 1.25 to 1.08 to 1.12 ms; the frame went from 39.7 to 36.9 ms in free roam (-7%) and from 38.4 to 35.9 ms driving
(-6.5%). The two colour copies are bound by arithmetic instead: the 7e3 conversion and the unpacking, for every sample of every pixel, are
some 120 operations, 1.8 million sample-pixels at 2 compute units of about 0.3 trillion operations a second is 0.8 ms of the 1.2. Loading
the two samples together changed nothing (a run in a slow state of the GPU, in which every category took 1.4 times as long, gave the same
ratios), and the next step, skipping the second sample's conversion where it equals the first's (exact: the samples of most pixels of a
multisampled picture are the same), is the one for that: it is in the SDK tree of the scratch build (C:\mcv\gpuw), not in the patch yet, and not
measured. The slow state is worth knowing
about: on some hours the integrated GPU does everything 1.4 times slower (the 2x runs of 2026-10-26 that came out 1.5 times slow were
that), so only runs next to each other, in the order off, on, on, off, can be compared.

## Options for weak GPUs

- **How often the off-screen passes are drawn** (`mcla_far_shadow_interval`, `mcla_env_map_interval`, `mcla_reflection_interval`; 0 is every frame,
  as shipped; also in the pause menu's PERFORMANCE tab). Before the main view the game draws, with the Radeon's times at native: the four sun-shadow
  cascades (1.35, 1.27, 1.43 and 2.83 ms, the last also redrawing the foliage impostors), the environment map of the car paint and the speedometer
  (1.6 and 1.5 ms), the night shadow texture (1.4 ms) and the reflection view (0.5 ms): 12.2 ms of 38.9 together with the others. A pass
  left out of a frame keeps its textures from the last time it was drawn: the render phase loop (`sub_822E6408`, `src/render_markers.cpp`) draws
  the phases set in the renderer's mask of enabled phases, which the game's own "noshadows" clears too (`mcla_shadows`), and the mask is cleared of
  the passes left out for that frame and restored after it. The options' frames are offset from each other. Far shadows (the two farthest
  cascades) lag the camera by up to the interval; a car's environment map and the reflections are slow-changing. Not measured yet.
- **A preset** (`mcla_preset` = `low`): anti-aliasing off (-9% at native, -26% at 2x, `m1`, `m2a`), the full-screen blur off, far shadows every 2nd
  frame, the environment map every 4th, the reflection every 2nd. The options it stands for take its values as their defaults, so one set by
  hand wins. The launcher has it as *Graphics preset*. Not measured yet as a whole.
- **Automatic graphics API** is Vulkan on AMD and Intel graphics (`src/mcla_app.cpp`): on the Radeon Vulkan is 6 to 10% faster at native
  (35.7 against 39.5 ms in free roam, three runs each; GPU time 36.4 against 38.9 ms) and 2.6% at 2x; Intel graphics take the SDK's slower
  rasterizer-ordered-view path on Direct3D 12 and are not measured. NVIDIA stays on Direct3D 12.

## What was not done, and why

- **Resolving straight into the host texture that is sampled next** (the resolve's write to shared memory and the texture load that reads it
  back are 9 to 10 ms of 39 at native). The texture's identity (address, size, pitch, format) is only known at the draw that samples it, after
  the resolve; the resolve would have to write the host texture speculatively as well as the memory (so as to stay exact: the guest can read the
  same memory as another format), in 7e3, float and depth formats that each have their own conversion in the load shaders, and without the
  memory write the texture would need a write-back when anything else reads the range. What it could save is the texture load, 3.8 ms at
  native and about 1.5 ms of that is the decode a dual-write resolve would still have to do; a quarter of the frame is not within reach for
  the risk.
- **Rendering the off-screen passes at a lower scale than the screen** (at 2x the shadow cascades, the environment map and the reflection
  view are 4x the pixels: 46 of 179 ms). The resolution scale is global in the render target cache, the resolve shaders and the texture
  cache (addresses in the scaled resolve buffer are the guest's times the scale), and the cascades are 640x640 pixels of 4x MSAA depth whose
  four samples the resolve turns into a 1280x1280 atlas: a per-render-target scale means a second set of addresses for everything. The
  options above (drawing those passes less often) are the cheap way to the same end.
- **Texture loads without the copy for unscaled resolved textures** (`texture_load_direct` only covers resolution-scaled ones): 2.2 ms of the
  3.8 ms at native on Direct3D 12. With Vulkan the default on AMD and Intel graphics, only the NVIDIA-only Direct3D 12 path would gain, and
  that is where the copy costs least.
- **Per-draw costs** (4,000 draws a frame, 93% under 10 microseconds): the draws that cost the Radeon time are the ones that shade many pixels;
  the rest is a floor of about 1.7 microseconds a draw that state deduplication in the SDK already works on.
