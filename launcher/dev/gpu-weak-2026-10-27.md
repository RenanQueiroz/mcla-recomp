# Weak GPUs: where the frame goes, and what was done (2026-10-27)

The GPU work of this fork was tuned on an RTX 5080, where a frame is bound by the game thread and by the cost of each draw. This note is
about the other end: the integrated Radeon of an AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D ("AMD Radeon(TM) Graphics": 2 RDNA2 compute units, 486 MiB of its
own memory and 15.9 GiB shared with the DDR5, driver 26.8.1), on Windows 11, Direct3D 12 and Vulkan, `--hdr=off`, windowed, 2x MSAA, the
scripted city run (`launcher/dev/devrun.py`, fixed 1/60 s steps, uncapped). It plays the game at 25 to 30 FPS at native resolution, 7 FPS
at 2x and 2.5 FPS at 3x, and the 3D engine is 96 to 99.5% busy: the frame is bound by the GPU. Other weak GPUs will differ in size, not in
kind (a GTX 1650 has 14 compute units and its own memory).

## Where the GPU time goes (native resolution, free roam, GPU timestamps)

Median of ten frames, ms, Direct3D 12 (the timestamp layers of `launcher/dev/gpu-timestamps*.sdk.patch`, `gt-phases.py`; the RTX 5080 for
reference).

| | Radeon 1x | Radeon 2x | RTX 5080 1x |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPU frame | 38.9 | 178.7 | 4.8 |
| Before the main view (shadow cascades, environment map, reflection) | 12.2 | 46.4 | 2.1 |
| The scene | 17.2 | 86.6 | 2.6 |
| Post | 4.9 | 22.5 | 0.4 |
| Tone map | 3.0 | 17.1 | 0.07 |
| Draws | 24.4 | 97.2 | |
| Resolves (render target to shared memory) | 8.2 | 50.8 | |
| Texture loads (shared memory to texture) | 3.8 | 15.7 | |
| EDRAM transfers, barriers | 1.5, 1.0 | 10.3, 4.9 | |

Copies and barriers are 37% of the frame at native and 46% at 2x. The scene's targets are resolved to memory and loaded again as textures,
the same screen-sized surfaces every frame, and on this GPU a pass over memory costs far more than on the 5080 (15 to 20 times, against 4 to
5 times for the draws).

## The resolves were waiting for memory, not moving it

The resolves took 8.2 ms of 38.9 at native (shadow mask 1.3, the HDR colour 1.25, the depth 0.87 and 0.70, the four 640x640 4x MSAA shadow
cascades 0.78, 0.70, 0.69 and 0.63) at 10 to 16 GB/s, while the texture load decode, a pass over memory of the same size, reached 50 GB/s on
this GPU; and 4 times the pixels (2x) cost 6.2 times as long. The direct resolve shaders (`rexglue-direct-resolve.patch`) loaded the samples
of a thread's 4 or 8 pixels one at a time, in loops kept rolled so that the conversions are there once, with a conditional on the format in
the loop: each load of a multisampled render target (through its FMASK and metadata) was waited for before the next one was issued, and a
GPU with 2 compute units does not have enough threads in flight to hide that. `rexglue-resolve-direct-batched-loads.patch` issues a thread's
loads together (and, in its last form, skips the second sample's conversion where it equals the first's). The numbers are in that patch's
section of `patches/README.md`; in short, on this GPU at native resolution:

| Direct3D 12, 1x, off against on | Off | On |
|---|---|---|
| Resolves per frame | 8.4 ms | 5.6 to 5.7 ms |
| Frame, free roam / driving | 39.2 / 38.2 ms | 36.3 to 36.7 / 34.9 to 35.4 ms (-6.4 to -8.5%) |
| Vulkan: resolves, frame in free roam | 7.27 ms, 35.70 ms | 5.46 ms, 33.89 ms (-5.1%) |
| 2x, Direct3D 12 (one pair): resolves, frame | 37.4 ms, 141.7 ms | 26.4 ms, 141.6 ms (the frame did not move) |
| RTX 5080, resolves at 3x, Direct3D 12 / Vulkan | 1.17 / 0.97 ms | 1.02 / 0.92 ms; the frame within noise |

- The depth copies halved (the 1280x720 ones 0.81 and 0.68 to 0.48 and 0.34 ms, the four cascades 2.77 to 1.37 ms). The two colour averaging copies
  are bound by arithmetic instead: the 7e3 conversion and the unpacking, for every sample of every pixel, are some 120 operations, 1.8 million
  sample-pixels at 2 compute units of about 0.3 trillion operations a second is 0.8 ms of the 1.2. Loading the two samples together changed
  nothing (a run in a slow state of the GPU gave the same ratios). Skipping the second sample's conversion where its raw bits equal the first's
  (exact: most pixels of a multisampled picture have the same samples) took the shadow mask from 1.22 to 1.03 ms and left the HDR colour at 1.08 to
  1.10: 0.1 to 0.2 ms of the frame, under what two runs can tell apart. It stays in, being exact by construction, and is what the bit-exactness
  check below is for.
- At 2x the resolves are 29% faster by their own timestamps but the frame did not move in the one pair that was run (141.6 against 141.7 ms): the
  draws, texture loads and post took 2 to 4 ms longer in the run with the patch. Either the stall moved to the work that follows, or one run was
  in a slightly slower hour. At 2x this GPU gives 7 FPS, where nobody plays; it is a question for a mid-range card at 2x, which was not available.
- The slow state is worth knowing about: on some hours the integrated GPU does everything 1.4 times slower (the 2x runs of 2026-10-26 that came
  out 1.5 times slow were that, and the first off run of the final measurement here), so only runs next to each other, in the order off, on,
  on, off, can be compared.
- Where the memory is made no difference (Direct3D 12, 1x, batched on): the shared memory as one committed buffer instead of the reserved, tiled one
  36.33 ms against 36.82 and 36.91 for the default; textures committed instead of in heaps 37.95 ms. Under 5% either way: nothing was done for it.
- Vulkan's texture load as a compute copy into the image (`vulkan_texture_load_storage_copy=on`; `auto` is on for NVIDIA only) took the
  texture loads from 3.5 to 3.1 ms and the frame from 33.89 to 33.41 ms with the batched loads on, 35.70 to 35.09 ms off (-1.4 to -1.7%, four
  pairs of segments in the same direction). It was not made the default for AMD: about 0.5 ms on one integrated GPU is too little for a change
  to every AMD card's Vulkan, none of which was available to try. `--vulkan_texture_load_storage_copy=on` is there for anyone who wants it.

### Checks

- **Direct3D 12 debug layer** (the final shaders; the Radeon and the RTX 5080 at 1x, the RTX at 3x): 22,561 lines, no errors, no corruption messages;
  the warnings are the known ones (initial states of buffers ignored, blend factors with MIN and MAX, live objects at exit).
- **Vulkan validation** (the Radeon and the RTX 5080 at 1x): `vkCmdEndQuery` 07007 and 01923 twice each and `ShaderOutputNotConsumed` (all of them
  in the earlier runs), and on the RTX `UNASSIGNED-Threading-MultipleThreads-Read` for the Reflex calls against the present (documented in
  `patches/README.md`, `rexglue-low-latency.patch`). Nothing about the resolve pipelines, their descriptors or barriers.
- **Bit-exactness against the EDRAM buffer path** (`direct-resolve-validate.sdk.patch`, `exe-val`: every Nth direct resolve is also done through the
  EDRAM buffer and the two results compared; the final shaders, the Radeon, batched loads on and off): **0 differing bytes in 8,150 validated
  resolves**. Direct3D 12 at 1x on a scripted drive (1,050 resolves on, 1,050 off), at 1x in a race (3,150, on) and at 2x (800, on); Vulkan at 1x
  (1,050 on, 1,050 off). Each run covered 15 to 22 kinds of resolve (fast and full copies at 8, 16, 32 and 64 bits; colour with 1, 2 and 4 samples,
  among them the 2_10_10_10 float colour the averaging copies skip the second conversion for; depth), every kind with validated resolves and none differing.
  The batched loads therefore stay on by default (`resolve_direct_batched_loads`). Not run on the RTX 5080 or on Intel.

## Options for weak GPUs

- **How often the off-screen passes are drawn** (`mcla_far_shadow_interval`, `mcla_env_map_interval`, `mcla_reflection_interval`; 0 is every frame,
  as shipped; also in the pause menu's PERFORMANCE tab). Before the main view the game draws, with the Radeon's times at native: the four sun-shadow
  cascades (1.35, 1.27, 1.43 and 2.83 ms, the last also redrawing the foliage impostors), the environment map of the car paint and the speedometer
  (1.6 and 1.5 ms), the night shadow texture (1.4 ms) and the reflection view (0.5 ms): 12.2 ms of 38.9 together with the others. A pass
  left out of a frame keeps its textures from the last time it was drawn: the render phase loop (`sub_822E6408`, `src/render_markers.cpp`) draws
  the phases set in the renderer's mask of enabled phases, which the game's own "noshadows" clears too (`mcla_shadows`), and the mask is cleared of
  the passes left out for that frame and restored after it. The options' frames are offset from each other. Far shadows (the two farthest
  cascades) lag the camera by up to the interval; a car's environment map and the reflections are slow-changing.
  Measured on the Radeon, Direct3D 12, native, MSAA kept, one run of about a minute each (the same run twice differs by up to 0.8 ms, so these are
  for ranking; mean of two runs of the unchanged game is 37.4 ms in free roam and 36.3 ms driving):

  | Option | Free roam, ms | Driving, ms | Saving of the frame |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | As shipped (twice) | 37.84, 37.02 | 36.48, 36.16 | |
  | Anti-aliasing off | 34.97 | 34.06 | 2.4 ms, 6 to 7% |
  | Far shadows every 2nd frame | 36.29 | 36.02 | 0.3 to 1.1 ms |
  | Far shadows every 4th frame | 36.05 | 34.78 | 1.4 to 1.5 ms |
  | Environment map every 2nd frame | 36.37 | 35.35 | 1.0 ms |
  | Environment map every 4th frame | 36.23 | 35.45 | 0.9 to 1.2 ms |
  | Reflection every 2nd frame | 37.12 | 35.98 | 0.3 ms |
  | Full-screen blur off | 36.97 | 35.72 | 0.5 ms |
  | Far 2nd, map 4th, reflection 2nd, blur off | 35.43 | 34.59 | 1.7 to 2.0 ms (5%) |

  The savings are about half of what the phases' times say (a pass left out also leaves its resolve and the texture load that follows in the
  frames that draw it, and the frame is not all GPU). Pictures of the same scene, as shipped and with the preset (`rg2`, the parked car at the spawn
  after 120 frames): the two are alike, down to the shadows under the buildings; the cars of the traffic differ, as they do between any two runs. Driving
  shots with the passes left out (a moving camera: no two runs can be the same) show nothing wrong. Whether the far shadows swim or the
  car's environment map lags in motion was not looked at beyond that.
  The same look at the final preset (`rg4`: title, map, free roam after 120 frames, driving, and two frames with the game frozen, as shipped and with
  `--mcla_preset=low`, each in one run): the parked car at the spawn has stair-stepped edges where the anti-aliasing was (the fence's bars, the
  car's roofline), which is what turning it off costs, and nothing else differs but what moves between two runs (traffic, the radio ticker). The time
  of day does: the game starts at a different time of day from one run to the next (pink dusk in one, orange sunset in another; the earlier
  batches' shots of the same API, GPU and scale show both skies too), so the light and the lengths of the shadows of the two runs cannot be compared. Within each run the
  driving and the frozen shots have shadows that fit their sun, the two frozen frames are alike, and the car's paint looks as it did. Far shadows
  and the environment map drawn at intervals were not seen to swim or lag in these stills (a still shows nothing of motion).
- **A preset** (`mcla_preset` = `low`): anti-aliasing off (6 to 7% of the frame at native, 9% in `m1`, 26 to 29% at 2x: `m2a`, `gt1b`), the
  full-screen blur off (0.5 ms), the far shadows every 2nd frame and the environment map every 4th (about 1 ms each at native). The reflection
  view is not in it: left out every 2nd frame it saved 0.3 ms (0.8%), for reflections that lag. Far shadows every 4th frame saved about as much as every 2nd within
  the noise, and lag twice as long, so it is 2. The options it stands for take its values as their defaults, so one set by hand wins. The
  launcher has it as *Graphics preset*. Measured as a whole, the final one against the game as shipped (`rg3`: Radeon, Direct3D 12, native, the batched
  loads on, two runs of about a minute each in the order shipped, preset, preset with the anti-aliasing kept by hand, the same again in reverse; the
  two runs of a kind differ by 0.23 ms at most):

  | | Free roam, ms | Driving, ms | Saving |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | As shipped | 36.78, 36.77 | 35.80, 35.74 | |
  | The preset (anti-aliasing off) | 32.19, 32.42 | 31.63, 31.77 | 4.5 ms (12.2%) in free roam, 4.1 ms (11.4%) driving |
  | The preset, anti-aliasing kept by hand | 35.21, 35.18 | 34.17, 34.17 | 1.6 ms (4.3%) in free roam, 1.6 ms (4.5%) driving |

  So the shadows, the map and the blur are 4%, and leaving the anti-aliasing off another 2.5 to 2.9 ms (7 to 8%). The 1% low of free roam went from
  25.2 to 27.9 FPS (mean of the two runs of each).
- **Automatic graphics API** (`src/mcla_app.cpp`, the launcher's `ShaderStamp.cs` repeats the rule): Vulkan on AMD's integrated GPUs (told by
  Direct3D 12's architecture query, UMA: an APU shares the CPU's memory, however much of it the BIOS sets aside for the GPU, which a size could not
  tell) and on Intel graphics; Direct3D 12 on AMD's discrete cards, on NVIDIA, and when nothing is known. Evidence: the Radeon above, Vulkan 6 to 10%
  faster at native (35.7 against 39.5 ms in free roam, three runs each; GPU time 36.4 against 38.9 ms; with the batched loads 33.9 against 36.3) and 2.6%
  at 2x; against fork issue #7 (a Radeon RX 6600 XT with an i5-10400: Direct3D 12 20 to 25% faster than Vulkan, whose GPU command thread costs more CPU);
  the RTX 5080: no gain from Vulkan in races. Intel graphics take the SDK's slower rasterizer-ordered-view path on Direct3D 12 and are not
  measured (no Intel GPU was available): the choice is documented, not tested. The GPU is the one Direct3D 12 would take (`d3d12_adapter`, else the first
  that is not software); a software adapter (Windows' WARP, by its number or `d3d12_adapter=-2`) is Direct3D 12's, the only API that can use it, so Automatic and
  the recommendation are Direct3D 12 for it in the game and in the launcher alike, and a number that names no adapter gets no recommendation. Vulkan needs `vulkan-1.dll` and a Vulkan device that is that GPU (matched by LUID, else by vendor and device id; the game
  then sets `vulkan_device` to it unless the player has): the one test run of the first version, with `--d3d12_adapter=1` and the API on Automatic,
  logged "Vulkan (Automatic on AMD graphics)" and ran on the RTX 5080 (6.8 ms a frame), because the SDK takes Vulkan's first device. Choosing the
  API in the launcher or the pause menu, or `--mcla_gpu_backend`, skips the decision. The launcher and the pause menu mark the API recommended for the GPU with
  "(recommended)"; the launcher also treats a shader cache made for the other API than Automatic now gives as out of date.

## What was not done, and why

- **Resolving straight into the host texture that is sampled next** (the resolve's write to shared memory and the texture load that reads it
  back are 9 to 10 ms of 39 at native). The texture's identity (address, size, pitch, format) is only known at the draw that samples it, after
  the resolve; the resolve would have to write the host texture speculatively as well as the memory (so as to stay exact: the guest can read the
  same memory as another format), in 7e3, float and depth formats that each have their own conversion in the load shaders, and without the
  memory write the texture would need a write-back when anything else reads the range. What it could save is the texture load, 3.8 ms at
  native and about 1.5 ms of that is the decode a dual-write resolve would still have to do; a quarter of the frame is not within reach for
  the risk.
- **Rendering the off-screen passes at a lower scale than the screen** (at 2x the shadow cascades, the environment map and the reflection
  view are 4x the pixels: 46 of 179 ms). The resolution scale is global in the render target cache, the resolve shaders and the texture
  cache (addresses in the scaled resolve buffer are the guest's times the scale), and the cascades are 640x640 pixels of 4x MSAA depth whose
  four samples the resolve turns into a 1280x1280 atlas: a per-render-target scale means a second set of addresses for everything. The
  options above (drawing those passes less often) are the cheap way to the same end.
- **Texture loads without the copy for unscaled resolved textures** (`texture_load_direct` only covers resolution-scaled ones): 2.2 ms of the
  3.8 ms at native on Direct3D 12. With Vulkan the default on AMD's integrated and Intel graphics, only the Direct3D 12 path of NVIDIA and AMD's
  cards would gain, and that is where the copy costs least.
- **Per-draw costs** (4,000 draws a frame, 93% under 10 microseconds): the draws that cost the Radeon time are the ones that shade many pixels;
  the rest is a floor of about 1.7 microseconds a draw that state deduplication in the SDK already works on.
