# Menu lag: answering a press at once, and a rate for the menus (2026-10-29)

The owner's report (4K / 3x, a 120 FPS cap, a controller, the build with the menu key repeat fix): "Navigation is working correctly now", but the menus are "still sluggish", which they put down to the update rate being limited for the menus. `KNOWN-ISSUES.md` has the summary; this is the detail. "Read in the code" is from the recompiled game, not from a run. "Measured" has the runs of 2026-10-29 (see the end).

## Read in the code

What the player sees changes only when the screen's Flash movie advances. Since `mcla_ui_timing_fix` (2026-10-14) each UI screen advances on a 30 a second schedule of its own (`mcla_ui_movie_advance`, the hook at `0x825EDC6C` in the advance `sub_825EDC08`). The game's own handling of a press is not delayed: the widget reacts in the update the key event is delivered in (the option row `sub_826316E0` changes its index at once, a list moves its selected row). So at 120 FPS:

- A press waits for the movie's next slot: 0 to 33.3 ms, 16.7 ms on average (computed from the schedule).
- Then the highlight and the page changes play at 30 movie frames a second, which is the console's pace, and every animation takes as long as on the console.

The order inside one update of the UI manager (`sub_821FC588`), from its listing:

| Step | Where | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `sub_8221DEA0` | the pad into the 24 inputs |
| 2 | `sub_82224920` -> `sub_82223CD8` | the UI input object: key down on the first update a button is seen pressed (`0x822240C0`), a repeat while it is held (`0x822241D0`); the widgets react here |
| 3 | `sub_822247A8`, `sub_8221EAE8`, the objects at `+96` | the input object's per-update work, other updates |
| 4 | the loop over the movie map (buckets at `+60`) | for each movie that is enabled (`+68`) and not hidden (`+69`): `sub_82720898` -> the player context's `vtable[1]` -> the advance `sub_825EDC08` (our hook) |

So a key event of an update is known to the movie hook of the same update, before any movie steps. That is what `mcla_ui_advance_on_input` uses; no new hook was needed: the two key hooks of `mcla_ui_repeat_fix` also note the event (a counter and a time), and the movie hook looks at it.

## The change

`src/frame_timing.cpp`:

- `mcla_ui_advance_on_input` (**on by default since v0.3.1**; it was off until the chain below had run). A UI screen (a movie of the UI manager's map except `HUDMAPMOVIE`) that sees a key event it has not answered, younger than 100 ms, and is not due yet (more than a quarter of its period before its next slot), advances at once, and its schedule restarts from that advance (next slot = now + period). If it was due anyway nothing changes. So per key event and screen at most one extra advance, and none within 100 ms of the screen's last extra advance (`kUiInputGapUs`; with `mcla_ui_repeat_fix` off a held direction repeats every 50 ms at 120 FPS, which this keeps from becoming 20 extra advances a second). At 30 FPS and below every update is a slot and nothing changes.
- Which screens: all of them, not only the focused one. The key goes to a widget tree (`sub_82209C38` -> the widgets' `vtable[32]`) that writes into the movie its widget belongs to: the pause menu's list and the pages this fork adds (they borrow its list) into `PAUSEMOVIE`, a pop-up into `POPUPMOVIE`, the button prompts into `PROMPTMOVIE`, a screen change into `TRANSITIONMOVIE` (names found in the image, not all seen in a run). The code has no cheap way to tell which one owns the focus, and only enabled screens are stepped at all. An advance on a screen that did not change costs half a movie frame of its own ambient animation on average. The HUD (`HUDMAPMOVIE` and the gauge movie) is left out: it has its own rate (`mcla_ui_hud_rate`) and no menu widgets.
- Held direction: with `mcla_ui_repeat_fix` a held direction repeats every 200 ms, six 33.3 ms slots. The key event of the next repeat lands 191.7 to 200 ms after the last (at 120 FPS, updates 8.3 ms apart), which is inside the quarter period of the slot at 200 ms, so the screen is "due anyway" and no extra advance is made: in a steady hold about 30 advances a second, the bound is 5 extra a second (17% fast). Computed; the chain measures the 1 s hold.
- `mcla_ui_menu_rate` (default 30; 60, 120, 0 = every frame). The screens' period. This is the other half of "sluggish": the animations. At 60 or 120 a page change or a pop-up arrives in half or a quarter of the time and every menu animation runs 2 or 4 times as fast; 0 is as the menus ran before the timing fix (the pause menu opened in 183 ms at 120 FPS, 883 ms with 30). In the pause menu as DISPLAY › MENU UPDATES (`menu-rate-row-2026-10-29.md`), not in the launcher.
- `mcla_ui_advance_log` (development): `ui adv: <NAME> ctx=<hex> t=<ms>.<us> ms in=<0|1>` for every advance of a UI screen (`in=1`: the extra advance). `t` is on the clock of the `ui key:` lines.

## The check

`launcher/dev/menu_input.py`, set `lag` (pad) and `lagreport` (report). One game run, native resolution, D3D12, `--mcla_ui_input_log=true --mcla_ui_advance_log=true`, the timing fix and the repeat fix on in every cell, the options changed with `set` during the run. Cells (cap; advance on input; menu rate): 120 off 30 (A), 120 on 30 (B), uncapped A, uncapped B, 60 A, 60 B, 120 on 60 (R), 120 on 0 (Z). In each cell:

| Test | What | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| IDLE | 3 s on the pause list, nothing pressed | advances a second by themselves (expect 30 with a rate of 30) |
| T0, T1 | taps of down and up, 450 ms apart, in the pause list and in PERFORMANCE | the lag of each tap; advances a second with taps (T1 and T1b) |
| T2 | down held 1 s | the lag of the 5 key events; advances in that second, and how many were extra |
| T3, T4 | right/left on a three-value row, A on a checkbox | lag, and the key-event check |

The lag of a key event is the time from its `ui key:` line to the next `ui adv:` line of `PAUSEMOVIE` (the pause menu and its pages); a gap over 100 ms counts as missed. The report gives median, 90th percentile and maximum, how many were answered by an extra advance, and the same advances a second for every screen the log shows. The key-event check of the existing menu input pad (every tap exactly one key event, a 1 s hold 5) runs in every cell and is the regression check for the cells with the option on.

The pause menu's opening, last in the script: Start closes the pause menu, 3 s, then Start opens it and `down` is pressed every 50 ms (30 ms long) until the list's selected row is 1; the time from the marker before the Start to the answer. Four openings (A, B, A, B) at a 120 cap. It is quantised to 50 ms and includes the 100 ms of the Start press, so it is not the 883 ms of `KNOWN-ISSUES.md` (a frame-by-frame picture count at a fixed time step) but is the same measure in A and B. It rests on the pad closing the menu with Start, which was not tried; a failure there ends the script after all the cells.

The pause menu's opening (Start to the first `down` the list took; 50 ms steps, 100 ms of the press included; a 120 FPS cap): change off 286 and 284 ms, on 285 and 286 ms.

What it shows (measured):
- At a 120 FPS cap and uncapped the wait for a press fell from 8.5 and 16.9 ms to 0.2 ms, the longest from 25 and 30 ms to 4 and 12 ms. At a 60 FPS cap the median was 0.3 ms with the change off too (about half of the taps landed on a slot, inferred from the spread); the 90th percentile fell from 16.8 to 0.3 ms.
- What it costs: nothing changes with nothing pressed (30.0 advances a second in every cell with a rate of 30). With a tap every 450 ms the screen advanced 0.4 to 1.2 times a second more (25 to 34 of the 48 presses got an extra advance), and in the 1 s hold 30 times with the change off and 30, 31 and 34 times on (the chain's limit was 36). The opening took the same time.
- The menu rate does what it says: 60.0 and 120.0 advances a second at a 120 FPS cap, and a press is answered in 0.2 ms at both.
- Not explained: the longest waits of 11 to 12 ms with the change on, uncapped, at the 60 FPS cap and at a menu rate of 60 (none of the 48 key events was missed, that is, waited over 100 ms; the median is untouched; not looked into).

What the reading predicted (before the run): a median of about 16 ms and a longest of about 33 ms with the change off at 120 FPS, under a few ms with it on, idle 30 a second, the hold 30 to 35, the opening the same. All held except the change-off median at 120 FPS, 8.5 ms instead of 16. A probable reason (inferred, not tested): the taps are 450 ms apart, 13.5 slots of 33.3 ms, so they fall on only two phases of the slot, 0.5 slot apart (the 90th percentile of 25 ms fits); the uncapped run, whose frame time does not divide 33.3 ms, gave 16.9.
