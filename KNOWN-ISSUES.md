# Known issues of this fork's changes

Upstream (holdmysocks/mcla-recomp) no longer publishes its list of known issues and findings; its README is its only documentation. This file covers only the changes this fork makes (`FORK.md`): what each one fixes, how it was checked, and what is not verified. The SDK patches are described in `patches/README.md`, and the measurements behind them in `launcher/dev/`. Everything here was built and measured on Windows only (`FORK.md`, "Testing so far").

## Frame pacing: the limiter waits at the swap (2026-10-14)

Upstream's notes on its frame limiter (2026-10-03) left frame-to-frame jitter of about plus or minus 4 ms at 30 as an open point, with a possible cause: "the limiter waits inside the timer update, not at present". That was the cause of uneven frames at a cap. The step was measured before the wait and the frame reached the screen after a variable amount of work, so at a 60 cap frames alternated around 12 and 22 ms and moving objects shook. `mcla_pace_at_swap` (default on) moves the wait to the swap (`mcla_present_interval`): frames are held to the target where they are presented, as vsync does but with a precise clock, and each step measures one presented interval. `--mcla_pace_at_swap=false` restores the old wait.

Windows, D3D12, RTX 5080 and Ryzen 7 9800X3D: on the title screen at 60 the frame-time standard deviation went from 3.1 ms to 0.38 ms. PresentMon at 45, 50 and 60 caps: median exactly on target, about 98% of presents within 1 ms of it, no dropped frames. In play the user reported it smooth at 60 and 120.

Measured with `--vsync=false` on the command line. Another open point in those notes was whether `vsync=false` set from the app reaches the GPU plugin. It does (rechecked on 2026-10-16, free roam, native, D3D12, 120 FPS cap):
- **At start:** with neither `--vsync` on the command line nor a `vsync` line in `mcla.toml`, `ConfigureFrameTiming` turns it off and the cap holds (8.33 ms median).
- **While running:** set from the game (as the pause menu's VSYNC does), `vsync=true` held the game to 60 (15.8 ms median), and `false` gave 120 back.

With vsync on, the GPU plugin's emulated 60 Hz vblank caps the game at 60 and paces it unevenly, so it stays off unless the player turns it on. An earlier note here said that a setting from the app never reached the plugin; that was not reproduced.

## Timer resolution kept while the window is hidden (2026-10-14)

Windows 11 stops honouring `timeBeginPeriod(1)` for a process none of whose windows can be seen: minimized, fully covered, or with the display asleep. The timer then runs at 15.6 ms, every limiter sleep overshoots, and frames come out at 16 ms or more whatever the target. `MclaApp::ConfigureFrameTiming` now opts out with `SetProcessInformation(ProcessPowerThrottling, PROCESS_POWER_THROTTLING_IGNORE_TIMER_RESOLUTION)` before raising the resolution and logs whether that worked. Checked with `NtQueryTimerResolution` while the display was asleep: 15.625 ms before, 1 ms after.

## Achievement pop-ups made the presenter repaint at the monitor's rate (fixed 2026-10-14)

Overlays showed 240 FPS on a 240 Hz display while the game made 30 to 60 frames. The SDK registers its achievement toast as an ImGui dialog for the whole session, and while any dialog is registered the ImGui drawer asks for another paint after each one, so the presenter showed every game frame several times. Besides wrong overlay numbers, the extra presents went to the queue the GPU command thread submits to and spoiled frame pacing. On upstream (54a5569) with D3D12 at native resolution they alone held the game at exactly 60 FPS uncapped, every frame 16.7 ms apart; with only the wrapper below added, free roam ran at 92 FPS (2026-10-17, measured for the first upstream PR). (Upstream's README reports the Linux build presenting about 1,000 times a second on Wayland unless MAILBOX and IMMEDIATE presenting are turned off, which fits the same cause.)

`src/achievement_toast.h` wraps the SDK's toast and registers it only while an achievement is on screen, from RecompiledHaloReach by way of ForzaHorizon1-ReImagined (both MIT, by Dyotson; the notices are in the file). With `patches/rexglue-imgui-dialog-repaint-on-demand.patch` the toast is also drawn with the game's own frames instead of asking for repaints while it shows. Windows, D3D12, 60 cap: presents equal game frames before, during and after a toast (60 a second; 240 during a toast without the SDK patch), and 99.4 to 100% of frames within 1 ms of the target while one shows. The unlock itself is saved before the toast appears (`XGIUserWriteAchievements`, `Documents\mcla\achievements\545407F8.toml`).

## Motorcycle lean above 30 FPS (2026-10-14)

`mcBikeGyro::Update` (sub_823688B8; identified by LARecomp from the game's RTTI, its pull request #9) eases a smoothed position and velocity toward the bike's by a fixed fraction per frame: 0.40 and 0.15 when the rounded frame rate is 60 or more, doubled below that. That matches the console only at 30. At 120 the position settles about 1.3 times and the velocity 1.8 times as fast, at 45 both 1.5 times, and at a 60 cap frames that round to 59 switch to a filter two to three times as stiff. It was the one frame-rate switch in LARecomp's list with no correction here.

Two mid-asm hooks (`mcla_bike_gyro_pos_smoothing` at 0x8236895C, `mcla_bike_gyro_vel_smoothing` at 0x823689F0, where both ways through the game's test meet) replace each factor with the console's 30 FPS fraction applied by elapsed time, `1 - (1 - k30)^(dt * 30)`, as upstream's camera and chassis filters do (`mcla_camera_pos_smoothing` and the others in `src/frame_timing.cpp`, from LARecomp). Written for this project from those facts, not copied (LARecomp has no licence). `--mcla_bike_gyro_fix=false` turns it off.

Checked: the hook sites in the generated code, the constants read at run time (2.0, 0.40, 0.15), and with a temporary probe at live 30, 60, 120 and uncapped frame rates, exactly 0.2 (position) and 0.7 (velocity) remaining per 1/30 s in every case. **Not verified by riding**: the function runs only with a motorcycle in play.

## Attract movie: other shots' colours over the picture with hot pages (fixed 2026-10-14)

Reported by the user from a test run: towards the end of the attract movie (the one that plays after idling on the title screen), a flashing pinkish outline over the picture. Window captures of the whole movie, compared with `attract720.bik` decoded by FFmpeg, located it: in runs with hot pages on (`--shared_memory_hot_page_faults=4`, the PS5's setting, tried on PC while profiling) the brightness of each frame was right but its colour came from earlier shots, so red and blue blobs and pink outlines of other shots' shapes lay over the current one (a green motorcycle turned grey with red circles beside it). Without hot pages, with every other setting of this fork on, all captures matched the movie. The flash-white frames with a pinkish smudge beside the rider at the very end, and the venetian-blind wipe a few seconds before, are in the movie itself.

Cause: a texture loaded from a hot page was never reloaded while the page stayed hot, as no write fault told the texture cache it had changed (details in `patches/README.md`, `rexglue-hot-pages-watched-ranges.patch`, which fixes it). Bink writes the movie's planes into textures every frame, so the colour planes stuck on old shots. Hot pages are off by default on PC; the PS5, which runs with them, should have had the same fault.

## Mouse pointer over the game during play (2026-10-14)

Playing with a controller, the mouse pointer stayed in the middle of the picture until it was moved off the screen. The game now hides it the way PC games do. It goes as soon as a controller is used, or once the mouse has been still for about 3 seconds, and comes back as soon as the mouse moves or is clicked. It is hidden only over the game's picture while the game has focus. With another window in front, over the title bar or borders in windowed mode, or while the SDK's console, settings or achievements overlay is open, it stays. `mcla_hide_cursor` (default on; `--mcla_hide_cursor=false` keeps the pointer) selects the SDK's auto-hidden cursor, which `patches/rexglue-cursor-auto-hide.patch` makes focus-aware and lets the pads hide (details and checks there). Verified by the user with a controller on 2026-10-16: the pointer goes as soon as the controller is used. The pause menu's CONTROLS › HIDE MOUSE POINTER switches it during play.

## Vulkan on Windows (2026-10-14)

`--mcla_gpu_backend=vulkan` (or the launcher's *Graphics API*) runs everything of this fork on Vulkan: the frame pacing, the toast and cursor changes and every SDK patch that applies to Vulkan were checked there, and the launcher's settings help on Vulkan at least as much as on D3D12 (`clear_memory_page_state=false` alone took 46% off the frame time per draw at native). Two SDK changes came out of it: the Vulkan backend now counts draws and the other per-frame counters (`rexglue-perf-counters-batched.patch`), and its draws no longer look a setting up in the cvar registry each (`rexglue-vulkan-memexport-check-order.patch`, 6% less GPU command thread time per draw). The hot-page fix for the attract movie works the same on Vulkan, where the PS5 needs it.

Two more Vulkan changes followed (2026-10-15), measured in the city with the scripted controller: shared memory uploads go before the render pass instead of ending it (`rexglue-vulkan-hoist-shared-memory-uploads.patch`; with hot pages every draw's upload had ended the render pass, about 800 times a frame; free roam 19 to 27% faster), and command buffers are recorded and submitted on their own thread as on D3D12 (`rexglue-threaded-submission-vulkan.patch`). On this machine (RTX 5080) Vulkan then matched D3D12 at native resolution and was 7 to 11% slower at 3x, where its texture loads took about three times as long on the GPU (fixed since, see below); it stuttered less than D3D12 in the map zoom-out after the title until D3D12's textures were placed in shared heaps (next sections). Validation (`--vulkan_validation_enabled=true`) reports three kinds of message, all present with every change of this fork off: a pixel shader output with no attachment (warning), an occlusion query begun outside a render pass and ended inside one, and a swapchain destroyed while in use on fullscreen switches. Measurements and method: `launcher/dev/vulkan-2026-10-14.md`. **Not verified**: races and the other places that need a player.

Vulkan on Vega graphics (reported 2026-10-27, fork issue #14): on a Ryzen 7 5700G (Radeon Vega 8) the HUD and the menus and some parts of the city were missing with *Graphics API* set to Vulkan, which Direct3D 12 does not do. The cause is not known (no Vega GPU was available here, and the report came without a log). So the *Automatic* graphics API takes Vulkan only on AMD's integrated graphics of the RDNA generation (the Radeon of a Ryzen 7000 or 9000 desktop processor, tried here on a 9800X3D) and on Intel graphics, and Direct3D 12 on everything else, the Vega APUs (Ryzen 3000G to 5000G) included. The game and the launcher tell RDNA from Vega by the smallest wave Direct3D 12 reports for the GPU (`WaveLaneCountMin`: 32 on RDNA, 64 on GCN and Vega) and agree on it (`src/automatic_api.h`; the launcher marks the recommended API); choosing Vulkan by hand still works as before. The rule is checked with made-up values (`launcher/dev/api-rule`) and on this PC's adapters; it has not been run on Vega, on a discrete AMD card or on Intel graphics.

Not fixed: in a window in SDR, Vulkan's presents are composed by DWM (PresentMon: "Composed: Copy with GPU GDI", 2026-10-17), which adds a copy and the compositor in the way (its latency not measured); in a window D3D12, and Vulkan in HDR, get independent flip, and in borderless fullscreen Vulkan in SDR gets flip presentation too (Hardware: Legacy Flip).

## Loading-screen pacing: uncapped stutter and the fast intro (fixed 2026-10-14)

Two faults in `PaceUnlimitedSwap` (`src/frame_timing.cpp`), the hold that keeps loops which never reach the engine timer (the start-up legal screens and logos, loading screens) at the console's 30 per second:

- Uncapped with the intro at `normal`, about a quarter of all frames took 33 ms. The hold took any swap with no pass through the frame limiter since the previous swap for such a loop. But the game updates and presents on different threads, and uncapped the two drift against each other: in normal play about one swap in forty has no update before it and the next one has two (counted per swap with the dev pad's frame log: zero-update swaps came only singly, never two in a row, in the title, the map zoom-out and free roam). Holding each of those to 33 ms also delayed the presenting thread, which made the pattern about ten times as frequent. Free roam, native, D3D12, uncapped: median 4.6 ms but mean 81 FPS and 26.7% of frames over 30 ms before; median 6.8 ms, mean 145 FPS and no frame over 30 ms after. The map zoom-out went from a mean of 66 to 132 FPS (frames over 30 ms 34.8% to 0.7%). A swap is now held only when the one before it had no update either, which a real untimed loop always shows.
- `mcla_intro=fast` (and `skip`) turned the hold off for good, so every later loading screen also ran as fast as the PC could present. They now leave it off only until the engine timer first runs, which is the end of the start-up logos.

Unchanged: the start-up sequence at `normal` (334 swaps over 11.3 s at 29.4 per second, against 327 over 11.1 s before), at `fast` (879 swaps in 3.7 s, against 893 in 3.8 s), and capped play, where every swap has exactly one update (120 cap, free roam: 1,200 of 1,200 swaps). In the reachable path (start-up, title, attract movie, map zoom-out, free roam) the start-up sequence is the only untimed loop, so the fast intro's new limit was checked by reading the code, not on a later loading screen.

## Menus and HUD animations ran faster above 30 FPS (menus fixed 2026-10-14, the HUD at a rate of its own since 2026-10-16)

Reported by the user at a steady 120 cap: the song title scrolling under the speedometer ran far too fast while the game itself ran at normal speed. Every UI screen is a Flash movie (menus, pop-ups, the HUD with its song ticker and "LOADING" badge), and the UI manager steps each one with the Flash player's one-frame mode, which advances exactly one movie frame per game update and ignores the elapsed time (from LARecomp's notes, used with its author's permission: mcUIManager::Update, sub_821FC588, passes byte_827DC1FC = 1, which nothing writes, to the advance, sub_825EDC08). The console updated 30 times a second, so at 120 FPS every Flash animation ran four times as fast.

`mcla_ui_movie_advance` (a mid-asm hook at 0x825EDC6C in the advance, `src/frame_timing.cpp`) now gives each of the UI manager's screens (menus, the pause menu, pop-ups, prompts, the GPS map's panels; found by name in its movie map) its own 30-per-second schedule; an update that comes before a movie is due skips that movie's advance, through the same exit the player takes when a movie is not due. A movie due within a quarter of a frame is advanced early, so at 30 FPS every update still advances once. A movie that falls behind (below 30 FPS) is not caught up, as on the console. The start-up logos are left out, so the fast intro still runs them as fast as possible. `--mcla_ui_timing_fix=false` restores one movie frame per update.

Measured with the dev pad's frame-by-frame screenshots, timedemo at each cap (`--mcla_fixed_dt` = the cap), native, D3D12:

| | 30 FPS | 120 FPS before | 120 FPS after |
|---|---|---|---|
| Song ticker under the speedometer (px/s at 720p), with the HUD paced at 30 | 59.9 | 239.9 | 60.0 |
| Pause menu opening (Start in the city) | 933 ms | 183 ms | 883 ms |

The 30 FPS figures are the same with the fix on and off (59.5 to 59.9 px/s). The car's path over the scripted drive from the spawn point differed between runs with the fix on and off no more than between runs with the same setting (up to 13 m apart either way; the closest pair, 0.4 m, was one of each), and the drive's frame time was unchanged. Short presses still reach the menus: the GPS map's legend toggled on each of eight 15 ms presses of X at 120 FPS with the fix on, as with it off.

What else this covers and what it does not:

- Changed with it (the same mechanism, not measured separately): pop-ups and notifications, the GPS map's panels, the title screen's prompts.
- Not affected: the minimap, which the game moves itself (it changed on every new frame with the fix on, as off), and the GPS map's zoom-out, which is a camera move.
- Not affected: the 3D icons over the world (`3DICONSMOVIE0`-`2`), which the game advances by elapsed time already.

The in-game HUD has a rate of its own, `mcla_ui_hud_rate` (default 60 since 2026-10-16: the user asked for the console's 30 first, then played at 60 and chose it). It is two movies: HUDMAPMOVIE and the gauge movie, which the HUD code creates itself, outside the UI manager's screens. The game sets the gauges' values every update (`GearValue`, `Nitrovalue`, `map_rotation` and so on, seen by logging the player's variable lookups) and the movies' own scripts draw them as they advance, in the same frame step (`sub_825FB0D8` on the root sprite, one call for timeline and scripts) that scrolls the song ticker. Each of the two movies is needed for both: with either one paced alone, the ticker ran at 60 px/s and the needle stepped. Splitting the timeline step from the scripts would mean changing the Flash player's frame step, so the needles move exactly as often as the ticker steps. The rate picks the balance: 60 a second, the default (smoother needles, the ticker twice its original speed), 30 as on the console (the ticker at its original speed, the needles in 30 Hz steps), 120 (the ticker four times as fast), or 0 for every frame, as with no pacing. The HUD's movies get their own schedule at that rate, the same way as the screens' at 30; the value is read at every advance, so a change takes effect at once. It is the launcher's *HUD updates* and DISPLAY › HUD UPDATES in the pause menu. Until 2026-10-16 the HUD was paced only with `mcla_ui_timing_hud=true`, off by default, so the default was every frame.

At a 120 FPS cap, timedemo (`--mcla_fixed_dt=120`), native, D3D12, measured on 2026-10-16 as the title scrolls in after arriving in the city, and with the car accelerating:

| `mcla_ui_hud_rate` | Song ticker | Tachometer needle |
|---|---|---|
| 30 | 59.2 px/s, as at 30 FPS | moved in 30 of 119 frame pairs |
| 60 (default) | 118.2 px/s | moved in 50 of 116 frame pairs |
| 120 | 237.2 px/s | moved in 75 of 117 frame pairs |
| 0 (every frame) | 238.7 px/s | moved in 74 of 115 frame pairs |

The ticker figures are a fit of its position over 2.5 s, shots every 2 frames; 60, 120 and 240 px/s are what 30, 60 and 120 steps of 2 px a second give. The needle was found by its colour in a shot every frame for 1 s of acceleration; it rests for part of that second at every rate, so it moves in fewer pairs than the rate allows. Before the HUD had a rate of its own, paced and unpaced gave 60.1 and 239.9 px/s, and 28 of 119 and 69 of 116 pairs.

The menus stay paced either way: the pause menu opened in 883 ms at 120 FPS with the default settings. LARecomp left its HUD out of the same kind of pacing for the same reason. The "LOADING" badge belongs to the HUD and follows its rate.

## D3D12 stutter in the map zoom-out and in races (fixed 2026-10-15)

On D3D12, the map zoom-out after the title had frames of 150 to 330 ms where Vulkan had 30 to 45 ms (1% low 9 against 31 FPS at 3x, 19 against 48 at native, real time), and races had frames of 60 to 140 ms. Every new texture was a committed resource, an allocation of its own that took about 0.19 ms to create and 0.08 ms to release. The zoom-out streams in 750 to 900 textures in a single frame when the city appears, and at 3x the texture cache goes over its memory limit and evicts 1,400 to 1,500 at once; races stream textures in as the car moves. Vulkan sub-allocates its textures from large blocks. `patches/rexglue-d3d12-placed-texture-heaps.patch` places D3D12's textures in shared 256 MB heaps the same way (setting `d3d12_texture_heaps`, default on). Map zoom-out, real time, 1% low: 54 FPS at native and 27 FPS at 3x (Vulkan 48 and 31), worst frame 44 and 47 ms. Race, 1% low: 50 to 96 FPS at native and 41 to 61 FPS at 3x, worst frame 102 to 13 ms and 61 to 20 ms. Median frames and 60 FPS pacing are unchanged. One frame of about 55 ms remains in the zoom-out on both APIs, about 3 s in; it shows no GPU work, so it is the game's own. Measurements and method: `launcher/dev/d3d12-zoomout-2026-10-15.md`; details in `patches/README.md`. **Not verified**: other GPUs; the heaps hold video memory in 256 MB steps, which matters most on cards with little of it.

## Vulkan texture loads slower than D3D12 at 3x (fixed 2026-10-15)

At 3x, Vulkan's texture loads took 4.4 ms of each GPU frame, against 1.6 ms on D3D12. GPU timestamps around each step of the loads put almost all of it in one place: the copy of a decoded texture into its image (`vkCmdCopyBufferToImage`) ran at about 120 GB/s on NVIDIA's Windows driver, against 400 to 870 GB/s for D3D12's copy. The textures loaded every frame are render targets the game resolves into textures, up to 66 MB each at 3x.

`patches/rexglue-vulkan-texture-load-storage-copy.patch` writes the single-level ones with a compute shader through an integer view of the image instead, bit for bit the same (setting `vulkan_texture_load_storage_copy`, default `auto`: on with NVIDIA only). In the scripted city run at 3x, frames got shorter:
- free roam: 14.9 to 12.0 ms (-19%);
- the drive: 15.1 to 12.2 ms (-19%);
- the map zoom-out: 13.1 to 11.4 ms (-13%).

1% lows rose from 59-60 to 71 FPS. At 3x Vulkan was then 10 to 14% faster than D3D12 (with D3D12's placed texture heaps). That no longer holds: since direct resolves and single-tile rendering, the two are within about 3% at 3x in the city and D3D12 is 13 to 15% faster in the race (2026-10-16, `launcher/dev/single-tile-edram-2026-10-16.md`); at native the two are the same.

Validation shows nothing new; screenshots and the attract movie look the same. Measurements and method: `launcher/dev/vulkan-2026-10-14.md`; details in `patches/README.md`.

**Not verified**: other GPUs and drivers, which is why it is on with NVIDIA only by default, and the bike.

## Clears drawn at 4x MSAA took a quarter of the GPU frame at 3x (fixed 2026-10-15)

The game clears its render targets the Direct3D 9 way, by drawing a rectangle over them, mostly with 4x MSAA over targets drawn with 2x (the main scene, in two tiles) or 1x (the HUD's depth). The emulator's host render targets can't change MSAA mode in place. So each such clear copied the whole range into a 4x MSAA target, drew there, and copied it all back for the next draw. At 3x that was 3.3 to 3.6 ms of a 13 to 14 ms GPU frame on D3D12, and 2.6 to 2.7 ms on Vulkan.

`patches/rexglue-clear-rectangle-draws.patch` does these draws as clears of the host render targets, like a resolve's clear (setting `clear_rectangle_draws`, default on, both APIs). It clears in the MSAA mode that the range was drawn with next after the same clear last time, so the scene's tiles are cleared in place with no copies. Timedemo at 3x, median frame:
- **D3D12:** standing in the city 14.0 to 10.7 ms (-24%), driving 13.8 to 10.3 ms (-26%), the map zoom-out 12.7 to 8.7 ms (-31%), the race 12.9 to 10.5 ms (-18%).
- **Vulkan:** 12.1 to 9.8 ms (-19%), 12.3 to 9.8 ms (-20%), 11.3 to 7.7 ms (-32%), 13.6 to 11.9 ms (-13%).
- **1% lows** rose with them, 59-61 to 74 FPS on D3D12 and 69-70 to 82-84 on Vulkan in the city.
- **Native and pacing:** native resolution and 60 FPS pacing are unchanged.

Screenshots taken with the setting switched on and off within one run look the same. The D3D12 debug layer and Vulkan validation report nothing new. Measurements and method: `launcher/dev/edram-transfers-2026-10-15.md`; details in `patches/README.md`.

**Not verified**: other GPUs.

## Resolved textures loaded in two passes at 3x (fixed 2026-10-15)

With resolution scaling, the game's render targets resolved to memory are decoded into textures again at their next use, and at 3x the screen-sized ones are reloaded every frame, some several times, because the game reuses that memory for other targets. Each load decoded into a scratch buffer and then copied that into the texture: 1.6 ms of the GPU frame on D3D12 and 1.85 ms on Vulkan, standing in the city.

`patches/rexglue-texture-load-direct.patch` decodes these textures straight into themselves through an unsigned integer view, in one pass (setting `texture_load_direct`, default on, both APIs; on Vulkan with the storage views of `vulkan_texture_load_storage_copy`, so NVIDIA only by default). Texture loads went to 1.1 ms on D3D12 and 1.4 ms on Vulkan. Timedemo at 3x, median frame: D3D12 10.6 to 10.2 ms standing in the city (-4%), 10.2 to 9.7 ms driving (-5%), 9.7 to 8.1 ms in the map zoom-out, 10.4 to 9.9 ms in the race; Vulkan 9.8 to 9.4 ms (-4%), 10.0 to 9.4 ms (-6%), 8.8 to 7.9 ms, 11.9 to 11.6 ms. Native resolution and 60 FPS pacing are unchanged.

Screenshots taken with the setting switched on and off within one run look the same; the D3D12 debug layer and Vulkan validation (with synchronization validation) report nothing new. Measurements, what drives the resolves and the reloads, and the options left: `launcher/dev/resolves-2026-10-15.md`; details in `patches/README.md`.

**Not verified**: other GPUs.

## Stutter while new shaders compile (fixed 2026-10-15)

On a first run, in new places or races, and after a driver update or a change to the translated shaders, the GPU emulation creates new pipelines, which the driver compiles. Draws whose pipeline was still being created were already left out, but at the end of every frame both APIs then waited for every pending pipeline, holding the frame for the slowest compile. Vulkan also compiled a stand-in pipeline for each one on the GPU command thread first. With the driver's cache cold:
- D3D12 frames of up to 330 to 400 ms;
- on Vulkan, up to 1.9 s, with 17 s a session in frames over 50 ms; the map zoom-out fell 1 to 2 s behind.

`patches/rexglue-async-pipelines-no-frame-wait.patch` stops the frame from waiting. A draw waits for its pipeline only within 4 ms a frame (`async_pipeline_wait_budget_ms`), and is otherwise left out until the pipeline is ready. Vulkan no longer creates the stand-in pipelines. A session started with nothing cached:
- **D3D12:** worst frame 160 to 190 ms instead of 330 to 400, half as much time in frames over 50 ms, map zoom-out 1% low 23 to 26 FPS instead of 13 to 19.
- **Vulkan:** worst frame 170 to 250 ms instead of 1.9 s, 0.4 s in frames over 50 ms instead of 17 s, race 1% low 51 to 55 FPS instead of 13 to 15.
- **What shows instead:** objects missing for a moment while their pipelines compile. In one screenshot of a cold Vulkan zoom-out, some buildings were missing.
- **The remaining slow frames:** the remaining frames over 100 ms are also there with everything cached (one at the title screen and the race's loading).
- **Warm sessions:** with the shader storage and the driver's cache warm, as in normal play after the first runs, nothing is compiled during play and nothing changes.

`patches/rexglue-vulkan-pipeline-storage-parallel-translation.patch` translates the stored shaders in parallel when Vulkan starts: 0.3 s to 0.05 s of every start.

`patches/rexglue-vulkan-pipeline-cache-file.patch` can keep the driver's pipeline cache between runs (`vulkan_pipeline_cache_file`, off by default). NVIDIA's driver has a cache of its own, so it gained nothing here; it is meant for drivers without one.

Measurements and method: `launcher/dev/shader-stutter-2026-10-15.md`; details in `patches/README.md`.

**Not verified**: other GPUs and drivers.

## D3D12: the shader storage preload used fewer threads and didn't wait for them (fixed 2026-10-17)

At start, the D3D12 pipeline cache never started its extra creation threads (a loop compared the thread count with itself, as in Xenia), so it also never reached the wait for the pipelines still being created. Start-up went on with 2–9 pipelines still being created (cold driver cache, the user's 1,684). `rexglue-d3d12-storage-preload-threads.patch` starts them (15 threads in place of 12 here) and waits for every pipeline. The cold preload went from 1,039 to 982 ms (median of 7, faster in every pair), the warm one from 51 to 48 ms.

## Vulkan: valid pipelines cut from the shader storage when some weren't supported (fixed 2026-10-17)

When the GPU or driver couldn't create some stored pipelines (features another device has), each start cut that many valid pipelines off the end of the storage file, and the next starts had fewer to create. `rexglue-vulkan-pipeline-storage-keep-unsupported.patch` keeps all of them and only skips the unsupported ones at load. A check that made a quarter of the user's 472 Vulkan pipelines unsupported: 358 left in the file before, all 472 now.

## Shaders prepared before the first start, and kept prepared (2026-10-17; the pack grown and stale entries retired 2026-10-25)

The storage keeps every pipeline the game has created, for the next start, but the first time anything is drawn (a first run, places and races not seen yet) its pipeline is compiled while playing: the draw is left out meanwhile, so objects are missing for a moment, and frames can hitch. After a graphics driver update the stored pipelines are compiled again at the next start.

**The shader pack.** The fork ships `shaderpack/545407F8.shaderpack`: 762 shaders and 5,876 pipeline descriptions (3,111 D3D12, 1,721 Vulkan, and 570 and 474 of D3D12's rasterizer-ordered views and Vulkan's fragment shader interlock), from the fork's test sessions, the fork owner's own and scripted playthroughs of the 38 arcade races on D3D12 `rtv` and Vulkan `fbo` (2026-10-25: 183 and 160 pipelines and 39 shaders more than the 723 and 5,533 before). It holds no game data: fingerprints (the storage's own XXH3 keys), two places in `default.xex` by address and length, and pipeline descriptions (render state and shader fingerprints), which the storage holds anyway. The shaders are rebuilt from your own game files, `default.xex` and the effects in `xarchive_cache.rpf`: pixel shaders as they are; vertex shaders bound to a mesh's vertex layout and to the pixel shader they feed by the game's own Direct3D code (recompiled, run as the game runs it), for layouts made from the effects' preset files, until the result is the shader the pack names. `launcher/dev/shaderpack/build_pack.py` refuses a pack with anything else, and accepts one only after `mcla.exe` rebuilt every shader in it.

**At every start** (`src/shader_prepare.cpp`, every platform), before the storage loads, the game adds whatever of the pack the storage lacks: after the storage was deleted, reset or cut, after an update brought a new pack, on a first run. When nothing is missing that takes 1 ms here (the pack and the storage files read and checked); when shaders must be rebuilt, 3.6 s here for all 762 (one thread; logged). The stored pipelines are then created as at any start; with the driver's cache cold that happens before the logos, with nothing on the screen.

**The launcher** compiles them beforehand, with a progress line: `mcla.exe --mcla_prepare_shaders=true` does the same import, then creates every stored pipeline with the settings the game is played with, and quits before the game starts. It runs after every build and update, from **More** › *Prepare shaders*, and before Play whenever the game's record says the driver cache is cold or the storage changed. After every start and every preparation the game writes that record, a stamp (`<cache>\shaders\local\545407F8.stamp.json`): the pack, the storage versions, the API, the pipeline storage file in use (which says the EDRAM path the GPU code chose), the resolution scale, the adapter and its driver (D3D12: the user-mode driver's version; Vulkan: its driver version and pipeline cache UUID), the display drivers installed, the files' sizes. At Play the launcher compares it with the pack the game will use (the checkout's, or the one a `--mcla_shader_pack` option names), the API and resolution scale the game will start with against the ones the last start *asked for* (`mcla.toml`, which the pause menu also changes, and the options the launcher passes; not against what the game ended up with, which differs when it clamps a resolution scale a weaker GPU can't do or falls back to another API), the display drivers installed now and the files as they are. With `mcla_shader_pack_auto = false` the pack counts only when the last run was a preparation. If the driver was updated, the card changed, the API or the resolution changed, the pack changed, or the files shrank or vanished, it prepares first, saying why ("the graphics driver was updated", "the shader cache was reset"), then starts the game, unless you end the preparation: **Play now** starts the game at once (it adds the pack itself and creates the stored pipelines as it starts) and **Stop** ends it and starts nothing; otherwise the game starts at once. What it can't notice: a graphics API left on "any" that the game resolves differently than before, a switch between two display adapters that both stay installed, and a storage format change without a new pack (which the game refuses; no preparation fixes it). The check reads a few small files and lists the display adapters: 8 to 25 ms here in a new process, under 1 ms after that, without starting `mcla.exe`. The anti-aliasing setting doesn't count: every stored pipeline is created whatever it is.

**Your storage is protected** (the review of 2026-10-17, `launcher/dev/shader-prepare-2026-10-17.md`):
- A session lock (`shaders\local\545407F8.lock`), taken by every `mcla.exe` before it reads the storage and held until it ends: a preparation refuses to run beside a game (exit code 7, "in use", the one failure the launcher tries again at the next Play: a sharing or lock violation, a storage file another program has open without delete sharing or mapped, such as an antivirus scan or a backup, or the lock held; a read-only file, a permission that denies the replacement or a lock file that can't be opened is code 6 and not tried again at every Play), and a game started during a preparation waits for it, up to two minutes (its window answers the system meanwhile, so it doesn't show as "not responding", but isn't painted until the preparation ends).
- A storage file that exists but can't be read (held by another program, a cloud placeholder that can't be fetched) stops the import before anything is written: it is never taken for a missing one.
- Only the four pipeline lists this build knows are accepted from a pack, by name, with headers and record sizes from the SDK's own constants; file names never come from the pack.
- Entries already in a file stay, in their order. Each file is written beside the old one (a name of its own per process), flushed to the disk and renamed over it, with a short retry while another program has it open; a failure removes the temporary file and puts back what was already replaced.
- A journal (`shaders\local\545407F8.import`) records, for each file an import changed, its length before, its length after and a hash of what was added. If the process ends after the import and before the stored pipelines were all created (a crash, a hang, a power cut, you ending a preparation, the launcher closing during one), the next start cuts the files back to their lengths before (a file the import created is removed), but only a file that still is what the import left: the recorded length and the hash of the added part. A file that grew since (the start that imported went on to play, and only the record of its success failed) or that anything else changed is left as it is; your own entries are never cut. Any such interrupted run counts as a failed start: after two in a row with the same pack, its import is disabled and the game uses your own entries only (the next start that gets through resets the count); *Prepare shaders* in the launcher (or deleting `545407F8.import`) tries it again, and also the shaders recorded as not rebuildable (a damaged `xarchive_cache.rpf` that you repaired).
- To start the storage over by hand: close the game and delete `Documents\mcla\cache\shaders`; the game compiles shaders as it meets them again, and adds the pack at its next start. `mcla_shader_pack_auto = false` in `mcla.toml` (or `--mcla_shader_pack_auto=false`) stops the import at start.

Measured on 2026-10-17 with the first pack (723 shaders; the grown pack's figures follow the list), at 3x on the test machine, with the driver's cache cold (a new `tmp_shader_salt`, as after a driver update or on a first run):

## Resolves dumped every sample to the EDRAM buffer first (fixed 2026-10-15)

With host render targets, every resolve first copied every sample of the render targets over its area into the EDRAM buffer, and only then converted that into the destination. At 3x that dump was 60% of the resolve time: 1.35 ms of the GPU frame standing in the city by day on D3D12, 1.0 ms in the race.

`patches/rexglue-direct-resolve.patch` makes the SDK's `direct_host_resolve` (on by default, until now a stub) read the render target that owns the area directly, with the same conversions as the dump and the same copy code after it, at every resolution scale, on both APIs. Resolves per frame standing in the city, D3D12 and Vulkan: 0.48 to 0.33 and 0.50 to 0.42 ms at native, 1.09 to 0.67 and 0.93 to 0.65 ms at 2x, 2.2 to 1.1 and 1.9 to 1.1 ms at 3x. Timedemo at 3x, median frame: D3D12 10.1 to 9.0 ms standing in the city (-11%), 9.8 to 8.6 ms driving (-12%), 10.2 to 9.3 ms in the race (-9%); Vulkan 9.3 to 8.5 ms (-9%), 9.4 to 8.7 ms (-8%), 11.3 to 10.8 ms (-4%). At 2x the frame here is close to the game thread's limit: -6% standing in the city on D3D12, -1% on Vulkan, -3 and -7% in the race, with higher 1% lows. With a 120 FPS cap at 3x, D3D12 now holds it (93% of frames within 1 ms of the target instead of 26%; Vulkan 97% instead of 84%). At native resolution the frame time here is the game thread's and doesn't change; the resolve time saved is what a GPU-bound system gains. 60 FPS pacing is unchanged. `--direct_host_resolve=false` dumps as before.

Direct resolves were compared byte for byte with the EDRAM buffer path in the same frame, one a frame in turn, 162,550 of them at native, 2x and 3x (the attract movie, the map zoom-out, the city by day, a drive and the race in the rain, on both APIs): all of them matched. That comparison first showed that the EDRAM path read some wrong samples of 4x MSAA depth at odd resolution scales, native included: a thread's 16 consecutive values crossing the middle of a depth tile, whose halves are swapped in the EDRAM layout, which showed in this game as columns 4 pixels wide, 120 pixels apart at 3x, in the daylight depth. `patches/rexglue-resolve-4xmsaa-depth-samples.patch` fixes that shader, so a resolve gives the same result whichever path it takes; screenshots with direct resolves on and off are identical at every still pixel. Measurements and method: `launcher/dev/direct-resolve-2026-10-15.md`; details in `patches/README.md`.

**Not verified**: other GPUs. On Vulkan drivers without multisampled integer sampled images, resolves from multisampled colour render targets go through the EDRAM buffer (not tested).

## The pause menu's settings (2026-10-16)

The submenus upstream added to the Settings tab of the pause menu (DISPLAY, PERFORMANCE, CONTROLS; `src/settings_menu.cpp`) had these faults.

- **The settings file collected other values.** Leaving a submenu saved with `rex::cvar::SaveConfig`, which rewrites `mcla.toml` with every option that differs from its default, wherever it came from. It wrote the launcher's command line, a host's own settings, the developer tools' options and their paths into the file. A run of the dev tools saved `log_file`, `user_data_root`, the pad script and more, and a Windows path came out as `"C:\Users\..."`, which is not valid TOML (`\U` starts an escape). At the next start the whole file failed to parse and none of its settings applied.
  - Now only the options changed in these menus are saved, merged into the file by the SDK (`rex::cvar::SaveConfigValues`, `patches/rexglue-cvar-toml-strings.patch`). Each one's `key = value` is replaced where it is, keeping a comment after it, or added before the first `[table]`. Every other line is kept as it was: comments, keys the game doesn't know, tables, values over several lines (a `key = ` line inside a multi-line string or array is not taken for a key), quoted keys, a BOM, CRLF or LF.
  - The file is written beside the old one (`mcla.toml.<pid>.tmp`), flushed to the disk and renamed over it. A file that exists but can't be read is not written. If the save fails, the changes stay pending and are tried again at the next save.
  - A double-quoted value whose backslashes are not TOML escapes, as old saves wrote paths, is rewritten as a literal string with the same text (not when the text holds a quote).
  - The SDK's own save (the F4 overlay's *Save to config*) writes valid strings too, merged the same way, and only the options changed while running: not the command line's, the file's or the game's PC defaults. A file that still fails to parse is read statement by statement, so one bad line no longer costs the rest.
- **The launcher's 40, 45 and 50 FPS showed as 30, 30 and 60** in FPS TARGET, which only offered 30, 60, 120 and uncapped; stepping the row then lost the value. The list now has 40, 45 and 50 on PC, and they display and save as themselves. The PS5 keeps 30 and 60.
- **Restart-only rows are marked from the options themselves.** The options the game reads once, at start-up, are declared `kRequiresRestart`, and their rows add "(NEXT START)" to their names. That covers RACE SHADOWS, SIMPLE CAR SHADOWS, DISTANT TREES, FULLSCREEN BLUR and INTRO (ANTI ALIASING was one until it was made to switch during play, below); the SDK's F4 overlay shows them as `[restart]` too, as it does `mcla_gpu_backend`.
  - DISTANT TREES said "(NEXT LOAD)". But the game creates the impostor targets with the world, once, at start-up (sub_82305230 → sub_8230C388 → sub_8230B778, where its switch is read). A temporary log in both showed one world setup and 53 impostor setups at start-up, and none when an arcade race started or was quit back to free roam. So it is "(NEXT START)" now.
- **After a submenu, the Settings list kept the submenu's title and had no tab strip** until the pause menu closed. Entering a submenu hides the strip (`tabs_count = 0`) and retitles the menu. Leaving it now calls the strip's own render (sub_82630E40, captured by a mid-asm hook at 0x82630E50), which sets the tabs again and renders the selected tab, title included. The call is made only when the captured object's vtable is in the game's image and holds that render. Leaving with B and with Start goes through the same path; both save. Checked: Start inside DISPLAY closes the pause menu; the next pause opens on PAUSE, and B backs out of it.

Checked on Windows, D3D12, on copies of the settings file through `devrun.py --toml`:
- a copy of a real one, saved with CRLF: three rows changed, those three lines replaced or added, the other five byte for byte the same;
- one with an old-style `log_file` path, a line that is not TOML, a comment and a table: it loaded (HUD UPDATES showed its 120); after a change, the path was a literal string, the bad line, the comment and the table were kept, and the new key went in before the table;
- one with a BOM, CRLF, a quoted key with a comment after its value, a multi-line string and an array that each hold a `key = value` line, and a table: FPS TARGET replaced the quoted key's value in place with its comment kept, HUD UPDATES went in before the table, everything else stayed byte for byte; after RESTART GAME the new game read it (DISTANT TREES off);
- that result loaded back with the values set in the menu;
- no file at all: a file with a heading comment and the one key changed.

The real `mcla.toml` stayed byte for byte the same throughout.

## One settings file for the launcher and the pause menu; more settings in the game (2026-10-16)

The launcher passed its choices on the command line at every start, and the command line beats `mcla.toml`, so a setting changed in the pause menu went back at the next start. Some settings were only in the launcher, and several only took effect after a restart.

**The launcher edits `mcla.toml`** (`launcher\src\GameSettings.cs`), with the rules the game uses when it saves (above): a key's value is replaced where it is or added before the first `[table]`, everything else is kept, and the file is written beside the old one (`mcla.toml.launcher-<pid>.tmp`), flushed to the disk and renamed over it. A file that isn't UTF-8 text is not rewritten. A value it can't read, such as `"\uD800"` (not a Unicode scalar value), shows as set by hand instead of stopping the launcher.
- **Shared values.** Its drop-downs show the file's values, or the game's defaults where the file has none, and a change is written at once. There is no "Game setting" choice any more: the launcher and the pause menu change the same values.
- **The command line.** Only *More mcla.exe options* is still passed, and it still wins.
- **While the game runs,** the drop-downs are greyed out with a note to use the pause menu: the game reads the file only when it starts, and writes it itself. Once the game has closed, the file is read again. The check every two seconds compares with what the window shows, so a game that started and closed between two checks doesn't leave them greyed out.
- **Migration.** The first time the new launcher opens, the choices that were forcing a value go into `mcla.toml`, with `vsync = false` along with any frame rate, after a copy of it is kept as `mcla.toml.before-launcher-settings` (README.md). It runs once: the copy marks it as done (an empty one when there was no file) and `settings.json` drops the old choices, so a lost flag can't bring them back over what the pause menu set since. Not while the game runs (it waits until it has closed), and not from a `settings.json` that can't be read (a copy is kept as `settings.json.unreadable`). A failed save of `settings.json` is shown.
- **Tests.** Checked on copies of a real `settings.json` and `mcla.toml` with a console program that compiles the launcher's code (a test copy, not the real files):
  - the migration: the copy made first and kept, the right values written (`vsync = false` too), CRLF and the other lines kept; with the flag lost and the old choices still there, a second run changing nothing over values set since; deferred while a game runs, then done; an empty copy when there was no file; a file that isn't UTF-8 left as it is and the move tried again later;
  - what each drop-down shows, including a value set by hand, and values such as `"\uD800"`, `"\UFFFFFFFF"` or an unclosed string, which no longer throw;
  - a file with a BOM, a quoted key with a comment after its value, a multi-line string and an array holding `key = value` lines, an old-style path and a table;
  - a new file.
  - The published launcher in a scratch copy of the folder layout, with a copy of the real `settings.json` and `mcla.toml`: started while a stand-in `mcla.exe` ran, nothing was written until it exited, then the file got `fullscreen`, `resolution_scale` and `mcla_ui_hud_rate` and `settings.json` its flag without the old choices; the drop-downs showed the moved values; a value written while "the game" ran showed once it had closed, and the next start moved nothing again.

**The launcher's four SDK settings are the game's own PC defaults** (`MclaApp::ApplyPcDefaults`, `src/mcla_app.cpp`): `clear_memory_page_state=false`, `execute_unclipped_draw_vs_on_cpu=true`, `shared_memory_hot_page_faults=4` and `shared_memory_hot_page_ms=10000`, each used unless the command line, an environment variable or `mcla.toml` sets it, and never written to the file. The log says which were set by the user. On Windows two CPU options are set by the number of logical processors too (section "The uncapped frame rate and the timer thread's core, by processor count" below). The PS5 host keeps its own (`ps5/game/main_ps5.cpp`). The launcher also stopped passing `--vsync=false`, which the game does by itself (section "Frame pacing" above).

**In the pause menu:**
- **DISPLAY › RESOLUTION and GRAPHICS API (Windows), (NEXT START).** The resolution scale is built into the render targets, caches and translated shaders when the GPU emulation starts, and the backend is chosen before the window opens. The label stays "(NEXT START)" however the row is stepped: the game scales the whole pause menu to its widest label (checked with stills of a GRAPHICS API step sequence: the values' widths, "(RECOMMENDED)" included, change nothing), so the "(RESTART NEEDED)" this row said once its value differed from the one the game started with made the menu jump larger at the first step and back at the third. The labels that still change while the menu is open (HDR's "(NOT AVAILABLE)", the low latency row's vendor name) are relabelled through the game's own row-label call (0x8263B860, which stores the text pointer in the row).
- **CONTROLS › HIDE MOUSE POINTER (PC)** takes effect at once: the option now has a change callback.
- **PERFORMANCE › ANTI ALIASING switches during play.** The game keeps the multisample mode in its renderer (+384: 2 for its 2x MSAA, 1 for none). Its frame code reads that mode every frame and picks the EDRAM tiling from it (sub_822E6408), so `src/perf_options.cpp` writes it when the option changes, at the swap (from `mcla_present_interval`), between two frames. One thing is sized from the mode at start-up only: the world setup creates the "Shadow Collector" and "Depth Buffer" targets with it (sub_82310E40 → sub_825F4750). Started with anti-aliasing off, they stay 1x when it is turned on. Checked on D3D12 (debug layer) and Vulkan (validation), each at 1x and 3x, starting with anti-aliasing off, against a fresh start with it on in the same mode, at the same places and times:
  - **Scenes:** the city at sunset (the lower half, the car and its shadow, poles and fences); the GPS map zoomed all the way out and in; an arcade race at midnight in the rain, turned on right before its loading screen, then the intro cameras and the countdown; the race, off and on again; its replay from the start. Each looked as in the fresh start: the same dithered pattern on thin geometry, the same shadows, nothing different in the lower half; the map views matched to within 0.5 of 255 on average.
  - **D3D12:** no errors; the warnings are the usual ones (initial states ignored, MIN/MAX blend factors, live objects at exit).
  - **Vulkan:** only the two `vkCmdEndQuery` errors it gives on the title screen, before any switch.
  - **Memory:** 50 switches off and on in each run (100 writes): video memory stayed flat, or rose once when the other mode's targets were first made (about 20 MB at 1x, 180 MB at 3x) and then stayed flat.
  - **Not reached:** the garage and customization, which need driving there, and a view with the rear-view mirror (no camera switch was found from the controller script).
- **RESTART GAME (Windows).** The first press relabels it "RESTART GAME (PRESS AGAIN)" for 5 seconds; a second press saves, starts `mcla.exe` again with the same command line and closes this one. The new process gets an inherited handle to the old one (`--mcla_restart_wait_handle`, passed through `PROC_THREAD_ATTRIBUTE_HANDLE_LIST`, so no other handle is inherited and a reused process id can't be mistaken for it) and waits on it before it loads its settings, its window or the GPU plugin. If the old one hasn't closed after 30 seconds, the new one says so in a message box and exits rather than run beside it. If the new process can't be started, this one stays open. As with QUIT GAME, progress since the last save is lost.
- **QUIT GAME closes the game.** It used `RequestDeferredQuit`, which left the UI loop. The app then waited for the game's main thread, which doesn't end, so the game played on behind a window that no longer answered (seen in a test run). On Windows and Linux, QUIT GAME and RESTART GAME now close the window on the UI thread, as its close button does, which ends the title and the process (`ReXApp::OnClosing`). The PS5 keeps `RequestDeferredQuit`, as before.
- **Closing waits for a career save being written.** Ending the title gives the game's threads 200 ms, which could cut a save short. QUIT GAME, RESTART GAME and the window's close button now first wait, for up to 10 seconds, while the game holds a file open for writing on a content device (where saves go), and log it. This was true of the close button before the fork's changes too. Checked: SAVE GAME, then the close button while the save was being written: "a save is being written; waiting for it", done 50 ms later, then the game closed. On the PS5, QUIT GAME runs the same wait (`src/save_wait.cpp`, built into both hosts) before it asks the system to close the title; not run on a console.
- **Closing the window saves too.** A change made in a submenu that is still open is saved when the window is closed (`MclaApp::OnWindowCloseRequested`).

**devrun** (`launcher\dev\devrun.py`) follows a RESTART GAME: it waits for the restarted game too, then puts `mcla.toml` back and checks the real data. Putting the file back is in a `finally`, after closing any game of the run, so an error or Ctrl+C on the way leaves the real file as it was. It no longer passes the four SDK settings, which are the game's defaults now. The dev pad has `saving` (whether a save is being written; `saving wait` until one is) and `close` (the close button's WM_CLOSE, which unlike `quit` waits for a save).

**Not verified:** Linux, where RESTART GAME isn't offered, and the PS5, whose menus have none of the Windows rows. The PC defaults apply on Linux too, where the fork's patches have not been built or run.

## Single-tile rendering (2026-10-16)

Upstream's `mcla_single_tile` (on by default) is meant to draw the main scene as one predicated tile instead of the Xbox 360's two. With 2x MSAA the game splits the 720p scene into two tiles (1280x512 and 1280x208) and submits every scene draw once per tile, about 1,370 draws each in the city. Until now it never took effect (`launcher/dev/single-tile-2026-10-16.md`): its hook acted only on a null render target, which no call passes, and the SDK's EDRAM is the console's 2,048 tiles, too few for one tile (1,472 tiles of colour and 1,472 of depth).

**Now it works:**
- **SDK** (`patches/rexglue-edram-size.patch`): an `edram_tiles` setting, 2048 (the console's, the default) or 4096. With 4096 the EDRAM wraps at 4,096 tiles, render target bases are 12 bits, the EDRAM buffer is twice the size (20 MiB at 1x, 180 MiB at 3x; host render targets don't grow) and the EDRAM resolve shaders have copies that wrap at 4,096.
- **Game:** on PC it sets `edram_tiles = 4096` as a default (`MclaApp::ApplyPcDefaults`, applied now before the GPU starts), and so does the PS5 build (`ps5/game/main_ps5.cpp`, untested on the console; below). The hook (`src/render_perf.cpp`, 0x8217A700 in `BeginTiledRendering`) acts on a tiled pass whose whole colour and depth fit in the EDRAM the GPU emulates (`IGraphicsSystem::edram_tile_count`, new in the SDK patch): the main scene with 2x MSAA becomes one 1280x720 tile, one bin and one resolve per target. The pause menu and the GPS map (4x MSAA, 2 x 2,944 tiles) keep their three tiles; with anti-aliasing off the game draws one tile by itself. `mcla_edram_limit` applies only with the larger EDRAM.
- **PERFORMANCE › SINGLE TILE RENDERING** is back in the pause menu, first in the list, and switches during play. With 2,048 tiles (`--edram_tiles=2048`, or a GPU that can't bind the larger EDRAM buffer) it says "(UNAVAILABLE)", A leaves it as it is (so such a session doesn't save a value for the next), and the hook changes nothing. The launcher has *Single tile rendering* (`mcla_single_tile` in `mcla.toml`).

**Gains,** one tile against two, anti-aliasing on, switched within each run:
- **3x:** frames 11.0% shorter in the city by day on D3D12 and 9.2% on Vulkan; 19.7% and 14.6% in the race.
- **Native:** the race 14.0% shorter on D3D12 and 25.3% on Vulkan, where the GPU command thread was the bottleneck (94% busy); the city stays at the game's own limit (about 7.0 ms).
- **GPU command thread:** 28 to 35% less CPU time; 30 to 37% fewer draws a frame.
- **GPU:** 1.0 ms less at 3x in the city, 1.1 (Vulkan) to 2.2 ms (D3D12) in the race. The earlier estimate was 0.8 and 1.3 ms.
- **120 FPS cap at 3x, standing in the city:** frames within 1 ms of the target 92.8 → 99.2% on D3D12, 94.4 → 99.0% on Vulkan.
- **Anti-aliasing off** is now 2 to 5% faster than on at 3x (it was 8 to 18% faster than two tiles).

**Checks:**
- **Resolves:** `launcher/dev/direct-resolve-validate.sdk.patch` compared each direct resolve with the same resolve through the EDRAM buffer, now with the 4,096-tile shaders (the one-tile scene's colour crosses tile 2,048): 33,700 resolves at native and 3x, city and race, both APIs, none different.
- **The picture:** one tile against two, switched within one run with time almost stopped (`mcla_fixed_dt`), shot on consecutive frames: the title, the map zoom-out, the city by day and at night, the pause menu and the GPS map at 1x, 2x and 3x on both APIs, also through the EDRAM buffer path (`--direct_host_resolve=false`) and on the ROV and FSI paths. The differences are frame-to-frame noise (under 0.5 of 255 on average), with no seam at the old tile boundary (row 512), apart from the ROV case below. The race, its pause menu and its replay look the same either way.
- **Nothing relies on the console's wrap-around** that was seen: `launcher/dev/edram-wrap-log.sdk.patch` logged every EDRAM range crossing tile 2,048. With 4,096 tiles the only ones are the one-tile scene's colour (tiles 1,472 to 2,912) and its resolve; with 2,048 tiles, through the title, the city, the menus and the GPS map, none at all.
- **Switching:** 50 switches of single tile and 50 of anti-aliasing during play, switches around and inside the pause menu and the GPS map, 20 of each in the race, and the pause menu's own row (A on SINGLE TILE RENDERING): the draws a frame went from one tile to two and back each time, and anti-aliasing off was one tile either way. Video memory stayed flat during the switches, or rose once when the other mode's targets were first made (two tiles: 42 MB at 1x, 360 MB at 3x; anti-aliasing off: 20 and 106 MB).
- **Debug layers:** the D3D12 debug layer, switching in the city at 1x and 3x and in the race at 3x: no errors, the usual warnings (initial states ignored, MIN/MAX blend factors, live objects at exit). Vulkan synchronization validation, in the city at 1x and 3x and in the race at 1x: only the write-after-write hazards between compute dispatches (the texture loads' scratch buffer, the EDRAM buffer's clears and dumps) that it reports with 2,048 tiles too, in about the same numbers.
- **The console's size:** with `--edram_tiles=2048`, two tiles whatever `mcla_single_tile` says, and the row says UNAVAILABLE. Two of the SDK patch's changes apply at 2,048 tiles too, and neither shows in this game, where nothing crosses tile 2,048 at that size: a resolve clear whose area wraps round the end of the EDRAM is no longer dropped (the offset from the render target's base is taken modulo the EDRAM size, a bug that is also in xenia), and the transfer shaders' address constant packs its pitches in 9 bits instead of 10.
- **The game's tile surface cache.** `BeginTiledRendering` keeps the surfaces it tiles into in 8 slots (keyed by MSAA mode, size and format) that are never freed; a ninth key would end in a function that never returns (`sub_821BD618`). The game's own keys take 4 (two 4x ones for the menus, 2x two-tile, 1x without anti-aliasing) and one tile adds one: 5 of 8 at most in every session measured (title, map zoom-out, city, pause menu, GPS map, a race with its pause menu and replay, 50 switches of each option, native and 3x, both APIs). The hook logs the count each time it grows, and doesn't make one tile when its key isn't there yet and 7 or more slots are taken. The garage was not reached.

**Anti-aliasing changed between one-tile frames** (fixed). The first scripted runs switched the options with the dev pad's `set`, which is what the SDK's F4 settings overlay and the console's `set` do too: during play, without the pause menu. Some sequences then left the vehicle pass, and what is drawn after it, in the other MSAA mode than the scene's (1x draws in the 2x one-tile scene, or 2x in the 1x one): on the default path EDRAM transfers every frame (0.6 ms at 3x in the city, 1 to 3 ms in the race) until the pause menu next opened or the scene went to two tiles for a few frames. It happens when the MSAA mode changes between two frames that are both one tile: anti-aliasing off (which the game draws as one tile itself) and anti-aliasing on with single tile, a combination the console never had. Changes made inside the pause menu were clean, the menu's own tiled passes coming in between. Now a change of MSAA mode between one-tile frames has frames in two tiles around it: after anti-aliasing is turned on, the hook draws the next 8 frames in two tiles; turning it off waits at the swap until 8 frames have been drawn in two tiles (`src/render_perf.cpp`, `src/perf_options.cpp`; 3 were not always enough, 4 were). Checked with frame logs on D3D12 at native (capped and uncapped) and 3x, through `set` sequences, the pause menu's rows (two tiles → anti-aliasing off → single tile on → anti-aliasing on in one menu session) and a start with anti-aliasing off turned on in the menu: no frame with the vehicles in the other mode, no transfers in the scene.

**The ROV path** (`--render_target_path_d3d12=rov`, the default on Intel GPUs on D3D12) showed foliage drawn wrong at night at 1x after a series of switches of the tiles without the menu, at frozen time; the same happened with 2,048 tiles after anti-aliasing switches, so it isn't the larger EDRAM. It came back in 6 of 8 runs of that sequence, also after the fix above (anti-aliasing was on throughout), and not with switches through the pause menu; the cause was not found. ROV is not the default on NVIDIA or AMD.

**The PS5** now asks for 4,096 tiles too (`ps5/game/main_ps5.cpp`, `MCLA_EDRAM_TILES`), so single-tile rendering is on there by default. **It is untested on the console** and needs a run there: the build, the GPU starting with "emulating an EDRAM of 4096 tiles" in the log, the picture, and the frame rate. RADV binds the larger buffer (`maxStorageBufferRange` 4,294,967,295). On PC, a Vulkan run with the PS5 host's own settings (`execute_unclipped_draw_vs_on_cpu=false`, its shared-memory settings, `clear_memory_page_state=false`, `vulkan_submit_on_primary_buffer_end=false`): one tile acts, the city by day and at night, the pause menu, the GPS map and a race look the same as two tiles, and the D3D12 frame logs with the same settings show no new EDRAM transfers. To go back: an `edram_tiles = 2048` line in `/data/mcla/mcla.toml` (the PS5 sets it as a default, which the file overrides), or build with `-DMCLA_EDRAM_TILES=2048`.

**Not verified:** Linux. On Vulkan, a GPU whose `maxStorageBufferRange` is under 180 MiB gets 2,048 tiles at 3x, with a warning in the log. Measurements and method: `launcher/dev/single-tile-edram-2026-10-16.md`.

## Display gamma for PC monitors (2026-10-16)

The game's tonemap writes sRGB-encoded colour (`1.055·x^(1/2.2) − 0.055`) into a plain 8-bit front buffer and leaves the display transfer to the console's gamma ramp: a 256-entry, 10-bit table that the display hardware applied and the SDK's presenter applies, on both APIs (`launcher/dev/frame-structure-2026-10-16.md`). Direct3D sets the ramp once, when the device is created: the game's identity ramp goes through `D3DDevice_SetGammaRamp` (sub_82416000), which adjusts it for the display with a table that the XDK helper sub_82426B68 builds from `VdGetCurrentDisplayGamma`. The SDK answers type 2, a TV (BT.709), as for an Xbox 360 on an HDTV, which makes the table BT.709 OETF(sRGB EOTF(x)). Type 1, sRGB, makes it the identity. On a PC monitor, which expects sRGB, the TV ramp makes the shadows darker than the game drew them: 8-bit levels 64, 128 and 192 come out as 193, 462 and 741 of 1023 (48, 115 and 185 of 255) instead of 256, 513 and 770.

`mcla_display_gamma` picks the type (`src/display_gamma.cpp`):
- `tv`, the default, is type 2, as before; `pc` is type 1, the identity ramp.
- The launcher's *Display gamma* and the pause menu's DISPLAY › DISPLAY GAMMA (TV, PC MONITOR) set it.
- A mid-asm hook right after the helper's call to `VdGetCurrentDisplayGamma` (0x82426B98) puts the chosen type where the call wrote its answer. The helper caches its table by type, so a new type builds a new table.
- **It switches during play.** The game never sets its ramp again, so when the option changes, an override of `D3DDevice_Swap` (sub_82419E98) calls `SetGammaRamp` again at the start of the next swap, with the identity ramp the device creation used (the PWL variant for a 10-bit front buffer, which this game doesn't use). That runs on the thread that owns the device, between two frames, and the swap sends the new ramp to the GPU with its frame, as a game changing its ramp would. A change shows on screen within a few frames (two or three at 60 FPS in the screenshots).
- The log says which ramp is in use, at the first swap and after each change, with its values at levels 0, 64, 128, 192 and 255. It is a game-side change; the SDK is not patched.

Checked on Windows, RTX 5080, with `devrun.py`, in the city at 23:30 (time of day poked), at 1x on both APIs and at 3x on D3D12:
- **The ramps.** TV: 0, 193, 462, 741 and 1023, the values `frame-structure-2026-10-16.md` read from the presenter; PC monitor: 0, 256, 513, 770 and 1023, the identity.
- **The picture.** Applying the TV ramp to a PC monitor screenshot gives the TV screenshot of the same moment to within 1 level on 97.6 to 99.98% of pixels (the rest is what moved between the two). The night city's average brightness went from 46 to 60 of 255, and the pixels below 64 from 73% to 61%; behind the pause menu, from 32 to 45. Dark detail such as the car's tyres, its rear bumper and the driver through the rear window shows with PC monitor and is black with TV.
- **Switching during play.** 47 switches a run, 40 of them one every 3 frames, from the dev pad and from the menu row (right, right, A): every screenshot was wholly one ramp or the other, with no mixed or damaged frame. The menu row saved `mcla_display_gamma = "pc"` to `mcla.toml`.
- **Starting with `pc`** in `mcla.toml` (through `--toml`): the first frame already had the identity ramp.
- **D3D12 debug layer:** no errors, only the usual warnings (buffer initial states, MIN/MAX blend factors, live objects at exit). **Vulkan validation** with synchronization validation: only what it reports without this change (`vkCmdEndQuery` 07007 and 01923, `ShaderOutputNotConsumed`, and 10 write-after-write hazards between texture-load dispatches during start-up, before any switch).

**Not verified**: other GPUs, Linux and the PS5 (the option is in the PS5's menu too). In HDR (below) the same table is applied, so the setting keeps its meaning.

## FSR 3.1 anti-aliasing (2026-10-17)

A third anti-aliasing choice beside the game's 2x MSAA and none: AMD FSR 3.1 at native resolution (PERFORMANCE › ANTI ALIASING, the launcher's Anti-aliasing; `mcla_anti_aliasing = "fsr"`). The scene's draws are moved by a sub-pixel jitter that changes every frame; at the game's motion blur draw, motion vectors are made from the depth, the vehicle IDs in the stencil and the game's own per-car reprojection matrices, FSR blends the frame with the ones before it, and the result goes back into the scene texture the blur, the tone map and HDR output read. `patches/README.md` (`rexglue-temporal-aa.patch`) has the details and the measurements.

Costs and limits:
- **GPU time:** the pass takes about 1.4 ms a frame at 3x on an RTX 5080 (1.38 ms on D3D12, 1.45 ms on Vulkan), 0.58 ms at 2x and 0.16 ms at native; MSAA is off with it, so the net cost is about 1 ms at 3x. **Video memory:** FSR's own resources, about 500 MB at 4K (AMD's figure), released 10 seconds after it's turned off.
- **Look:** a slightly softer picture than MSAA (FSR SHARPNESS, 0.2 by default, sharpens it back); possible faint trails behind fast-moving things. Cars move with the game's own per-car matrices; pedestrians, wheels and animated textures only with the camera.
- **Rain, smoke and particles** have no motion vectors of their own and may leave faint trails or fade. A reactive mask for them (FSR leans on the current frame where translucent effects changed the picture) is in, experimental and off by default (`temporal_aa_reactive`, 4 is a start; `temporal_aa_debug 5` shows it): without it, the smoke plumes in the night race and tire smoke in burnouts and drifts showed no trails (alike to MSAA), while it marks stable glows too (the headlights' pools under a standing car), so it stays off.
- **Exposure:** FSR works out its own (its auto-exposure), not the game's.
- **Availability:** FSR is offered only where its runtime for the graphics API in use was built and the device has what it needs (the Vulkan runtime needs the Vulkan SDK at build time and a Vulkan 1.3 device); elsewhere, and if it fails, the game uses MSAA. Linux and the PS5 build it too (the Vulkan runtime from prebuilt shaders, no Windows tool; on the PS5 linked in, or left out with `MCLA_PS5_FSR=0`), labelled FSR 3.1 (UNTESTED): it has not been built or run on either. On the PS5 it is a choice for the next start (FSR 3.1 (NEXT START) until then): the console's Vulkan device gets the features FSR uses only when the game starts with FSR chosen, so that everyone else's device stays as it was, and if FSR then can't run, the setting goes back to MSAA.
- **With MSAA or off** nothing of it runs: no draw is moved, and the game's frame is drawn as before. On Vulkan the device now enables the 16-bit, storage-image and subgroup-size features it has (for FSR; on Linux too, unmeasured there; on the PS5 only when starting with FSR); measured against the previous build at 3x, frame times are within run-to-run noise (Vulkan with MSAA: race -0.1%, city -1.1%; Vulkan off: race -0.9%; D3D12 with MSAA: race -0.6%).

## FSR 3.1 upscaling (2026-10-27)

With FSR anti-aliasing on, the 3D scene can be drawn at a smaller size and upscaled by FSR 3.1: FSR ULTRA Q. (4/3x: exactly 1080p of a 2x output, 1620p of 4K), QUALITY (1.5x: 1440p for a 4K output), BALANCED (1.7x) and PERFORM. (2x: 1080p for 4K) in the pause menu's PERFORMANCE › ANTI ALIASING (the render size is in the label), the launcher's FSR upscaling (`mcla_upscaling = "ultra_quality"`, `"quality"`, `"balanced"`, `"performance"`; off by default). The scene's draws go into the top-left part of the output-sized render targets, the scene's resolves are stretched back to the full size, so that everything that reads them (shadows, fog, the post-processing, the HUD) is unchanged and stays at the full resolution and sharp, and FSR is given the render size and the output size at the game's motion blur draw. `patches/README.md` (`rexglue-fsr-upscaling.patch`) has the details and the measurements.

Costs and limits:
- **GPU time** on an RTX 5080 at 3x (PresentMon's GPU busy per present, medians): D3D12 FSR native AA 9.39 ms, Quality 8.59, Performance 8.22, MSAA 8.41; Vulkan 9.44, 8.57, 8.17, 8.29. Quality saves 0.8 ms (8.5%) and Performance 1.2 ms (12.5%) against FSR at native resolution. At 2x (1440p) the card isn't GPU bound (85 to 90% busy): 6.15, 5.80, 5.64 and 5.78 ms. What stays at the output size bounds the gain: FSR's own pass (about 1.4 ms at 3x), the shadow cascades, the environment map, the resolves, the texture loads, the post-processing and the HUD. On the integrated Radeon of the test machine (2 compute units, where FSR's own pass costs about 9 ms at 720p output) the saving is larger in milliseconds, but what stays at the output size still takes most of the frame: at 2x (1440p) on D3D12 FSR native AA 204 ms, Quality 175 (-14%), Performance 147 (-28%), MSAA 153; on Vulkan 211, 174, 155, 146; at 1x (720p) on D3D12 45.2, 39.1, 36.5, MSAA 39.6. So on such a GPU upscaling brings FSR anti-aliasing down to about the cost of MSAA; it doesn't make 2x playable (about 7 FPS).
- **Look:** softer than FSR at native resolution (mean |Laplacian| of a 4K still 19% lower at Ultra Quality, 22% at Quality and 33% at Performance; a 1440p still 19% at Ultra Quality), more trails behind fast-moving cars (only five are tagged in the game), pedestrians and rain, smoke and particles, because FSR leans on the frames before to rebuild the detail; FSR SHARPNESS helps a little. The reactive mask is off while upscaling. Textures are sampled with a negative LOD bias (-log2 of the ratio) as FSR recommends.
- **Where it applies:** the 3D world in gameplay and in races, one tile, host render targets (not the rasterizer-ordered-view path, which Intel on D3D12 uses by default: the levels are then not offered), FSR available. The pause menu's and the map's worlds (three 4x MSAA tiles) are drawn at the full size, FSR doesn't run in them, and the first frame after them starts FSR's history afresh. After a change of the level FSR is made again for the new size (about 0.6 s the first time) and the scene is shown unscaled and without anti-aliasing meanwhile.
- **If it can't:** a scene resolve the stretch can't do (the game doesn't make one, as far as the runs show) makes upscaling give itself up for the session, logged once, and the game plays at FSR native resolution; the pause menu then offers only FSR 3.1 NATIVE AA.
- **Not squeezed yet:** the shadow cascades and the environment map (the biggest second tier, 1.8 ms by day at 3x), which the same machinery could do.
- **Not checked:** the garage, replays, cutscenes, a photo mode, close-ups of burnout smoke at the upscaled levels, long drives for ghosting of traffic and pedestrians (only stills and a few seconds of driving were looked at), 1x output on the integrated Radeon, and any display but the 4K one. Linux and the PS5 (not built or run there; the levels are marked UNTESTED in the menu).

## Screen scaling filter (2026-10-27)

The launcher's *Screen scaling filter* (`present_effect = "bilinear"`, `"fsr"` or `"cas"`; bilinear by default; read when the game starts, so it isn't in the pause menu) chooses how the finished picture (the scene, the HUD and the menus together) is scaled to the window or screen when its size differs from the game's resolution: Native (720p) on a 1080p, 1440p or 4K screen is enlarged, 2x on a 1080p screen is shrunk (the SDK has no way to render at 1.5x, so a 1080p screen gets 2x or Native). The SDK's presenter always had the filters, but this fork's build compiled them out (only `bilinear` was allowed); `rexglue-fsr-upscaling.patch` builds them in on Windows. `patches/README.md` has what each does, from the code.

Costs and limits:
- **Enlarging:** bilinear is a plain smooth stretch, the softest. FSR 1 rebuilds edges with EASU (steps of at most 2x) and sharpens with RCAS (`present_fsr_sharpness_reduction`, 0.2 stops by default). CAS sharpens and enlarges by up to 2x in one pass, the rest with a bilinear stretch (`present_cas_additional_sharpness`, 0 by default). Both run over the whole picture, the HUD's text included.
- **Shrinking (2x on a 1080p screen):** FSR 1 has nothing to enlarge and takes the CAS path, so FSR 1 and CAS are the same single pass, a sharpening resample, while bilinear just shrinks the picture. At the same size bilinear changes nothing and FSR 1 and CAS only sharpen.
- **Not with HDR or frame generation:** HDR output and frame generation always scale bilinearly (the filters are made for SDR values; the frame generation pacer paints in the present callback, which has no intermediate passes).
- **Windows only:** Linux and the PS5 keep bilinear (not built there). `fsr2` and `fsr3`, the SDK's experimental temporal paths, are not offered: they need FidelityFX's runtime linked into the presenter.
- **Checked** on an RTX 5080 with both APIs, enlarging the 1x and 2x pictures to a 2880x1620 window: FSR 1 is clearly the sharpest, CAS a little sharper than bilinear, with no artefacts seen (`patches/README.md` has the numbers). Shrinking (2x on a 1080p screen) and the GPU time weren't measured.

## Frame generation in the settings (2026-10-24)

AMD FSR 3.1 frame generation, a generated frame between each two the game renders (DISPLAY › FRAME GENERATION in the pause menu, the launcher's Frame generation; `frame_generation = "fsr"`; off by default), with a frame-rate cap of its own while it is on (`mcla_fps_frame_generation`: Half display by default, FPS TARGET in the pause menu). How it works and what was measured: `patches/README.md` (rexglue-frame-generation.patch).

Costs and limits:
- **Switching during play** works on both graphics APIs and costs one longer frame: the largest gap between presents within 1.5 s of a switch (240 Hz) was 33 to 54 ms turning it on and 17 ms off on Direct3D 12, 9 to 20 ms on and 9 to 16 ms off on Vulkan.
- **Under 100 Hz** there is no Half display (half would be under 50 rendered, choppy between the generated frames): FPS TARGET keeps the player's own caps, shown as `N (2N SHOWN)` with frame generation on, and the row's launcher tip says it wants 120 Hz or more, or G-Sync or FreeSync. Fixed-refresh displays are untested.
- **Half display follows the display the window is on** (the refresh rate Windows reports for it, read at start and every two seconds; a variable-refresh display's highest). Where it isn't known (Linux and the PS5 don't read it yet) the player's own cap stays.
- **A cap above Half display adds latency** (22.6 ms from swap to screen uncapped at 240 Hz native against 6.3 ms at 116); the menu and the launcher allow it.
- **Where it can't run** the row says why in the menu (`OFF (NEEDS VULKAN 1.3)`, `OFF (NO FIDELITYFX RUNTIME)`, `OFF (FAILED)`, ...) and can't be turned on; the launcher offers FSR 3.1 only where the FidelityFX runtime for the graphics API was built. A generation that fails while playing is remembered for that session only: `mcla.toml` keeps `frame_generation = "fsr"`, so the next start tries again.
- **Linux and the PS5** offer the row (FSR 3.1 labelled UNTESTED, off by default; the PS5's reads FSR 3.1 (NEXT START) while the device was made without FSR's features, without UNTESTED: a longer value is clipped by the row, as the 30-character one was in a screenshot on Windows). The Linux build looks for `libamd_fidelityfx_vk.so` next to the executable and loads it before offering the row (`NO FIDELITYFX RUNTIME`, `RUNTIME NOT LOADED`); the PS5's title checks that FidelityFX is linked. The runtime has frame generation's components on both since `rexglue-frame-generation-nonwindows.patch`, and the PS5 presents the generated and the real frame back to back on its fixed-refresh display (FIFO spaces them; `frame_generation_pacing = timed` gives the PC pacing back). The PS5's device gets FSR's features only when the game starts with frame generation (or FSR anti-aliasing) chosen, so there it takes effect at the next start (NEXT START). **Built on neither, run on neither**: the Windows static-link test of FSR and frame generation passes, and the Linux and PS5 code that differs from Windows' compiled in a syntax check with the platform forced (`patches/README.md` says which files). The refresh rate of the display is read on Windows only, so Half display is not offered on Linux or the PS5 (the PS5's 59.94 Hz is under 100 Hz anyway) and the player's own cap applies.

## NVIDIA Reflex and AMD Anti-Lag 2 (2026-10-24)

The GPU makers' latency reducers: the driver holds the game back at the start of each frame so that no frames queue in front of the GPU (DISPLAY › NVIDIA REFLEX or AMD ANTI LAG 2 in the pause menu, the launcher's setting of the same name; `low_latency` = `auto` | `off` | `on` | `boost`). **On by default for NVIDIA Reflex**, off for AMD Anti-Lag 2 (untested), and on whenever frame generation is on. How it works and what was measured: `patches/README.md` (rexglue-low-latency.patch).

Costs and limits:
- **A frame-rate cost on Direct3D 12 at 3x** (RTX 5080, uncapped): 3 to 5% with On (2.7%, 4.0% and 4.8% in three runs) and about 6% with On + Boost, for 13.6 ms less from swap to screen and 22.5 ms less from input to screen. On Vulkan about 1%. At native resolution none on either (the GPU has room and the reducer changes nothing). It is on by default anyway (the user's decision: the latency is worth the frames); `low_latency = "off"` in `mcla.toml`, or the setting, turns it off.
- **With frame generation on Direct3D 12 it does nothing** (23.50 against 23.60 ms from swap to screen at 3x; the same with the Half display cap): AMD's frame interpolation swap chain presents from a queue and thread of its own, and the driver never sees a frame waiting for the GPU through it. Four ways of marking its presents were tried, none worked, and its queue can't be reached to declare it out of band. It is forced on there anyway (it costs nothing); `low_latency_frame_generation = "off"` in `mcla.toml` turns that off.
- **With frame generation on Vulkan at 3x it works, and the pacing gets worse** (swap to screen 31.0 to 18.6 ms, input to screen 45.7 to 26.7 ms, for 2.7% of the rendered frames): the sigma of the step against the display interval goes from 0.50 to 1.05 ms, the presents' spacing (p1 to p99) from 5.1 to 6.2 ms to 4.7 to 7.8 ms, and the 95th percentile of the swap to screen is 32 ms. At the Half display cap (the default) it changes nothing (the GPU has room). `low_latency_frame_generation = "off"` takes the forced reducer off.
- **The Vulkan latency gap** (3x with frame generation, uncapped: 31.0 ms from swap to screen on Vulkan, 23.5 ms on Direct3D 12) is a queue of about two thirds of a rendered frame, not the pacer's timing: at the Half display cap the two are the same (6.4 and 6.3 ms). Reflex takes it away.
- **Who holds the cap.** With the reducer on (and frame generation off), the cap goes to the driver (`low_latency_limiter = auto`): the wait comes before the game reads its input, so a 60 FPS cap shows the input 8.2 ms newer (45 FPS: 13.6 ms), at the same rate and presents' spacing and with a more even step than the game's own limiter at 60 and 120 FPS (the sigma of the step against the display interval 0.42 against 0.66 ms at 60 on Direct3D 12; equal within the noise at 45). Its wait is before the engine timer reads the time, so the simulation step measures the frames' starts after the wait (the shake of a limiter at the timer, above, is the step measured before it; measured worse in all 10 comparisons). With frame generation on, the game's own limiter stays (the driver's was 2.8 ms worse on D3D12 and 2.0 ms on Vulkan at the Half display cap). Only where the reducer has been tested (NVIDIA). Where the driver can't do it (no swapchain yet, a refused call) the game's own limiter holds the cap.
- **The driver's own timing report on Vulkan** is shifted by about 5 ms against PresentMon's (its clock domain), so it is not used for numbers.
- **Needs** a GeForce driver with Reflex (Direct3D 12: `nvapi64.dll`) or with `VK_NV_low_latency2` (Vulkan); otherwise the row says `NO NVAPI` / `NEEDS A NEWER DRIVER` (the full list of reasons is in `patches/README.md`).
- **When it stops working** the row reads OFF (reason) and the game's own limiter holds the cap: the driver refusing the mode three times (a second apart), a Vulkan swapchain made without the latency mode, or the driver's wait before a frame taking over a quarter of a second (four of the cap's intervals if longer) three times among the last eight (`SLEEP TOO LONG`; Direct3D 12's sleep can't be cancelled, so one that never returns is not covered, as Vulkan's bounded wait is). The driver is told to leave the mode then. The last one is tried again after 5 s, then 10, 20, 40 and at most every 60 s while it keeps failing, so a hitch or a window that isn't presenting costs the setting seconds, not the session; none happened when the window was minimised, hidden or covered for 15 s on either API, here. Frame ids are paired by counting the swap packets written and the frames started; a swap with no frame and then two frames (S T T S: once in 1,800 frames in a 60 FPS run) leaves one pending frame, which is written off, so the ids never stay shifted (at once where frames normally don't wait, after half a second of waiting at every pass where the update thread runs a frame ahead of the swaps, as it does uncapped at native resolution; `low_latency_debug` 16 counts it).
- **Untested:** AMD Anti-Lag 2 on both graphics APIs (no Radeon: written from AMD's header and documentation; the row says UNTESTED, and it is off by default there); Linux and the PS5 (the Vulkan paths are built in and labelled UNTESTED; neither was built or run; the PS5's Vulkan device asks for the extensions only when the game starts with the reducer asked for).
- **A DLL that is there but doesn't load** (FSR's runtimes, a missing import) is now reported unavailable at start, for FSR and for frame generation, with `RUNTIME NOT LOADED` in the menu, instead of failing at the first frame; the check loads the DLL from its own folder and the system folder, which costs a few ms at start. The FidelityFX DLLs are built with the C runtime linked in (and the build warns if one of them imports it again), so a PC needs no Visual C++ runtime for them.

## A lattice of dots on opaque geometry: alpha to mask and an alpha of 0.99999994 (fixed 2026-10-27)

Fork issue #8 ("white texture/block issues", on an RTX 2060 at 1600x900; it was closed as "an emulation artefact", and the owner says it is all over the game): a fine grid of dots, one pixel in every 2x2 quad, on the posts of the bus shelter in the story's first cutscene (the dark shelter looks like a dotted grid against the building behind it), on a lamp pole, a palm trunk and bushes in front of a lit shop window (inside the window's rectangle only, where something bright is behind); the owner also sees it on the title screen's palms and their shadows. An earlier note in this file called "the same dithered pattern on thin geometry" normal for the game. Part of it is: the game dithers foliage and the distant trees' billboards on purpose (alpha to mask with offsets 3, 1, 0, 2). The lattice on opaque things is not.

What it is: the pixel of each quad whose dither offset is 0 needs alpha of exactly 1.0 for its second 2x MSAA sample (the thresholds are 0.5 - offset / 8 and 1 - offset / 8), and the alpha the shaders compute for opaque world geometry comes out 1 - epsilon on the host's GPU, so that pixel loses a sample. `patches/rexglue-alpha-to-mask-tolerance.patch` subtracts a tolerance (`alpha_to_mask_tolerance`, 1/512 by default, half a step of 8-bit alpha) from the thresholds in both translators. The evidence, measured at the dusk spawn of free roam on D3D12 and Vulkan (RTX 5080) and by the night spawn, and the Xbox 360 footage that shows none of it, are in that patch's section and in `launcher/dev/dither-2026-10-27.md`. Turn it off with `--alpha_to_mask_tolerance=0` (next start).

- **What the fix changes:** the dots on the pole, the trunk and the posts are gone; the dithered edges of bushes, the foliage and the billboards (alpha a fraction, not 1 - epsilon) are as they were.
- **Not known:** whether the owner's pictures are the same thing in every scene (they were reproduced as the same pattern, in the same place for the window, and by the same cause in the dusk and night spawns; the cutscene and the title screen's palm scene were not reproduced here). AMD and Intel GPUs were not tried: the cause is the host's float rounding, which may differ there. The PS5 build has the same SDK code and was not run. The two translators number the pixels of a quad differently (D3D12 and Vulkan put the same dither on different pixels); that was not changed and not checked against the console.

## Radio: the XMA decoder lost frames in a long stream (fixed 2026-10-27)

Fork issue #2 (the radio's balance "goes wild" after a couple of seconds, "as in Xenia") and the radio part of #7 (songs out of step, static). The cause was found in the SDK's XMA decoder: the game plays a stereo song as two mono XMA contexts, one per channel, and the decoder dropped a 512-sample frame in each channel on its own schedule (one every 10 to 20 s), so the two channels drifted apart by whole frames (11 to 35 ms): the stereo image wandered, the channels combed against each other, a click at each loss, and the song ran ahead of its time. `rexglue-xma-decoder-fixes.patch` carries the fixes Xenia Canary and Edge made to the same decoder (`xma_canary_fixes`, on by default; `--xma_canary_fixes=false` restores the SDK's behaviour); its section in `patches/README.md` has the credits and `launcher/dev/audio-2026-10-27.md` the runs.

- **Measured** against FFmpeg's decode of the same input, on a 16-thread PC: the SDK's decoder lost 6 to 11 frames in the first 95 s of the radio in every one of 11 runs (50 to 100 % of the blocks off, left/right correlation about 0), the fixed one 0 in 5 runs (0 to 0.2 % off, correlation 0.76 to 0.95), sample for sample FFmpeg's; at 4 logical processors 7 frames lost against 0 (85.5 % of the blocks off against 0.0 %).
- **No audio underruns with the defaults** after the game's first second of audio, at 16 or at 4 logical processors; the output device's 50 silent frames in the first 0.3 s of every run are the device starting before the game's mixer has made a frame.
- **The other audio switches are off** (`xma_race_fixes`, `audio_thread_priority`, `audio_client_lock`, `audio_adaptive_queue`, the statistics and dumps): they changed nothing that could be measured on the test PC.
- **`mcla_pump_sleep_max_ms` (the cap on the game's frame-pacing Sleep) stays -1**: at 4 logical processors it caused 504 to 859 steady underruns (silences of up to 158 frames). `mcla_pump_wake_on_demand` is the form of it meant for play, and the game turns it on by itself with 6 or more logical processors (below): the thread comes round early only when the main thread is waiting for it, and a steady underrun switches it off for the session. At 4 logical processors it is off: one run in three had 2 steady underruns.
- **Not heard**: nobody has listened to the result, and the reports' other symptoms (reverb and static on voices in cutscenes) were not reproduced: the route does not reach a cutscene, and a CPU slower than the test PC's was not tested.
- **Untested**: the PS5 and Linux (the decoder fix is platform neutral code; the MMCSS call is Windows only).

## The uncapped frame rate and the timer thread's core, by processor count (2026-10-27)

Two CPU options whose best setting depends on how many logical processors the game may run on. At the start the game counts them (`MclaApp::ApplyPcDefaults` > `ApplyCpuDefaults`, `src/mcla_app.cpp`, Windows only): the bits of the process's affinity mask, which includes a job object's limit, or, when the mask is the whole processor group, every group's processors (a machine with more than 64). The rule, as the options' defaults (`rex::cvar::SetDefaultValue`, like the other PC defaults: a value from the command line, an environment variable or `mcla.toml` wins, and none is written to the file):
- **6 or more: `mcla_pump_wake_on_demand` on**, `timer_queue_hr_wait` off. The game held the uncapped frame rate to 143 to 145 FPS with the 7 ms Sleep of the thread that frees the command ring's slots (section "Radio" mentions the cap of it that starved the audio). With the thread woken when the main thread waits for it, the city runs at 217 to 240 FPS uncapped at 16, 8 and 6 logical processors, the caps unchanged and no audio underrun.
- **5 or fewer: `timer_queue_hr_wait` on**, `mcla_pump_wake_on_demand` off. The timer thread no longer keeps a core busy (the llvm-mingw builds' condition variable cuts its wait to whole milliseconds, and a timer is always due within 1 ms). At 4 logical processors the process uses 23 to 58% less CPU, and the long frames and the late audio wake-ups that came from the missing core go: uncapped +17 to 21% FPS, p99 27 and 30 ms to 10 and 12 ms, the 120 and 60 caps held.
- The log has one line, e.g. `CPU: 4 logical processors: timer queue high-resolution wait on, pump wake on demand off (automatic; 5 or fewer)`, with `(set by the player)` after a value that came from the player. `--mcla_pump_wake_on_demand=false` and `--timer_queue_hr_wait=false` (or `=true`) override it; `mcla_pump_sleep_max_ms` is not part of it.
- **The line is at 6** because each option fails on the other side of it: wake on demand at 4 gained only 11 to 16% and had a steady underrun in one run of three and a 19% longer p99 at the 120 cap; the high-resolution wait at 6 left the frame 1.7% longer when driving (the rule allows 1%) for 31 to 50% less CPU, and at 16 and 8 it made the frame 1 to 2.4% longer. 5 logical processors was not measured; it follows the 4.
- **Linux and the PS5 keep both off**: the high-resolution wait is a Windows wait, the PS5 sets its own defaults (`ps5/game/main_ps5.cpp`) and the wake-on-demand code was neither built nor run there (it is the same code with a condition variable in place of the event).
- Both options are read where they are used (the timer thread at every wait, the pump thread at every round), so the default set at the start is in effect for the whole run.

Measured (A/B, 3 runs a side after a warm-up, D3D12, native, the city route of `launcher/dev/scripts/wake-ab.pad`, uncapped and at 120, 60 and 30 FPS caps, the game limited to 16, 8, 6 and 4 logical processors, exclusive lock, display awake; `launcher/dev/profiling-2026-10-26.md` has the tables): wake on demand passes every check at 16, 8 and 6 and fails at 4; the high-resolution wait passes at 4 and fails the frame-time rule at 6 (and at 16 and 8, earlier). The defaults themselves were checked in the game at 16 and at 4 logical processors: the log line says what is above, and `--mcla_pump_wake_on_demand=false` (at 16) and `--timer_queue_hr_wait=false` (at 4) win and are marked "set by the player"; at 4 the automatic default repeated the measured result (+19% and +22% FPS uncapped, p99 -65% and -60%, the 120 cap held).

**Not verified**:
- The processors are the test PC's (a Ryzen 7 9800X3D) limited by affinity: the 4-logical runs are the first four logical processors of a fast CPU with its large cache, not a slower 4-thread CPU, and none of the counts was measured on another CPU. 5, 7, 10, 12 and the counts above 16 were not measured; 12 and more follow the 6 or more case.
- The audio with the high-resolution wait at 5 or fewer processors: no steady underrun in the 6 runs at 4 and 6 logical processors, but nobody listened to it.
- Both options together (nothing turns them on together by default).
- The PS5 and Linux.

## HDR output (2026-10-16)

The game in HDR on a display in HDR mode: its HUD, menus, movies and legal screens at the SDR level (as Windows shows SDR on an HDR desktop), and its scene from its own tone map's inputs: the same as in SDR up to 60% of the game's white, never darker than SDR from there to its white, and brighter above it. Settings: the launcher's *HDR*, *HDR peak brightness*, *HDR paper white* and *HDR highlights*, the pause menu's DISPLAY › HDR, PEAK BRIGHTNESS, PAPER WHITE and HIGHLIGHTS, and `hdr`, `hdr_peak_nits`, `hdr_paper_white_nits`, `mcla_hdr_highlights` in `mcla.toml`; all change during play. On Windows `hdr` is `auto` by default (`MclaApp::ApplyPcDefaults`: HDR whenever Windows has HDR on for the display, SDR otherwise; a value in `mcla.toml` or on the command line wins); the SDK's own default stays `off`, for other titles, and Linux and the PS5 keep `off` (the display's HDR state isn't read there yet). The look was signed off by the user on an ASUS ROG Swift PG32UCDP (WOLED, Gaming HDR mode, Uniform Brightness on), 2026-10-16: with *Auto* peak and paper white and *Highlights* at Brighter, which is the default since. Peak brightness goes from 200 to 4,000 nits in steps of 50 and paper white from 80 to 480 in steps of 10 (the range of Windows' SDR content brightness), AUTO first: sliders with an *Auto* box in the launcher, rows in the pause menu where holding left or right goes faster (the game repeats a held direction every 6 frames or so, so the menu steps once per press, again after 0.4 s, and faster from 1.2 and 2.2 s, at about the same speed at any frame rate: 200 to 4,000 nits in about 3 s at 150 and at 30 FPS). The peak is never set below paper white: a step that would cross it stops at the last one that doesn't, and the other value isn't moved. Values typed into `mcla.toml` between the steps are kept and shown as they are (537 NITS), and a step goes from them to the next step that way (550). HDR's row says "(NOT AVAILABLE)" whenever the display says it isn't in HDR mode, read when DISPLAY opens (also with `hdr` off, which otherwise doesn't read the display) and after that while it's open.

How it works (`launcher/dev/hdr-2026-10-16.md` has the details and the measurements; `patches/README.md` the SDK patches):
- **Markers.** The game writes NOP markers into its GPU command stream where its 3D scene ends, its tone map and its UI start (`src/render_markers.cpp`, `mcla_render_markers`, on). They draw nothing: screenshots with them on and off are identical. Past the command buffer's limit a marker makes room the way the game's own writers do.
- **Output.** HDR10 (PQ, BT.2020), on both APIs. The game's 8-bit colour still goes through its display table, so DISPLAY GAMMA means the same in HDR. Peak and paper white default to what Windows reports (the HDR Calibration app's peak, the SDR content brightness slider; 1,000 and 200 nits if it reports nothing usable), read about once a second while HDR isn't off; above 0.75 of the peak, light rolls off towards it. The scene's shoulder tops out at the peak in the game's units, and with that roll-off its brightest highlights reach about 90% of the peak setting; bloom and contrast take some past that, towards the peak.
- **UI.** The HUD and menus are separated from the scene as they are drawn, and the game's own 8-bit frame is put back for what it reads; the UI is then blended over the scene as the game blends it. Every scene reached took this path: legal screens and logos (as SDR), the intro and attract movies, title, map zoom-out, play, pause and GPS map with their fades, race menus, loading, intro, countdown, race, race pause, replay confirmation and replay, the race-over screen.
- **Scene.** After the game's tone map draw, its operator runs again on the GPU from the same inputs, with a shoulder from 60% of the game's white towards the peak instead of the clip, never under the game's own curve up to its white; the light glows the game adds after it at night come over too. *Highlights* (Normal, Bright, Brighter, Brightest; Brighter by default) scales how fast the shoulder rises and the glows' gain: at dusk the brightest pixel goes from 785 to 1209 nits on the test display, with the midtones unchanged. The tone map draw is recognised by its shaders, its target, its coverage of the whole output and the texture settings the pass reads; a frame where it isn't keeps the 8-bit scene, and every change is logged with the reason.

The second review (2026-10-16, phases 3 and 4) brought: the shoulder never under the game's curve near its white (at Normal it had been 5 to 16% darker there, depending on the peak; Brighter is unchanged: in the same paused dusk frame at peak 1317 and paper white 280 the brightest pixel went from 1086 to 1125 nits at Normal and stayed at 1209 at Brighter); the checks on the tone map draw; the glows' gain only for light added after the tone map; HDR's "(NOT AVAILABLE)" kept current; the markers keeping the host's floating-point mode when they make room; the textures released with HDR off; guards against NaN (`launcher/dev/hdr-2026-10-16.md`, "Second review").

Costs and limits:
- **GPU time:** about 0.5 ms a frame at 3x (0.51 on D3D12 and 0.51 on Vulkan since the second review's fixes, the scene pass 0.20 of it), against 0.04 ms for SDR's gamma pass.
- **Video memory:** about 200 MB of textures at 3x while HDR is on; they're released a couple of seconds after it's turned off, and made again when it comes back.
- **The first time HDR is on,** a few new UI pipelines compile; in the tests no draw was left out for them.
- **Screenshots** (the dev pad's, captures) are SDR: highlights are clipped, and the roll-off is not undone.
- **HDR10 only;** no scRGB output yet.
- **Not reached in the tests:** the garage, photo mode and cutscenes. Photo-mode freeze frames would show as SDR (they don't go through the tone map). A UI drawn with a blend this doesn't know falls back for that frame (logged once): the UI is told from the scene by comparing with the picture under it, or the frame is shown as SDR.
- **With HDR off** nothing of this runs except the markers and a check per draw; a few small pipelines are created at start-up, and pipelines made for HDR in an earlier session are recreated from the pipeline storage. The picture is unchanged: every steady boot frame is byte-identical to the build without it.

**Not verified**: other HDR displays than the one the look was signed off on; switching Windows HDR on and off while the game runs; other GPUs; Linux (HDR10 on Wayland is planned; the presenter uses HDR10 where the surface offers it, but reads the display's levels only on Windows); the PS5, whose build applies the SDK patches but hasn't been compiled with them, and keeps SDR (no HDR rows in its menu).

## The F4 settings overlay halved the game's frame rate while it was open (fixed 2026-10-17)

While the SDK's F4 settings overlay or console was open, the UI thread presented at the monitor's rate (240 a second on a 240 Hz display). The game itself fell from about 146 to 60 to 77 frames a second with F4 open, and to 60 to 113 with the console (Windows, D3D12 and Vulkan, native, uncapped, free roam; PresentMon and the presenter's log). It is the mechanism the achievement pop-ups had before `src/achievement_toast.h`: a dialog that repaints continuously keeps the UI thread painting, on the queue the GPU command thread submits to.

`patches/rexglue-presenter-ui-overlay.patch` now draws the SDK's UI into a layer that is put over each game frame, so every frame is presented once with the overlays open (146 a second, both APIs; figures there).
- **The overlays now refresh at the game's frame rate:** 30 a second at the launcher's default cap, instead of the display's. The mouse pointer stays smooth.
- **The previous way of painting comes back** when no game frame comes for 250 ms with an overlay open (a hang, the time before the first frame), until frames have come steadily for 250 ms again.
- **Not checked on the PS5 or Linux:** it applies only where presenting doesn't wait for vertical sync (on Vulkan, the IMMEDIATE or MAILBOX present mode); with FIFO alone the previous way is kept.
- **`--present_ui_overlay=false`** restores the previous way.

On Vulkan the UI thread's paints also waited for the GPU every time, about two game frames each, so only about every other guest frame was shown; `rexglue-vulkan-paint-pipeline-format.patch` (2026-10-17) fixes that part. With it, every guest frame is presented while the overlay is open (`display-switches.pad` with validation, Vulkan IMMEDIATE and FIFO: all refreshes presented in the overlay steps); the game's own rate still falls, as on D3D12.

## The game ran on the integrated graphics on PCs with two GPUs (fixed 2026-10-27)

Fork issue #1 (an RTX 4060 user, and a second user, at 3 to 6 FPS right after the Rockstar logos) and #15 (a laptop with an i5-7300, a GTX 1050 4 GB and 8 GB of RAM: choppy, with audio hiccups) are **likely cases of this, not confirmed**: neither report came with a log, and no PC here lists an integrated GPU first, so the symptom itself was not reproduced. What was read in the code: the SDK's Direct3D 12 provider (`src/ui/d3d12/d3d12_provider.cpp`) takes the first adapter DXGI lists that isn't software while `d3d12_adapter` is -1, its Vulkan provider takes the loader's first device while `vulkan_device` is -1, and the fork's *Automatic* graphics API (`DecideAutomaticBackend`) and the launcher looked at that same first adapter, so on an Intel + NVIDIA laptop it would even have chosen Vulkan on the Intel GPU. On a laptop with switchable graphics Windows lists the GPU that drives the screen first, which is the integrated one unless Windows' graphics settings say otherwise for the program. A 4-thread i5-7300 with its HD Graphics 630 and a GTX 1050 beside it is that layout; an RTX 4060 laptop with an iGPU is another.

What the game does now (Windows only; `src/gpu_choice.h` is the rule, `src/mcla_app.cpp` `ChooseGpu` applies it, the launcher's `DisplayDrivers.ChooseGpu` in `ShaderStamp.cs` repeats it, and the two must agree):

1. A `d3d12_adapter` you set (the command line, an environment variable or `mcla.toml`) wins, with the SDK's own meaning: a number is that adapter in the order DXGI lists them (`--d3d12_adapter=1` is the same GPU as before), -2 the software adapter, another negative number the first that isn't software.
2. `mcla_prefer_dedicated_gpu=false` takes the first adapter that isn't software, as before.
3. Otherwise Windows' own preference for `mcla.exe` is honoured: the value named by the exe's full path in `HKCU\Software\Microsoft\DirectX\UserGpuPreferences`, which *Settings › System › Display › Graphics* writes (data such as `GpuPreference=2;`; 1 *Power saving*, 2 *High performance*; the data may also hold other items, such as `AppStatus=0;`). Power saving takes the first GPU of `IDXGIFactory6::EnumAdapterByGpuPreference`'s minimum-power order, high performance the first of its high-performance order.
4. With no entry, or *Let Windows decide* (0), the high-performance GPU is taken.

An adapter counts only if it isn't software and Direct3D 12 can make a device on it at feature level 11_0 (what the SDK requires); without `IDXGIFactory6` (Windows 10 before 1803) the first such adapter is taken, as the SDK would. The adapter's number in DXGI's own order becomes `d3d12_adapter`'s default (`SetDefaultValue`: never written to `mcla.toml`), so the SDK takes exactly it. The *Automatic* API decides on that GPU: an Intel + NVIDIA laptop gets Direct3D 12 on the NVIDIA GPU, an RDNA iGPU + NVIDIA PC too, Intel-only and RDNA-iGPU-only PCs get Vulkan as before. And whenever the API ends up Vulkan, chosen by *Automatic* or by the player, `vulkan_device` defaults to the Vulkan device with the same LUID (else vendor and device id) as the chosen GPU; before, only *Automatic*'s Vulkan did that and a Vulkan chosen by hand took the loader's first device. `mcla.exe` also exports `NvOptimusEnablement` and `AmdPowerXpressRequestHighPerformance` (both 1), which NVIDIA's and AMD's drivers read to run a program on the dedicated GPU: a belt-and-braces measure for whatever path the choice above does not cover. They are fixed in the exe and `mcla_prefer_dedicated_gpu` does not turn them off.

**Where to look:** the log (`--log_file=run.log --log_level=info`, or the launcher's log) has, at every start, one line per adapter (`GPU:   [0] 'Intel(R) HD Graphics 630' vendor 0x8086 device 0x5912, 128 MiB dedicated video memory`; the numbers are `d3d12_adapter`'s), Windows' two rankings, the exe's entry in Windows' graphics settings, and the choice with its reason: `GPU: 'NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050' (the high-performance GPU; Windows lists 'Intel(R) HD Graphics 630' first)`, or `(Windows' graphics settings ask for power saving for this app)`, or `(d3d12_adapter = 1 set by the user)`. The SDK's own `DXGI adapter:` or `Vulkan device '...'` line, a few lines further, is the GPU actually used. That is what to ask for when someone reports the game as slow.

**Checked:** the rule with made-up PCs (an Intel iGPU listed first and a GeForce second; an RDNA iGPU and a GeForce; one GPU; WARP only and `d3d12_adapter = -2`; Windows' entry 0, 1 and 2; an explicit `d3d12_adapter`; the off switch; no `IDXGIFactory6`; a ranked GPU that can't make a Direct3D 12 device): 41 rows, and 18 more for the parsing of the registry data, the same tables in C++ (`launcher/dev/api-rule/gpu_choice_test.cpp`) and in the launcher's C# (`apitest`), 0 failures. The launcher's choice on this PC's adapters (the RTX 5080 listed first, the Ryzen's Radeon second, WARP third), with the registry value written for a scratch copy of `mcla.exe` only and removed after each case (the key exactly as before): no entry, 0 or 2 → the RTX 5080, Direct3D 12; 1 → the Radeon, Vulkan; `mcla_prefer_dedicated_gpu=false` → the RTX 5080; `d3d12_adapter` 0, 1, 2, 3, -2 → the RTX 5080, the Radeon (Vulkan), WARP, no adapter, WARP. The exports are in the built `mcla.exe` (`llvm-objdump -p`: ordinals 1 and 2, both variables 1 in the file). CI (host code against the SDK, launcher, pins and scripts) passes on the branch. The game's own starts on this PC (21 starts of a scratch `mcla.exe`, the registry value written for that exe only and the key exactly as before after each; `launcher/dev/gpu-choice-2026-10-27.md` has the log lines): no entry, 0 or 2 → the RTX 5080, Automatic Direct3D 12; 1 → the Radeon, Automatic Vulkan on Vulkan device 0, the Radeon; `--mcla_gpu_backend=vulkan` → Vulkan device 0, the RTX 5080 (with 1: the Radeon); `--mcla_gpu_backend=d3d12` with 1 → Direct3D 12 on the Radeon; `--d3d12_adapter=1`, also with 2 → the Radeon ("set by the user"); `--vulkan_device=0` with 1 → kept ("set by the user"); `mcla_prefer_dedicated_gpu=false` → Windows' first adapter. Booted to free roam: the RTX 5080 (Direct3D 12, 144 FPS), the Radeon by Windows' setting (Vulkan, 29 FPS), Vulkan on the RTX 5080. **Windows' setting does reorder DXGI's list for the old code:** with `GpuPreference=1;` the build before this change (and this one with the off switch) found the Radeon at `EnumAdapters1` index 0 and ran on it, and with 2 the RTX 5080.

**Not verified:** a laptop where Windows lists the integrated GPU first (only the made-up PCs above cover it); what the two exported variables change on a real Optimus or PowerXpress laptop; Windows 10 before 1803 (no `IDXGIFactory6`); the launcher's shader check does not notice a switch between two adapters that both stay installed and use the same API (its API check does notice *Automatic* leaving Vulkan on an Intel GPU for Direct3D 12 on an NVIDIA one).

**Workaround for v0.1.3 and earlier:** in Windows' *Settings › System › Display › Graphics*, add `mcla.exe` of the install folder (`out\build\win-amd64-release`) and set *Options › High performance* (Windows then lists that GPU first, and the old code takes the first; checked the other way round on this PC, above); or put `d3d12_adapter = N` in `mcla.toml` (N counts from 0 in the order Windows lists the adapters; the log's `DXGI adapter:` line names the one used), and with *Graphics API* on Vulkan `vulkan_device = N` too (Vulkan's own numbering, the `Available Vulkan physical devices` list in the log).

Linux and the PS5 are not touched: the option, the registry read and the exports exist only in the Windows build, and the PS5 host does not use `mcla_app.cpp`.

## Custom vinyls missing on cars outside the vinyl editor (issues #11 and #14; fixed 2026-10-27 for the arcade race's opponents)

Reports: vinyls made in the editor show only inside it and are gone when you leave (#11, as in Xenia), and the cars of the story and the NPCs lack their own factory vinyls (#14). The cars' vinyls are drawn by the game into a render target and read by the game's own code on the CPU; the SDK keeps a resolved render target on the GPU unless `readback_resolve` copies it into guest memory, so the CPU baked an empty livery. `readback_resolve=full` fixes it (as in Xenia Canary) but waits for the GPU after every resolve of every frame.

`patches/rexglue-resolve-readback-rules.patch` adds `readback_resolve_rules`, which reads back only the resolves a rule matches, and a resolve log to find the right one. In the arcade race *Sunset and Vine* with 7 opponents the log shows what the race adds to the city and the menus: a 512x512 `8_8_8_8` render target at guest address `0x050A0000` (1 MB) with its whole mip chain to `0x051F7000`, resolved 113 times in the UI phase while the cars of the race load (guest frames 2523 to 2810), and nothing else of that size in 15,707 city and menu resolves. The game's default (`mcla_vinyl_readback`, on) is the rule `len=1048576,pitch=512,height=512,fmt=6;addr=0x051A0000-0x051F7FFF`: the base level by its class (it still matches if the address moves) and the mip levels by address.
- **Checked** (RTX 5080, the dev pad, the same race with `readback_resolve=none`, the rule alone, the whole chain by address and `full` on D3D12; none, the whole chain by address and the shipped rule on Vulkan): with none the green opponent is plain; with the rule, with the chain by address and with `full` it carries its white and green livery (the 6, PIRELLI, WORK, HKS), on both APIs. A rule on the next-largest new class (`len=200704,pitch=224,height=220,fmt=6`, a gauge or map target in the post phase) restores nothing. In a second race of the same session (the first one quit through the pause menu's QUIT RACE, then the same menu route again; D3D12, the rule set by the pad) the cars with vinyls were drawn with them again (the opponents of a race are picked at random, so it is not the same cars).
- **Cost:** none of the 15,707 resolves logged in the city and the menus matches (no 512x512 target there), so free roam and the menus read back nothing; the race's loading took 5.3 s with the rule and without it (5.3 to 5.6 s with `none`, 5.5 to 5.8 s with `full`); 113 resolves of 1 MB are read back while the 8 cars load. A free-roam timedemo with the rule against none (display awake, exclusive lock) was not run: the GPU lock was never free for it; the log is the evidence.
- **Off switch:** `--mcla_vinyl_readback=false`; a `readback_resolve_rules` of your own (`mcla.toml` or the command line) is used instead of the default.
- **Not verified:** the player's own vinyls from the editor (the garage is a place in the city: the pause menu has no entry for it, and the career save has no vinyls), the story cars of #14 (the arcade opponents carry vinyls of their own and are the same bake, but the story's cars were not seen), that the vinyls persist after a save and a load, and the PS5 (the same SDK code and the same defaults in its host; nothing ran there). The address half of the rule is the address the arcade race used; if the game allocates that render target somewhere else in another place, the base level is still read back and the small mip levels may stay stale.

## A soft smear over a car's plate and rear lights next to the speedometer, on Vulkan (issue #11; fixable, off by default, 2026-10-27)

Report: a round, soft smear over the license plate and the taillights of the player's car while the camera is turned round the car (right stick), at dusk, slow driving, Vulkan, no frame generation, any anti-aliasing; turning the game's motion blur off does not remove it.

Reproduced with the dev pad (RTX 5080, Vulkan, a car creeping at 15 mph, `stick rx` held at 0.6 to 0.8, which turns the chase camera 30 to 70 degrees, dusk and night; `launcher/dev/vinyls-2026-10-27.md`): inside a rectangle of the screen that extends about 100 pixels (at 720p) to the left of the speedometer gauge's ring, the plate's text and the rear lights are blurred; outside it they are sharp. The rectangle does not move with the car: it is the HUD's speedometer box. The same frames on D3D12 are sharp, and so are the ones with the car standing still. What it is not, by trying each at the same camera angles: the game's depth of field (`mcla_depth_of_field=false`), the full-screen blur, anti-aliasing off, occlusion queries off, `depth_float24_round`, `clear_rectangle_draws=false`, `depth_transfer_not_equal_test=false`, `direct_host_resolve=false`, `native_2x_msaa=false`, `readback_resolve=full`; the game's motion blur off makes it fainter and leaves it. What removes it: `depth_float24_convert_in_pixel_shader=true` (the SDK's option; the Vulkan `fsi` path does the same conversion and also removes it).
- **Option:** `mcla_vulkan_depth_convert` (default off): on Vulkan it sets the SDK's option as a default (an option of your own still wins). Switch on with `--mcla_vulkan_depth_convert=true` or `mcla_vulkan_depth_convert = true` in `mcla.toml`; it applies at the next start.
- **Why it is off:** it converts the depth in every pixel shader, which costs GPU time (timedemos, the drive of `drive-spawn.pad`, median frame): RTX 5080 at 3x 8.4 to 10.2 ms (+21%, two repeats each); at 1x it is CPU-bound and unchanged (6.84 and 6.86 ms); the Ryzen's Radeon iGPU at 1x 35.1 to 46.7 ms (+33%, 28.7 to 21.2 FPS, repeated). A GTX 1060 was not measured; it is not a cost to put on everyone for an artifact inside one HUD box. D3D12 is not affected, and with it on D3D12 the SDK's option fails to create some pipelines (186 errors in a run), so the game applies it to Vulkan only.
- **Not found:** which draw makes the blur, and why the conversion changes it. That the option that removes it changes how the host holds the 24-bit float depth, and that D3D12's default path (the same host depth, no conversion) shows nothing, point at the Vulkan path's handling of depth (guess, not tested). A fix without the per-pixel cost would be a change there.
- **Not verified:** the player's GTX 1060 and settings (their clip's HUD and car match the picture above: the gray-white smear is left of the speedometer ring), other drivers, the PS5 (its host applies the same default, off).

## The sun's glare did not follow the sun's view (occlusion queries; fixed 2026-10-28, written without a way to run it)

What it was: in every run of this fork's builds (the logs of the runs with the diagnostic show it at 1x and 2x, D3D12 and Vulkan), early in the session the SDK switched its host occlusion queries off and told the game a fake count of 1,000 samples. The game uses one occlusion query, for the sun (a global at `0x827E1048`, render phase `0x02000000`): it divides the count by the 1,250 samples it expects and scales the sun's glare by the result, so the glare was at 80 % whatever hid the sun. The cause is in `patches/rexglue-occlusion-queries.patch`'s section: the SDK paired the begin and the end of a query by one address, and the game's Direct3D 9 uses two (the end snapshot at the record's start, the begin snapshot 0x20 after it), so the first end of every run was "the query of another buffer". It happens at 1x and 2x, D3D12 and Vulkan.

- **Fix:** `occlusion_query_async` (default on): the two events are paired by order, the host query's count is copied without waiting for the GPU and written to the game when the GPU has it, a frame or so later; until then the game is told the record's last real count. The game reads each query two frames after it issues it (two query objects in turn) and keeps its old value while the count is missing, so the late count costs nothing; there is no stall of the CPU on the GPU.
- **Off switch:** `--occlusion_query_async=false` (the fake count again, at once, also during play). Related options: `occlusion_query_keep_submission_open`, `occlusion_query_log`.
- **What changes on screen:** not seen yet. The glare should reach full strength when the sun is fully visible (ratio 1.0 against 0.8) and fade out as buildings and trees cover the sun. `launcher/dev/occlusion-2026-10-28.md` has the chain that measures it (the sun's samples and ratio over time, the pictures, the cost) and how to read it.
- **Not verified:** all of it in a running game; that the host count in the sun's pass is the pixels of the sun's quad alone (render target transfers or clears inside the query would add samples); the cost; other users of occlusion queries in the game (the log lists every record); Linux, the PS5.
