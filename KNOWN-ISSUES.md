# Known issues of this fork's changes

Upstream (holdmysocks/mcla-recomp) no longer publishes its list of known issues and findings; its README is its only documentation. This file covers only the changes this fork makes (`FORK.md`): what each one fixes, how it was checked, and what is not verified. The SDK patches are described in `patches/README.md`, and the measurements behind them in `launcher/dev/`. Everything here was built and measured on Windows only (`FORK.md`, "Testing so far").

## Frame pacing: the limiter waits at the swap (2026-10-14)

Upstream's notes on its frame limiter (2026-10-03) left frame-to-frame jitter of about plus or minus 4 ms at 30 as an open point, with a possible cause: "the limiter waits inside the timer update, not at present". That was the cause of uneven frames at a cap. The step was measured before the wait and the frame reached the screen after a variable amount of work, so at a 60 cap frames alternated around 12 and 22 ms and moving objects shook. `mcla_pace_at_swap` (default on) moves the wait to the swap (`mcla_present_interval`): frames are held to the target where they are presented, as vsync does but with a precise clock, and each step measures one presented interval. `--mcla_pace_at_swap=false` restores the old wait.

Windows, D3D12, RTX 5080 and Ryzen 7 9800X3D: on the title screen at 60 the frame-time standard deviation went from 3.1 ms to 0.38 ms. PresentMon at 45, 50 and 60 caps: median exactly on target, about 98% of presents within 1 ms of it, no dropped frames. In play the user reported it smooth at 60 and 120.

Measured with `--vsync=false` on the command line. Another open point in those notes was whether `vsync=false` set from the app reaches the GPU plugin. It does (rechecked on 2026-10-16, free roam, native, D3D12, 120 FPS cap):
- **At start:** with neither `--vsync` on the command line nor a `vsync` line in `mcla.toml`, `ConfigureFrameTiming` turns it off and the cap holds (8.33 ms median).
- **While running:** set from the game (as the pause menu's VSYNC does), `vsync=true` held the game to 60 (15.8 ms median), and `false` gave 120 back.

With vsync on, the GPU plugin's emulated 60 Hz vblank caps the game at 60 and paces it unevenly, so it stays off unless the player turns it on. An earlier note here said that a setting from the app never reached the plugin; that was not reproduced.

## Timer resolution kept while the window is hidden (2026-10-14)

Windows 11 stops honouring `timeBeginPeriod(1)` for a process none of whose windows can be seen: minimized, fully covered, or with the display asleep. The timer then runs at 15.6 ms, every limiter sleep overshoots, and frames come out at 16 ms or more whatever the target. `MclaApp::ConfigureFrameTiming` now opts out with `SetProcessInformation(ProcessPowerThrottling, PROCESS_POWER_THROTTLING_IGNORE_TIMER_RESOLUTION)` before raising the resolution and logs whether that worked. Checked with `NtQueryTimerResolution` while the display was asleep: 15.625 ms before, 1 ms after.

## Achievement pop-ups made the presenter repaint at the monitor's rate (fixed 2026-10-14)

Overlays showed 240 FPS on a 240 Hz display while the game made 30 to 60 frames. The SDK registers its achievement toast as an ImGui dialog for the whole session, and while any dialog is registered the ImGui drawer asks for another paint after each one, so the presenter showed every game frame several times. Besides wrong overlay numbers, the extra presents went to the queue the GPU command thread submits to and spoiled frame pacing. (Upstream's README reports the Linux build presenting about 1,000 times a second on Wayland unless MAILBOX and IMMEDIATE presenting are turned off, which fits the same cause.)

`src/achievement_toast.h` wraps the SDK's toast and registers it only while an achievement is on screen, an approach ported from ForzaHorizon1-ReImagined (MIT; notice in the file). With `patches/rexglue-imgui-dialog-repaint-on-demand.patch` the toast is also drawn with the game's own frames instead of asking for repaints while it shows. Windows, D3D12, 60 cap: presents equal game frames before, during and after a toast (60 a second; 240 during a toast without the SDK patch), and 99.4 to 100% of frames within 1 ms of the target while one shows. The unlock itself is saved before the toast appears (`XGIUserWriteAchievements`, `Documents\mcla\achievements\545407F8.toml`).

## Motorcycle lean above 30 FPS (2026-10-14)

`mcBikeGyro::Update` (sub_823688B8; identified by LARecomp from the game's RTTI, its pull request #9) eases a smoothed position and velocity toward the bike's by a fixed fraction per frame: 0.40 and 0.15 when the rounded frame rate is 60 or more, doubled below that. That matches the console only at 30. At 120 the position settles about 1.3 times and the velocity 1.8 times as fast, at 45 both 1.5 times, and at a 60 cap frames that round to 59 switch to a filter two to three times as stiff. It was the one frame-rate switch in LARecomp's list with no correction here.

Two mid-asm hooks (`mcla_bike_gyro_pos_smoothing` at 0x8236895C, `mcla_bike_gyro_vel_smoothing` at 0x823689F0, where both ways through the game's test meet) replace each factor with the console's 30 FPS fraction applied by elapsed time, `1 - (1 - k30)^(dt * 30)`, as upstream's camera and chassis filters do (`mcla_camera_pos_smoothing` and the others in `src/frame_timing.cpp`, from LARecomp). Written for this project from those facts, not copied (LARecomp has no licence). `--mcla_bike_gyro_fix=false` turns it off.

Checked: the hook sites in the generated code, the constants read at run time (2.0, 0.40, 0.15), and with a temporary probe at live 30, 60, 120 and uncapped frame rates, exactly 0.2 (position) and 0.7 (velocity) remaining per 1/30 s in every case. **Not verified by riding**: the function runs only with a motorcycle in play.

## Attract movie: other shots' colours over the picture with hot pages (fixed 2026-10-14)

Reported by the user from a test run: towards the end of the attract movie (the one that plays after idling on the title screen), a flashing pinkish outline over the picture. Window captures of the whole movie, compared with `attract720.bik` decoded by FFmpeg, located it: in runs with hot pages on (`--shared_memory_hot_page_faults=4`, the PS5's setting, tried on PC while profiling) the brightness of each frame was right but its colour came from earlier shots, so red and blue blobs and pink outlines of other shots' shapes lay over the current one (a green motorcycle turned grey with red circles beside it). Without hot pages, with every other setting of this fork on, all captures matched the movie. The flash-white frames with a pinkish smudge beside the rider at the very end, and the venetian-blind wipe a few seconds before, are in the movie itself.

Cause: a texture loaded from a hot page was never reloaded while the page stayed hot, as no write fault told the texture cache it had changed (details in `patches/README.md`, `rexglue-hot-pages-watched-ranges.patch`, which fixes it). Bink writes the movie's planes into textures every frame, so the colour planes stuck on old shots. Hot pages are off by default on PC; the PS5, which runs with them, should have had the same fault.

## Mouse pointer over the game during play (2026-10-14)

Playing with a controller, the mouse pointer stayed in the middle of the picture until it was moved off the screen. The game now hides it the way PC games do. It goes as soon as a controller is used, or once the mouse has been still for about 3 seconds, and comes back as soon as the mouse moves or is clicked. It is hidden only over the game's picture while the game has focus. With another window in front, over the title bar or borders in windowed mode, or while the SDK's console, settings or achievements overlay is open, it stays. `mcla_hide_cursor` (default on; `--mcla_hide_cursor=false` keeps the pointer) selects the SDK's auto-hidden cursor, which `patches/rexglue-cursor-auto-hide.patch` makes focus-aware and lets the pads hide (details and checks there). Verified by the user with a controller on 2026-10-16: the pointer goes as soon as the controller is used. The pause menu's CONTROLS › HIDE MOUSE POINTER switches it during play.

## Vulkan on Windows (2026-10-14)

`--mcla_gpu_backend=vulkan` (or the launcher's *Graphics API*) runs everything of this fork on Vulkan: the frame pacing, the toast and cursor changes and every SDK patch that applies to Vulkan were checked there, and the launcher's settings help on Vulkan at least as much as on D3D12 (`clear_memory_page_state=false` alone took 46% off the frame time per draw at native). Two SDK changes came out of it: the Vulkan backend now counts draws and the other per-frame counters (`rexglue-perf-counters-batched.patch`), and its draws no longer look a setting up in the cvar registry each (`rexglue-vulkan-memexport-check-order.patch`, 6% less GPU command thread time per draw). The hot-page fix for the attract movie works the same on Vulkan, where the PS5 needs it.

Two more Vulkan changes followed (2026-10-15), measured in the city with the scripted controller: shared memory uploads go before the render pass instead of ending it (`rexglue-vulkan-hoist-shared-memory-uploads.patch`; with hot pages every draw's upload had ended the render pass, about 800 times a frame; free roam 19 to 27% faster), and command buffers are recorded and submitted on their own thread as on D3D12 (`rexglue-threaded-submission-vulkan.patch`). On this machine (RTX 5080) Vulkan then matched D3D12 at native resolution and was 7 to 11% slower at 3x, where its texture loads took about three times as long on the GPU (fixed since, see below); it stuttered less than D3D12 in the map zoom-out after the title until D3D12's textures were placed in shared heaps (next sections). Validation (`--vulkan_validation_enabled=true`) reports three kinds of message, all present with every change of this fork off: a pixel shader output with no attachment (warning), an occlusion query begun outside a render pass and ended inside one, and a swapchain destroyed while in use on fullscreen switches. Measurements and method: `launcher/dev/vulkan-2026-10-14.md`. **Not verified**: races and the other places that need a player.

## Loading-screen pacing: uncapped stutter and the fast intro (fixed 2026-10-14)

Two faults in `PaceUnlimitedSwap` (`src/frame_timing.cpp`), the hold that keeps loops which never reach the engine timer (the start-up legal screens and logos, loading screens) at the console's 30 per second:

- Uncapped with the intro at `normal`, about a quarter of all frames took 33 ms. The hold took any swap with no pass through the frame limiter since the previous swap for such a loop. But the game updates and presents on different threads, and uncapped the two drift against each other: in normal play about one swap in forty has no update before it and the next one has two (counted per swap with the dev pad's frame log: zero-update swaps came only singly, never two in a row, in the title, the map zoom-out and free roam). Holding each of those to 33 ms also delayed the presenting thread, which made the pattern about ten times as frequent. Free roam, native, D3D12, uncapped: median 4.6 ms but mean 81 FPS and 26.7% of frames over 30 ms before; median 6.8 ms, mean 145 FPS and no frame over 30 ms after. The map zoom-out went from a mean of 66 to 132 FPS (frames over 30 ms 34.8% to 0.7%). A swap is now held only when the one before it had no update either, which a real untimed loop always shows.
- `mcla_intro=fast` (and `skip`) turned the hold off for good, so every later loading screen also ran as fast as the PC could present. They now leave it off only until the engine timer first runs, which is the end of the start-up logos.

Unchanged: the start-up sequence at `normal` (334 swaps over 11.3 s at 29.4 per second, against 327 over 11.1 s before), at `fast` (879 swaps in 3.7 s, against 893 in 3.8 s), and capped play, where every swap has exactly one update (120 cap, free roam: 1,200 of 1,200 swaps). In the reachable path (start-up, title, attract movie, map zoom-out, free roam) the start-up sequence is the only untimed loop, so the fast intro's new limit was checked by reading the code, not on a later loading screen.

## Menus and HUD animations ran faster above 30 FPS (menus fixed 2026-10-14, the HUD at a rate of its own since 2026-10-16)

Reported by the user at a steady 120 cap: the song title scrolling under the speedometer ran far too fast while the game itself ran at normal speed. Every UI screen is a Flash movie (menus, pop-ups, the HUD with its song ticker and "LOADING" badge), and the UI manager steps each one with the Flash player's one-frame mode, which advances exactly one movie frame per game update and ignores the elapsed time (from LARecomp's notes, used with its author's permission: mcUIManager::Update, sub_821FC588, passes byte_827DC1FC = 1, which nothing writes, to the advance, sub_825EDC08). The console updated 30 times a second, so at 120 FPS every Flash animation ran four times as fast.

`mcla_ui_movie_advance` (a mid-asm hook at 0x825EDC6C in the advance, `src/frame_timing.cpp`) now gives each of the UI manager's screens (menus, the pause menu, pop-ups, prompts, the GPS map's panels; found by name in its movie map) its own 30-per-second schedule; an update that comes before a movie is due skips that movie's advance, through the same exit the player takes when a movie is not due. A movie due within a quarter of a frame is advanced early, so at 30 FPS every update still advances once. A movie that falls behind (below 30 FPS) is not caught up, as on the console. The start-up logos are left out, so the fast intro still runs them as fast as possible. `--mcla_ui_timing_fix=false` restores one movie frame per update.

Measured with the dev pad's frame-by-frame screenshots, timedemo at each cap (`--mcla_fixed_dt` = the cap), native, D3D12:

| | 30 FPS | 120 FPS before | 120 FPS after |
|---|---|---|---|
| Song ticker under the speedometer (px/s at 720p), with the HUD paced at 30 | 59.9 | 239.9 | 60.0 |
| Pause menu opening (Start in the city) | 933 ms | 183 ms | 883 ms |

The 30 FPS figures are the same with the fix on and off (59.5 to 59.9 px/s). The car's path over the scripted drive from the spawn point differed between runs with the fix on and off no more than between runs with the same setting (up to 13 m apart either way; the closest pair, 0.4 m, was one of each), and the drive's frame time was unchanged. Short presses still reach the menus: the GPS map's legend toggled on each of eight 15 ms presses of X at 120 FPS with the fix on, as with it off.

What else this covers and what it does not:

- Changed with it (the same mechanism, not measured separately): pop-ups and notifications, the GPS map's panels, the title screen's prompts.
- Not affected: the minimap, which the game moves itself (it changed on every new frame with the fix on, as off), and the GPS map's zoom-out, which is a camera move.
- Not affected: the 3D icons over the world (`3DICONSMOVIE0`-`2`), which the game advances by elapsed time already.

The in-game HUD has a rate of its own, `mcla_ui_hud_rate` (default 60 since 2026-10-16: the user asked for the console's 30 first, then played at 60 and chose it). It is two movies: HUDMAPMOVIE and the gauge movie, which the HUD code creates itself, outside the UI manager's screens. The game sets the gauges' values every update (`GearValue`, `Nitrovalue`, `map_rotation` and so on, seen by logging the player's variable lookups) and the movies' own scripts draw them as they advance, in the same frame step (`sub_825FB0D8` on the root sprite, one call for timeline and scripts) that scrolls the song ticker. Each of the two movies is needed for both: with either one paced alone, the ticker ran at 60 px/s and the needle stepped. Splitting the timeline step from the scripts would mean changing the Flash player's frame step, so the needles move exactly as often as the ticker steps. The rate picks the balance: 60 a second, the default (smoother needles, the ticker twice its original speed), 30 as on the console (the ticker at its original speed, the needles in 30 Hz steps), 120 (the ticker four times as fast), or 0 for every frame, as with no pacing. The HUD's movies get their own schedule at that rate, the same way as the screens' at 30; the value is read at every advance, so a change takes effect at once. It is the launcher's *HUD updates* and DISPLAY › HUD UPDATES in the pause menu. Until 2026-10-16 the HUD was paced only with `mcla_ui_timing_hud=true`, off by default, so the default was every frame.

At a 120 FPS cap, timedemo (`--mcla_fixed_dt=120`), native, D3D12, measured on 2026-10-16 as the title scrolls in after arriving in the city, and with the car accelerating:

| `mcla_ui_hud_rate` | Song ticker | Tachometer needle |
|---|---|---|
| 30 | 59.2 px/s, as at 30 FPS | moved in 30 of 119 frame pairs |
| 60 (default) | 118.2 px/s | moved in 50 of 116 frame pairs |
| 120 | 237.2 px/s | moved in 75 of 117 frame pairs |
| 0 (every frame) | 238.7 px/s | moved in 74 of 115 frame pairs |

The ticker figures are a fit of its position over 2.5 s, shots every 2 frames; 60, 120 and 240 px/s are what 30, 60 and 120 steps of 2 px a second give. The needle was found by its colour in a shot every frame for 1 s of acceleration; it rests for part of that second at every rate, so it moves in fewer pairs than the rate allows. Before the HUD had a rate of its own, paced and unpaced gave 60.1 and 239.9 px/s, and 28 of 119 and 69 of 116 pairs.

The menus stay paced either way: the pause menu opened in 883 ms at 120 FPS with the default settings. LARecomp left its HUD out of the same kind of pacing for the same reason. The "LOADING" badge belongs to the HUD and follows its rate.

## D3D12 stutter in the map zoom-out and in races (fixed 2026-10-15)

On D3D12, the map zoom-out after the title had frames of 150 to 330 ms where Vulkan had 30 to 45 ms (1% low 9 against 31 FPS at 3x, 19 against 48 at native, real time), and races had frames of 60 to 140 ms. Every new texture was a committed resource, an allocation of its own that took about 0.19 ms to create and 0.08 ms to release. The zoom-out streams in 750 to 900 textures in a single frame when the city appears, and at 3x the texture cache goes over its memory limit and evicts 1,400 to 1,500 at once; races stream textures in as the car moves. Vulkan sub-allocates its textures from large blocks. `patches/rexglue-d3d12-placed-texture-heaps.patch` places D3D12's textures in shared 256 MB heaps the same way (setting `d3d12_texture_heaps`, default on). Map zoom-out, real time, 1% low: 54 FPS at native and 27 FPS at 3x (Vulkan 48 and 31), worst frame 44 and 47 ms. Race, 1% low: 50 to 96 FPS at native and 41 to 61 FPS at 3x, worst frame 102 to 13 ms and 61 to 20 ms. Median frames and 60 FPS pacing are unchanged. One frame of about 55 ms remains in the zoom-out on both APIs, about 3 s in; it shows no GPU work, so it is the game's own. Measurements and method: `launcher/dev/d3d12-zoomout-2026-10-15.md`; details in `patches/README.md`. **Not verified**: other GPUs; the heaps hold video memory in 256 MB steps, which matters most on cards with little of it.

## Vulkan texture loads slower than D3D12 at 3x (fixed 2026-10-15)

At 3x, Vulkan's texture loads took 4.4 ms of each GPU frame, against 1.6 ms on D3D12. GPU timestamps around each step of the loads put almost all of it in one place: the copy of a decoded texture into its image (`vkCmdCopyBufferToImage`) ran at about 120 GB/s on NVIDIA's Windows driver, against 400 to 870 GB/s for D3D12's copy. The textures loaded every frame are render targets the game resolves into textures, up to 66 MB each at 3x.

`patches/rexglue-vulkan-texture-load-storage-copy.patch` writes the single-level ones with a compute shader through an integer view of the image instead, bit for bit the same (setting `vulkan_texture_load_storage_copy`, default `auto`: on with NVIDIA only). In the scripted city run at 3x, frames got shorter:
- free roam: 14.9 to 12.0 ms (-19%);
- the drive: 15.1 to 12.2 ms (-19%);
- the map zoom-out: 13.1 to 11.4 ms (-13%).

1% lows rose from 59-60 to 71 FPS. At 3x Vulkan was then 10 to 14% faster than D3D12 (with D3D12's placed texture heaps). That no longer holds: since direct resolves and single-tile rendering, the two are within about 3% at 3x in the city and D3D12 is 13 to 15% faster in the race (2026-10-16, `launcher/dev/single-tile-edram-2026-10-16.md`); at native the two are the same.

Validation shows nothing new; screenshots and the attract movie look the same. Measurements and method: `launcher/dev/vulkan-2026-10-14.md`; details in `patches/README.md`.

**Not verified**: other GPUs and drivers, which is why it is on with NVIDIA only by default, and the bike.

## Clears drawn at 4x MSAA took a quarter of the GPU frame at 3x (fixed 2026-10-15)

The game clears its render targets the Direct3D 9 way, by drawing a rectangle over them, mostly with 4x MSAA over targets drawn with 2x (the main scene, in two tiles) or 1x (the HUD's depth). The emulator's host render targets can't change MSAA mode in place. So each such clear copied the whole range into a 4x MSAA target, drew there, and copied it all back for the next draw. At 3x that was 3.3 to 3.6 ms of a 13 to 14 ms GPU frame on D3D12, and 2.6 to 2.7 ms on Vulkan.

`patches/rexglue-clear-rectangle-draws.patch` does these draws as clears of the host render targets, like a resolve's clear (setting `clear_rectangle_draws`, default on, both APIs). It clears in the MSAA mode that the range was drawn with next after the same clear last time, so the scene's tiles are cleared in place with no copies. Timedemo at 3x, median frame:
- **D3D12:** standing in the city 14.0 to 10.7 ms (-24%), driving 13.8 to 10.3 ms (-26%), the map zoom-out 12.7 to 8.7 ms (-31%), the race 12.9 to 10.5 ms (-18%).
- **Vulkan:** 12.1 to 9.8 ms (-19%), 12.3 to 9.8 ms (-20%), 11.3 to 7.7 ms (-32%), 13.6 to 11.9 ms (-13%).
- **1% lows** rose with them, 59-61 to 74 FPS on D3D12 and 69-70 to 82-84 on Vulkan in the city.
- **Native and pacing:** native resolution and 60 FPS pacing are unchanged.

Screenshots taken with the setting switched on and off within one run look the same. The D3D12 debug layer and Vulkan validation report nothing new. Measurements and method: `launcher/dev/edram-transfers-2026-10-15.md`; details in `patches/README.md`.

**Not verified**: other GPUs.

## Resolved textures loaded in two passes at 3x (fixed 2026-10-15)

With resolution scaling, the game's render targets resolved to memory are decoded into textures again at their next use, and at 3x the screen-sized ones are reloaded every frame, some several times, because the game reuses that memory for other targets. Each load decoded into a scratch buffer and then copied that into the texture: 1.6 ms of the GPU frame on D3D12 and 1.85 ms on Vulkan, standing in the city.

`patches/rexglue-texture-load-direct.patch` decodes these textures straight into themselves through an unsigned integer view, in one pass (setting `texture_load_direct`, default on, both APIs; on Vulkan with the storage views of `vulkan_texture_load_storage_copy`, so NVIDIA only by default). Texture loads went to 1.1 ms on D3D12 and 1.4 ms on Vulkan. Timedemo at 3x, median frame: D3D12 10.6 to 10.2 ms standing in the city (-4%), 10.2 to 9.7 ms driving (-5%), 9.7 to 8.1 ms in the map zoom-out, 10.4 to 9.9 ms in the race; Vulkan 9.8 to 9.4 ms (-4%), 10.0 to 9.4 ms (-6%), 8.8 to 7.9 ms, 11.9 to 11.6 ms. Native resolution and 60 FPS pacing are unchanged.

Screenshots taken with the setting switched on and off within one run look the same; the D3D12 debug layer and Vulkan validation (with synchronization validation) report nothing new. Measurements, what drives the resolves and the reloads, and the options left: `launcher/dev/resolves-2026-10-15.md`; details in `patches/README.md`.

**Not verified**: other GPUs.

## Stutter while new shaders compile (fixed 2026-10-15)

On a first run, in new places or races, and after a driver update or a change to the translated shaders, the GPU emulation creates new pipelines, which the driver compiles. Draws whose pipeline was still being created were already left out, but at the end of every frame both APIs then waited for every pending pipeline, holding the frame for the slowest compile. Vulkan also compiled a stand-in pipeline for each one on the GPU command thread first. With the driver's cache cold:
- D3D12 frames of up to 330 to 400 ms;
- on Vulkan, up to 1.9 s, with 17 s a session in frames over 50 ms; the map zoom-out fell 1 to 2 s behind.

`patches/rexglue-async-pipelines-no-frame-wait.patch` stops the frame from waiting. A draw waits for its pipeline only within 4 ms a frame (`async_pipeline_wait_budget_ms`), and is otherwise left out until the pipeline is ready. Vulkan no longer creates the stand-in pipelines. A session started with nothing cached:
- **D3D12:** worst frame 160 to 190 ms instead of 330 to 400, half as much time in frames over 50 ms, map zoom-out 1% low 23 to 26 FPS instead of 13 to 19.
- **Vulkan:** worst frame 170 to 250 ms instead of 1.9 s, 0.4 s in frames over 50 ms instead of 17 s, race 1% low 51 to 55 FPS instead of 13 to 15.
- **What shows instead:** objects missing for a moment while their pipelines compile. In one screenshot of a cold Vulkan zoom-out, some buildings were missing.
- **The remaining slow frames:** the remaining frames over 100 ms are also there with everything cached (one at the title screen and the race's loading).
- **Warm sessions:** with the shader storage and the driver's cache warm, as in normal play after the first runs, nothing is compiled during play and nothing changes.

`patches/rexglue-vulkan-pipeline-storage-parallel-translation.patch` translates the stored shaders in parallel when Vulkan starts: 0.3 s to 0.05 s of every start.

`patches/rexglue-vulkan-pipeline-cache-file.patch` can keep the driver's pipeline cache between runs (`vulkan_pipeline_cache_file`, off by default). NVIDIA's driver has a cache of its own, so it gained nothing here; it is meant for drivers without one.

Measurements and method: `launcher/dev/shader-stutter-2026-10-15.md`; details in `patches/README.md`.

**Not verified**: other GPUs and drivers.

## Resolves dumped every sample to the EDRAM buffer first (fixed 2026-10-15)

With host render targets, every resolve first copied every sample of the render targets over its area into the EDRAM buffer, and only then converted that into the destination. At 3x that dump was 60% of the resolve time: 1.35 ms of the GPU frame standing in the city by day on D3D12, 1.0 ms in the race.

`patches/rexglue-direct-resolve.patch` makes the SDK's `direct_host_resolve` (on by default, until now a stub) read the render target that owns the area directly, with the same conversions as the dump and the same copy code after it, at every resolution scale, on both APIs. Resolves per frame standing in the city, D3D12 and Vulkan: 0.48 to 0.33 and 0.50 to 0.42 ms at native, 1.09 to 0.67 and 0.93 to 0.65 ms at 2x, 2.2 to 1.1 and 1.9 to 1.1 ms at 3x. Timedemo at 3x, median frame: D3D12 10.1 to 9.0 ms standing in the city (-11%), 9.8 to 8.6 ms driving (-12%), 10.2 to 9.3 ms in the race (-9%); Vulkan 9.3 to 8.5 ms (-9%), 9.4 to 8.7 ms (-8%), 11.3 to 10.8 ms (-4%). At 2x the frame here is close to the game thread's limit: -6% standing in the city on D3D12, -1% on Vulkan, -3 and -7% in the race, with higher 1% lows. With a 120 FPS cap at 3x, D3D12 now holds it (93% of frames within 1 ms of the target instead of 26%; Vulkan 97% instead of 84%). At native resolution the frame time here is the game thread's and doesn't change; the resolve time saved is what a GPU-bound system gains. 60 FPS pacing is unchanged. `--direct_host_resolve=false` dumps as before.

Direct resolves were compared byte for byte with the EDRAM buffer path in the same frame, one a frame in turn, 162,550 of them at native, 2x and 3x (the attract movie, the map zoom-out, the city by day, a drive and the race in the rain, on both APIs): all of them matched. That comparison first showed that the EDRAM path read some wrong samples of 4x MSAA depth at odd resolution scales, native included: a thread's 16 consecutive values crossing the middle of a depth tile, whose halves are swapped in the EDRAM layout, which showed in this game as columns 4 pixels wide, 120 pixels apart at 3x, in the daylight depth. `patches/rexglue-resolve-4xmsaa-depth-samples.patch` fixes that shader, so a resolve gives the same result whichever path it takes; screenshots with direct resolves on and off are identical at every still pixel. Measurements and method: `launcher/dev/direct-resolve-2026-10-15.md`; details in `patches/README.md`.

**Not verified**: other GPUs. On Vulkan drivers without multisampled integer sampled images, resolves from multisampled colour render targets go through the EDRAM buffer (not tested).

The submenus upstream added to the Settings tab of the pause menu (DISPLAY, PERFORMANCE, CONTROLS; `src/settings_menu.cpp`) had these faults.

- **While the game runs,** the drop-downs are greyed out with a note to use the pause menu: the game reads the file only when it starts, and writes it itself. Not while the game runs (it waits until it has closed), and not from a `settings.json` that can't be read (a copy is kept as `settings.json.unreadable`). A failed save of `settings.json` is shown.

## Single-tile rendering (2026-10-16)

Upstream's `mcla_single_tile` (on by default) is meant to draw the main scene as one predicated tile instead of the Xbox 360's two. With 2x MSAA the game splits the 720p scene into two tiles (1280x512 and 1280x208) and submits every scene draw once per tile, about 1,370 draws each in the city. Until now it never took effect (`launcher/dev/single-tile-2026-10-16.md`): its hook acted only on a null render target, which no call passes, and the SDK's EDRAM is the console's 2,048 tiles, too few for one tile (1,472 tiles of colour and 1,472 of depth).

**Now it works:**
- **SDK** (`patches/rexglue-edram-size.patch`): an `edram_tiles` setting, 2048 (the console's, the default) or 4096. With 4096 the EDRAM wraps at 4,096 tiles, render target bases are 12 bits, the EDRAM buffer is twice the size (20 MiB at 1x, 180 MiB at 3x; host render targets don't grow) and the EDRAM resolve shaders have copies that wrap at 4,096.
- **Game:** on PC it sets `edram_tiles = 4096` as a default (`MclaApp::ApplyPcDefaults`, applied now before the GPU starts), and so does the PS5 build (`ps5/game/main_ps5.cpp`, untested on the console; below). The hook (`src/render_perf.cpp`, 0x8217A700 in `BeginTiledRendering`) acts on a tiled pass whose whole colour and depth fit in the EDRAM the GPU emulates (`IGraphicsSystem::edram_tile_count`, new in the SDK patch): the main scene with 2x MSAA becomes one 1280x720 tile, one bin and one resolve per target. The pause menu and the GPS map (4x MSAA, 2 x 2,944 tiles) keep their three tiles; with anti-aliasing off the game draws one tile by itself. `mcla_edram_limit` applies only with the larger EDRAM.
- **PERFORMANCE › SINGLE TILE RENDERING** is back in the pause menu, first in the list, and switches during play. With 2,048 tiles (`--edram_tiles=2048`, or a GPU that can't bind the larger EDRAM buffer) it says "(UNAVAILABLE)", A leaves it as it is (so such a session doesn't save a value for the next), and the hook changes nothing. The launcher has *Single tile rendering* (`mcla_single_tile` in `mcla.toml`).

**Gains,** one tile against two, anti-aliasing on, switched within each run:
- **3x:** frames 11.0% shorter in the city by day on D3D12 and 9.2% on Vulkan; 19.7% and 14.6% in the race.
- **Native:** the race 14.0% shorter on D3D12 and 25.3% on Vulkan, where the GPU command thread was the bottleneck (94% busy); the city stays at the game's own limit (about 7.0 ms).
- **GPU command thread:** 28 to 35% less CPU time; 30 to 37% fewer draws a frame.
- **GPU:** 1.0 ms less at 3x in the city, 1.1 (Vulkan) to 2.2 ms (D3D12) in the race. The earlier estimate was 0.8 and 1.3 ms.
- **120 FPS cap at 3x, standing in the city:** frames within 1 ms of the target 92.8 → 99.2% on D3D12, 94.4 → 99.0% on Vulkan.
- **Anti-aliasing off** is now 2 to 5% faster than on at 3x (it was 8 to 18% faster than two tiles).

**Checks:**
- **Resolves:** `launcher/dev/direct-resolve-validate.sdk.patch` compared each direct resolve with the same resolve through the EDRAM buffer, now with the 4,096-tile shaders (the one-tile scene's colour crosses tile 2,048): 33,700 resolves at native and 3x, city and race, both APIs, none different.
- **The picture:** one tile against two, switched within one run with time almost stopped (`mcla_fixed_dt`), shot on consecutive frames: the title, the map zoom-out, the city by day and at night, the pause menu and the GPS map at 1x, 2x and 3x on both APIs, also through the EDRAM buffer path (`--direct_host_resolve=false`) and on the ROV and FSI paths. The differences are frame-to-frame noise (under 0.5 of 255 on average), with no seam at the old tile boundary (row 512), apart from the ROV case below. The race, its pause menu and its replay look the same either way.
- **Nothing relies on the console's wrap-around** that was seen: `launcher/dev/edram-wrap-log.sdk.patch` logged every EDRAM range crossing tile 2,048. With 4,096 tiles the only ones are the one-tile scene's colour (tiles 1,472 to 2,912) and its resolve; with 2,048 tiles, through the title, the city, the menus and the GPS map, none at all.
- **Switching:** 50 switches of single tile and 50 of anti-aliasing during play, switches around and inside the pause menu and the GPS map, 20 of each in the race, and the pause menu's own row (A on SINGLE TILE RENDERING): the draws a frame went from one tile to two and back each time, and anti-aliasing off was one tile either way. Video memory stayed flat during the switches, or rose once when the other mode's targets were first made (two tiles: 42 MB at 1x, 360 MB at 3x; anti-aliasing off: 20 and 106 MB).
- **Debug layers:** the D3D12 debug layer, switching in the city at 1x and 3x and in the race at 3x: no errors, the usual warnings (initial states ignored, MIN/MAX blend factors, live objects at exit). Vulkan synchronization validation, in the city at 1x and 3x and in the race at 1x: only the write-after-write hazards between compute dispatches (the texture loads' scratch buffer, the EDRAM buffer's clears and dumps) that it reports with 2,048 tiles too, in about the same numbers.
- **The console's size:** with `--edram_tiles=2048`, two tiles whatever `mcla_single_tile` says, and the row says UNAVAILABLE. Two of the SDK patch's changes apply at 2,048 tiles too, and neither shows in this game, where nothing crosses tile 2,048 at that size: a resolve clear whose area wraps round the end of the EDRAM is no longer dropped (the offset from the render target's base is taken modulo the EDRAM size, a bug that is also in xenia), and the transfer shaders' address constant packs its pitches in 9 bits instead of 10.
- **The game's tile surface cache.** `BeginTiledRendering` keeps the surfaces it tiles into in 8 slots (keyed by MSAA mode, size and format) that are never freed; a ninth key would end in a function that never returns (`sub_821BD618`). The game's own keys take 4 (two 4x ones for the menus, 2x two-tile, 1x without anti-aliasing) and one tile adds one: 5 of 8 at most in every session measured (title, map zoom-out, city, pause menu, GPS map, a race with its pause menu and replay, 50 switches of each option, native and 3x, both APIs). The hook logs the count each time it grows, and doesn't make one tile when its key isn't there yet and 7 or more slots are taken. The garage was not reached.

**Anti-aliasing changed between one-tile frames** (fixed). The first scripted runs switched the options with the dev pad's `set`, which is what the SDK's F4 settings overlay and the console's `set` do too: during play, without the pause menu. Some sequences then left the vehicle pass, and what is drawn after it, in the other MSAA mode than the scene's (1x draws in the 2x one-tile scene, or 2x in the 1x one): on the default path EDRAM transfers every frame (0.6 ms at 3x in the city, 1 to 3 ms in the race) until the pause menu next opened or the scene went to two tiles for a few frames. It happens when the MSAA mode changes between two frames that are both one tile: anti-aliasing off (which the game draws as one tile itself) and anti-aliasing on with single tile, a combination the console never had. Changes made inside the pause menu were clean, the menu's own tiled passes coming in between. Now a change of MSAA mode between one-tile frames has frames in two tiles around it: after anti-aliasing is turned on, the hook draws the next 8 frames in two tiles; turning it off waits at the swap until 8 frames have been drawn in two tiles (`src/render_perf.cpp`, `src/perf_options.cpp`; 3 were not always enough, 4 were). Checked with frame logs on D3D12 at native (capped and uncapped) and 3x, through `set` sequences, the pause menu's rows (two tiles → anti-aliasing off → single tile on → anti-aliasing on in one menu session) and a start with anti-aliasing off turned on in the menu: no frame with the vehicles in the other mode, no transfers in the scene.

**The ROV path** (`--render_target_path_d3d12=rov`, the default on Intel GPUs on D3D12) showed foliage drawn wrong at night at 1x after a series of switches of the tiles without the menu, at frozen time; the same happened with 2,048 tiles after anti-aliasing switches, so it isn't the larger EDRAM. It came back in 6 of 8 runs of that sequence, also after the fix above (anti-aliasing was on throughout), and not with switches through the pause menu; the cause was not found. ROV is not the default on NVIDIA or AMD.

**The PS5** now asks for 4,096 tiles too (`ps5/game/main_ps5.cpp`, `MCLA_EDRAM_TILES`), so single-tile rendering is on there by default. **It is untested on the console** and needs a run there: the build, the GPU starting with "emulating an EDRAM of 4096 tiles" in the log, the picture, and the frame rate. RADV binds the larger buffer (`maxStorageBufferRange` 4,294,967,295). On PC, a Vulkan run with the PS5 host's own settings (`execute_unclipped_draw_vs_on_cpu=false`, its shared-memory settings, `clear_memory_page_state=false`, `vulkan_submit_on_primary_buffer_end=false`): one tile acts, the city by day and at night, the pause menu, the GPS map and a race look the same as two tiles, and the D3D12 frame logs with the same settings show no new EDRAM transfers. To go back: an `edram_tiles = 2048` line in `/data/mcla/mcla.toml` (the PS5 sets it as a default, which the file overrides), or build with `-DMCLA_EDRAM_TILES=2048`.

**Not verified:** Linux. On Vulkan, a GPU whose `maxStorageBufferRange` is under 180 MiB gets 2,048 tiles at 3x, with a warning in the log. Measurements and method: `launcher/dev/single-tile-edram-2026-10-16.md`.

## Display gamma for PC monitors (2026-10-16)

The game's tonemap writes sRGB-encoded colour (`1.055·x^(1/2.2) − 0.055`) into a plain 8-bit front buffer and leaves the display transfer to the console's gamma ramp: a 256-entry, 10-bit table that the display hardware applied and the SDK's presenter applies, on both APIs (`launcher/dev/frame-structure-2026-10-16.md`). Direct3D sets the ramp once, when the device is created: the game's identity ramp goes through `D3DDevice_SetGammaRamp` (sub_82416000), which adjusts it for the display with a table that the XDK helper sub_82426B68 builds from `VdGetCurrentDisplayGamma`. The SDK answers type 2, a TV (BT.709), as for an Xbox 360 on an HDTV, which makes the table BT.709 OETF(sRGB EOTF(x)). Type 1, sRGB, makes it the identity. On a PC monitor, which expects sRGB, the TV ramp makes the shadows darker than the game drew them: 8-bit levels 64, 128 and 192 come out as 193, 462 and 741 of 1023 (48, 115 and 185 of 255) instead of 256, 513 and 770.

`mcla_display_gamma` picks the type (`src/display_gamma.cpp`):
- `tv`, the default, is type 2, as before; `pc` is type 1, the identity ramp.
- The launcher's *Display gamma* and the pause menu's DISPLAY › DISPLAY GAMMA (TV, PC MONITOR) set it.
- A mid-asm hook right after the helper's call to `VdGetCurrentDisplayGamma` (0x82426B98) puts the chosen type where the call wrote its answer. The helper caches its table by type, so a new type builds a new table.
- **It switches during play.** The game never sets its ramp again, so when the option changes, an override of `D3DDevice_Swap` (sub_82419E98) calls `SetGammaRamp` again at the start of the next swap, with the identity ramp the device creation used (the PWL variant for a 10-bit front buffer, which this game doesn't use). That runs on the thread that owns the device, between two frames, and the swap sends the new ramp to the GPU with its frame, as a game changing its ramp would. A change shows on screen within a few frames (two or three at 60 FPS in the screenshots).
- The log says which ramp is in use, at the first swap and after each change, with its values at levels 0, 64, 128, 192 and 255. It is a game-side change; the SDK is not patched.

Checked on Windows, RTX 5080, with `devrun.py`, in the city at 23:30 (time of day poked), at 1x on both APIs and at 3x on D3D12:
- **The ramps.** TV: 0, 193, 462, 741 and 1023, the values `frame-structure-2026-10-16.md` read from the presenter; PC monitor: 0, 256, 513, 770 and 1023, the identity.
- **The picture.** Applying the TV ramp to a PC monitor screenshot gives the TV screenshot of the same moment to within 1 level on 97.6 to 99.98% of pixels (the rest is what moved between the two). The night city's average brightness went from 46 to 60 of 255, and the pixels below 64 from 73% to 61%; behind the pause menu, from 32 to 45. Dark detail such as the car's tyres, its rear bumper and the driver through the rear window shows with PC monitor and is black with TV.
- **Switching during play.** 47 switches a run, 40 of them one every 3 frames, from the dev pad and from the menu row (right, right, A): every screenshot was wholly one ramp or the other, with no mixed or damaged frame. The menu row saved `mcla_display_gamma = "pc"` to `mcla.toml`.
- **Starting with `pc`** in `mcla.toml` (through `--toml`): the first frame already had the identity ramp.
- **D3D12 debug layer:** no errors, only the usual warnings (buffer initial states, MIN/MAX blend factors, live objects at exit). **Vulkan validation** with synchronization validation: only what it reports without this change (`vkCmdEndQuery` 07007 and 01923, `ShaderOutputNotConsumed`, and 10 write-after-write hazards between texture-load dispatches during start-up, before any switch).

**Not verified**: other GPUs, Linux and the PS5 (the option is in the PS5's menu too). In HDR (below) the same table is applied, so the setting keeps its meaning.

## FSR 3.1 anti-aliasing (2026-10-17)

A third anti-aliasing choice beside the game's 2x MSAA and none: AMD FSR 3.1 at native resolution (PERFORMANCE › ANTI ALIASING, the launcher's Anti-aliasing; `mcla_anti_aliasing = "fsr"`). The scene's draws are moved by a sub-pixel jitter that changes every frame; at the game's motion blur draw, motion vectors are made from the depth, the vehicle IDs in the stencil and the game's own per-car reprojection matrices, FSR blends the frame with the ones before it, and the result goes back into the scene texture the blur, the tone map and HDR output read. `patches/README.md` (`rexglue-temporal-aa.patch`) has the details and the measurements.

Costs and limits:
- **GPU time:** the pass takes about 1.4 ms a frame at 3x on an RTX 5080 (1.38 ms on D3D12, 1.45 ms on Vulkan), 0.58 ms at 2x and 0.16 ms at native; MSAA is off with it, so the net cost is about 1 ms at 3x. **Video memory:** FSR's own resources, about 500 MB at 4K (AMD's figure), released 10 seconds after it's turned off.
- **Look:** a slightly softer picture than MSAA (FSR SHARPNESS, 0.2 by default, sharpens it back); possible faint trails behind fast-moving things. Cars move with the game's own per-car matrices; pedestrians, wheels and animated textures only with the camera.
- **Rain, smoke and particles** have no motion vectors of their own and may leave faint trails or fade. A reactive mask for them (FSR leans on the current frame where translucent effects changed the picture) is in, experimental and off by default (`temporal_aa_reactive`, 4 is a start; `temporal_aa_debug 5` shows it): in the night race the smoke plumes showed no trails without it, so it stays off until a scene shows it helps.
- **Exposure:** FSR works out its own (its auto-exposure), not the game's.
- **Availability:** FSR is offered only where its runtime for the graphics API in use was built and the device has what it needs (the Vulkan runtime needs the Vulkan SDK at build time and a Vulkan 1.3 device); elsewhere, and if it fails, the game uses MSAA. Not on Linux or the PS5 yet.
- **With MSAA or off** nothing of it runs: no draw is moved, and the game's frame is drawn as before. On Vulkan the device now enables the 16-bit, storage-image and subgroup-size features it has (for FSR); measured against the previous build at 3x, frame times are within run-to-run noise (Vulkan with MSAA: race -0.1%, city -1.1%; Vulkan off: race -0.9%; D3D12 with MSAA: race -0.6%).

## HDR output (2026-10-16)

The game in HDR on a display in HDR mode: its HUD, menus, movies and legal screens at the SDR level (as Windows shows SDR on an HDR desktop), and its scene from its own tone map's inputs: the same as in SDR up to 60% of the game's white, never darker than SDR from there to its white, and brighter above it. Settings: the launcher's *HDR*, *HDR peak brightness*, *HDR paper white* and *HDR highlights*, the pause menu's DISPLAY › HDR, PEAK BRIGHTNESS, PAPER WHITE and HIGHLIGHTS, and `hdr`, `hdr_peak_nits`, `hdr_paper_white_nits`, `mcla_hdr_highlights` in `mcla.toml`; all change during play. On Windows `hdr` is `auto` by default (`MclaApp::ApplyPcDefaults`: HDR whenever Windows has HDR on for the display, SDR otherwise; a value in `mcla.toml` or on the command line wins); the SDK's own default stays `off`, for other titles, and Linux and the PS5 keep `off` (the display's HDR state isn't read there yet). The look was signed off by the user on an ASUS ROG Swift PG32UCDP (WOLED, Gaming HDR mode, Uniform Brightness on), 2026-10-16: with *Auto* peak and paper white and *Highlights* at Brighter, which is the default since. Peak brightness goes from 200 to 4,000 nits in steps of 50 and paper white from 80 to 480 in steps of 10 (the range of Windows' SDR content brightness), AUTO first: sliders with an *Auto* box in the launcher, rows in the pause menu where holding left or right goes faster (the game repeats a held direction every 6 frames or so, so the menu steps once per press, again after 0.4 s, and faster from 1.2 and 2.2 s, at about the same speed at any frame rate: 200 to 4,000 nits in about 3 s at 150 and at 30 FPS). The peak is never set below paper white: a step that would cross it stops at the last one that doesn't, and the other value isn't moved. Values typed into `mcla.toml` between the steps are kept and shown as they are (537 NITS), and a step goes from them to the next step that way (550). HDR's row says "(NOT AVAILABLE)" whenever the display says it isn't in HDR mode, read when DISPLAY opens (also with `hdr` off, which otherwise doesn't read the display) and after that while it's open.

How it works (`launcher/dev/hdr-2026-10-16.md` has the details and the measurements; `patches/README.md` the SDK patches):
- **Markers.** The game writes NOP markers into its GPU command stream where its 3D scene ends, its tone map and its UI start (`src/render_markers.cpp`, `mcla_render_markers`, on). They draw nothing: screenshots with them on and off are identical. Past the command buffer's limit a marker makes room the way the game's own writers do.
- **Output.** HDR10 (PQ, BT.2020), on both APIs. The game's 8-bit colour still goes through its display table, so DISPLAY GAMMA means the same in HDR. Peak and paper white default to what Windows reports (the HDR Calibration app's peak, the SDR content brightness slider; 1,000 and 200 nits if it reports nothing usable), read about once a second while HDR isn't off; above 0.75 of the peak, light rolls off towards it. The scene's shoulder tops out at the peak in the game's units, and with that roll-off its brightest highlights reach about 90% of the peak setting; bloom and contrast take some past that, towards the peak.
- **UI.** The HUD and menus are separated from the scene as they are drawn, and the game's own 8-bit frame is put back for what it reads; the UI is then blended over the scene as the game blends it. Every scene reached took this path: legal screens and logos (as SDR), the intro and attract movies, title, map zoom-out, play, pause and GPS map with their fades, race menus, loading, intro, countdown, race, race pause, replay confirmation and replay, the race-over screen.
- **Scene.** After the game's tone map draw, its operator runs again on the GPU from the same inputs, with a shoulder from 60% of the game's white towards the peak instead of the clip, never under the game's own curve up to its white; the light glows the game adds after it at night come over too. *Highlights* (Normal, Bright, Brighter, Brightest; Brighter by default) scales how fast the shoulder rises and the glows' gain: at dusk the brightest pixel goes from 785 to 1209 nits on the test display, with the midtones unchanged. The tone map draw is recognised by its shaders, its target, its coverage of the whole output and the texture settings the pass reads; a frame where it isn't keeps the 8-bit scene, and every change is logged with the reason.

The second review (2026-10-16, phases 3 and 4) brought: the shoulder never under the game's curve near its white (at Normal it had been 5 to 16% darker there, depending on the peak; Brighter is unchanged: in the same paused dusk frame at peak 1317 and paper white 280 the brightest pixel went from 1086 to 1125 nits at Normal and stayed at 1209 at Brighter); the checks on the tone map draw; the glows' gain only for light added after the tone map; HDR's "(NOT AVAILABLE)" kept current; the markers keeping the host's floating-point mode when they make room; the textures released with HDR off; guards against NaN (`launcher/dev/hdr-2026-10-16.md`, "Second review").

Costs and limits:
- **GPU time:** about 0.5 ms a frame at 3x (0.51 on D3D12 and 0.51 on Vulkan since the second review's fixes, the scene pass 0.20 of it), against 0.04 ms for SDR's gamma pass.
- **Video memory:** about 200 MB of textures at 3x while HDR is on; they're released a couple of seconds after it's turned off, and made again when it comes back.
- **The first time HDR is on,** a few new UI pipelines compile; in the tests no draw was left out for them.
- **Screenshots** (the dev pad's, captures) are SDR: highlights are clipped, and the roll-off is not undone.
- **HDR10 only;** no scRGB output yet.
- **Not reached in the tests:** the garage, photo mode and cutscenes. Photo-mode freeze frames would show as SDR (they don't go through the tone map). A UI drawn with a blend this doesn't know falls back for that frame (logged once): the UI is told from the scene by comparing with the picture under it, or the frame is shown as SDR.
- **With HDR off** nothing of this runs except the markers and a check per draw; a few small pipelines are created at start-up, and pipelines made for HDR in an earlier session are recreated from the pipeline storage. The picture is unchanged: every steady boot frame is byte-identical to the build without it.

**Not verified**: other HDR displays than the one the look was signed off on; switching Windows HDR on and off while the game runs; other GPUs; Linux (HDR10 on Wayland is planned; the presenter uses HDR10 where the surface offers it, but reads the display's levels only on Windows); the PS5, whose build applies the SDK patches but hasn't been compiled with them, and keeps SDR (no HDR rows in its menu).
